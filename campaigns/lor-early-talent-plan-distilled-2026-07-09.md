## The one-line plan

Day-in-the-life content built around four roles, shot across two days in early August, warming up through results season, launching the week of 21 September. Fewer, better applicants.

## The priority: day in the life

This is the campaign's engine. One real person per role, their actual day, told simply. Fabricated moments are fine (the coffee, the commute, the client meeting, the gym) because the job is to show the shape of a life, not document one. Two production tiers:

1. **Hero assets:** high production, shot on the two shoot days.
2. **Individual stories:** iPhone-level, self-filmed by the cohort, lightly tidied so the format feels consistent.

## The four roles

Structural engineers, building services, design management, procurement.

One deliberate rule: engineering has the biggest headcount and will dominate by default. We hold space for building services because it is the hardest to recruit and the least understood. Where it helps, we combine multiple people into a single asset to cover more roles per piece.

## Production: two shoot days

1. **Site day, start of August.** Site TBC, this is the item to lock first so crew and talent can be organised properly.
2. **Office day, Dartford.** Design management, procurement, planning.

CMC/factory is handled separately. New cohort filming (October) is held until Emma is back. Everton was considered and parked: it skews the audience Northwest and can't be repeated year on year.

## The organic layer

- **Cohort self-filming.** Short brief going to the new cohort (format, framing, what to capture). Minimal editing on our side to normalise the look.
- **Written posts.** Identify grads with active LinkedIn profiles and invite them to write. Last year's clearest lesson: authentic individual voices outperform everything (Vital's assembly specialist post).

## Channels

LinkedIn leads. Instagram for discovery. Facebook added this year, positioned toward parents. Roles go live on LinkedIn within 24 hours of posting (talent acquisition access is in place).

## Timeline

- **August:** warm-up. GCSE and A-level results posts, graphic carousels, a "coming soon" teaser.
- **Week of 14 September:** warm-up post goes live.
- **Week of 21 September:** launch.

## Budget

Around £28k across three windows: July to September, October to December, January to March. Window one carries the shoot days and is heavier; two and three are lighter. The number needs trimming, and Emma confirms the exact figures on her return. Until then we concentrate spend and effort on day in the life.

## Paid

Confirmed as needed. Strategy shaped by what worked on the assembly specialist campaign. GTI partnership (Target Jobs / Handshake) fans roles out to 50+ universities, and a programmatic tool is being explored for targeted placement. Location targeting stays loose until September placement locations are confirmed.

## Quality over quantity

20,000+ applications came in this year and quality was poor. The whole campaign is built to reverse that: content that is explicit about what LOR is looking for, plus the new motivational questions designed to be un-ChatGPT-able.

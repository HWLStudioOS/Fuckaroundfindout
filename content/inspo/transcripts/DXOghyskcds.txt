 My least favourite question to be asked is "Where are you from? Do you be my background? My accent? Where I live? Or what the hell I'm drinking?"
 Because every question will give you a different answer.
 G'day, my name is Tom. I've grown up culturally conflicted and ethically confused.
 I was born in Australia to a father who was as true glue as they come. Thongs, footy surfboard.
 And an Argentinian mother who left home at 21 with barely a word of English to marry my dad.
 Dad built on Sydney's northern beaches and we'd go back to Argentina most years.
 Weeks, sometimes months, summers at the beach, Monday every hour, dinner at 11pm,
 cards until midnight. See, I grew up eating a saddle and vegemite,
 playing rugby and surfing, and drinking more mother than any Australian should.
 I've always struggled with my identity. Sometimes I don't know who I am.
 I've tried to box myself into molds and versions of myself I never was.
 I've neglected parts of me that define my character. I've tried to fit in,
 but I'm always left feeling like an outsider.
 Impossible to truly be understood. I mean, do I even understand myself?
 But as I tell this story from Western Australia, 4,000 kilometers from my family,
 I've realized something. My unconventional upbringing and misaligned sense of self
 is what defines me. I can adapt my surroundings and find comfort in so many ways,
 because home was never a place, it was a feeling. So where am I from?
 My parents raised me, but Australia is my own.

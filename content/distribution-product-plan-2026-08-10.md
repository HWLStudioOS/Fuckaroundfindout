# Distribution and product plan

## The decision

Do this.

Do not copy Oliur's aesthetic or Daniel Dalen's life. Copy the economic architecture.

**Harrison's version is Daniel's operator documentary, Oliur's taste-led product catalogue, and HWL's real proof.** A filmmaker building a one-person studio on AI systems, showing the work, then selling useful artefacts left behind by that work.

The July strategy got the identity right. It got one sequence wrong. The line saying no product until an audience exists is retired. The audience and product shelf should grow together.

This is not permission to build five products. It is a decision to put one good paid product on the shelf now, distribute the real work consistently, and let customer behaviour determine product two.

## What the research says

### Oliur

The useful lesson is not that a large creator can sell wallpapers.

- He began selling small digital assets before YouTube. His own account starts with phone icons and Tumblr themes. The themes reached $5,000 to $30,000 in some months before the current audience existed.
- He calls YouTube the top of funnel. It overtook his client income in 2021, after six years of posting, and then justified a dedicated studio.
- His second channel came after the main channel had years of history. It separates business, finance and self-improvement from the main tech channel. It is not a cold-start tactic.
- His official link hub does not treat every social platform equally. It centres two YouTube channels, X, Instagram, a newsletter and the product store. LinkedIn and TikTok are not presented as core parts of the machine.
- The catalogue compounds. Current low-ticket products include wallpapers, presets, LUTs, mock-ups and AI guides, mostly around $16 to $39. Bundles raise order value and make every new release improve an old product.
- In his own videos, he reports more than $50,000 from one wallpaper collaboration and more than $95,000 from five preset packages. The principle he repeats is simple: package the work people already notice and ask about.
- He publicly claims more than $2 million in total digital-product revenue. Treat the number as self-reported, but the live catalogue, pricing and long operating history are observable.

The correction for Harrison is important. Oliur's lesson is not "get famous, then monetise". It is "make useful or desirable products, charge from the start, and let distribution and catalogue compound together".

### Daniel Dalen

Daniel's useful lesson is different. The product is not a preset pack. The product is belief in the operator.

His public surface is built around companies he is actively building. The videos make decisions, travel, product development and operating problems watchable. His website then turns that attention into an owned email relationship. Ecomflow gives the story a real commercial object behind it.

For Harrison, this is the office search, the studio, live client constraints, the agent system, the product build, the money decisions, the race training and London life. The work is already there. It needs a camera and a point of view, not invented storylines.

### X in 2026

X is worth a serious bet, especially while Harrison is small.

The current For You system mixes followed accounts with posts retrieved from a global corpus, then predicts actions including replies, profile clicks, dwell and follows. That gives relevant original work a credible route outside the existing follower graph.

But X is a discovery and relationship layer, not the asset. On 7 August 2026 X stopped new enrolments in its old revenue-sharing programme and announced its replacement. That is a three-day-old example of platform volatility. Platform payouts do not belong in the business model.

The job of X is to find the right people, test language quickly, and route attention into YouTube, email and products.

## The business architecture

| Surface | Job | 90-day rhythm | Commercial route |
|---|---|---:|---|
| YouTube | Trust and compounding depth | One video every two weeks | Email and relevant product |
| X | Discovery, relationships, idea testing | Three originals and 25 good replies a week | Profile, email and product |
| LinkedIn | Buyer trust for HWL | Two posts a week | Buyer conversation and studio site |
| Instagram and TikTok | Visual discovery and texture | Two native pieces a week combined | YouTube and profile hub |
| Email | Owned relationship | One note every two weeks | Product releases and durable trust |
| Personal shop | Permanent product shelf | One product until demand proves the next | Direct revenue |

This is one content system, not five separate jobs. One real event or observation feeds every surface in its native form.

## YouTube shape

Start with one personal channel. Do not create an "Oliur Online" equivalent yet.

Use two working containers inside the same channel:

1. **Operator documentary.** The Dalen shape. Building HWL, finding and fitting the office, running client work, solving cashflow and capacity, shipping products, using agents inside the actual business.
2. **Direct studio note.** The Oliur Online shape. Harrison at the desk explaining one current decision, system, failure or AI experiment with receipts.

These are containers, not final public series names. Harrison's own words determine the names after the first three uploads.

Split channels only after at least 12 long-form videos and clear evidence that the two subjects serve incompatible returning viewers. Oliur earned the split after years. Copying it at zero would divide weak signal into two weaker signals.

The cadence is fortnightly for this pilot. July prescribed weekly and nothing shipped. Six good-enough videos in twelve weeks is a better test than another weekly system that exists only in a file.

## The product shelf

### Product one, now

**The AI Content Engine, Founding Edition.** Public price: £19 until 25 customers, then £29.

It already exists at `/Users/harrison/hwl-content-engine`. The method is real, it was taught to a room of founders, and the complete product is built. It is the shortest path from planning to a paid customer.

Public buyers pay from day one. Year One gets it free because that was already promised. That is not a free public launch. It is honouring a specific prior commitment.

Before launch it needs:

- the full asset moved behind a checkout, with a clear preview page;
- a downloadable package and twenty-minute walkthrough;
- the licensed-font question resolved;
- a receipt and support path;
- a simple email opt-in for product updates;
- three examples showing the output from real users, added as they arrive.

Do not call it passive income. It is a small paid validation of the portfolio thesis.

### Product two, only after demand

Likely options, in order:

1. **A Harrison grade pack.** One LUT plus the Lightroom presets actually used across office, London, running and studio photographs. Release only after the look is visibly repeated in public and people ask about it.
2. **The one-person studio operator pack.** Clean, generic versions of the morning brief, verifier pattern, project setup, money review and agent instructions. Build from repeated questions, never by dumping the private OS.
3. **Wallpaper or desk assets.** These are catalogue fillers once the office and visual library exist. They are not the first strategic product.

No course until at least 25 people have bought a smaller product and the same deeper questions recur. No software product until a manual product proves a recurring problem.

## The office

The office is a multiplier, not the starting gun.

It should be taken only if the current studio economics justify it for focus, secure equipment, fibre and production. Future creator revenue does not belong in that calculation.

The story begins before the keys. The search, constraints, trade-offs, viewing, decision, move, build and first proper week are all material. Waiting for a perfect room would repeat the existing content failure in a nicer location.

Once the room exists, run one asset-harvest block each month:

- one YouTube episode;
- twenty strong photographs;
- ten reusable B-roll sequences;
- two or three product demonstrations;
- one honest operator note about what changed or failed.

The room needs to make work and distribution easier. It does not need to perform wealth.

## The 90-day pilot

### 10 to 16 August, turn the system on

- Capture a 20-minute voice note or camera conversation on exactly what triggered this plan. No script.
- Clean the X profile around one sentence, one proof point and one link.
- Make 15 thoughtful X replies across the week and publish two original posts from live work.
- Package the AI Content Engine for a paid public launch and a free Year One delivery.
- Record video one from the current desk. Do not wait for the office.

### 17 August to 6 September, first transaction

- Publish the first two YouTube videos.
- Put the AI Content Engine live by 23 August.
- Send the promised free version to Year One.
- Publish six X originals, four LinkedIn posts, and four supporting visual pieces.
- Add email capture to the personal hub and send the first two notes.

The goal is one stranger paying. Not scale.

### 7 September to 4 October, build the loop

- Hold the cadence without adding a product.
- Use buyer questions, support requests, saves, replies and click behaviour to improve the Content Engine.
- Document the office process if it is real by then. Do not manufacture an office arc if the decision has not happened.
- Publish one transparent build receipt. Traffic, sales, what did not convert, and the next change.

### 5 October to 8 November, make a decision from evidence

- Complete six total YouTube videos.
- Complete 36 original X posts and 300 specific replies across the pilot.
- Complete 24 LinkedIn posts and 24 supporting visual pieces combined across Instagram and TikTok.
- Reach 25 paid customers or produce a clear diagnosis of whether the problem was reach, offer or conversion.
- Decide whether to improve product one, build the grade pack, or stop. One of those three. No vague continuation.

## Weekly operating loop

Time budget: five to eight hours.

1. **Capture, 45 minutes.** One conversation or voice note from something that actually happened.
2. **YouTube, three hours every second week.** Talk is the spine. Existing footage supplies texture. Stop before it turns into a client edit.
3. **Writing, 90 minutes.** Two LinkedIn posts and three X posts from Harrison's captured substance.
4. **Relationships, 15 minutes on five weekdays.** Five useful X replies each day. Split some of the same block into LinkedIn comments when a real buyer is present.
5. **Visual support, 45 minutes.** Two native cuts or photo sets. No separate shoot unless the moment earns one.
6. **Email, 30 minutes every second week.** The note that accompanies the YouTube release, with one relevant product link.

AI can transcribe, find structure, cut variants, package and schedule. It cannot invent Harrison's opinion or pretend an event happened.

## Scoreboard

The main input is loops run. The first commercial proof is paid customers.

| Date | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| 23 August | Product one live, video one live |
| 10 September | Three paid customers and two videos, or a written traffic/conversion diagnosis |
| 4 October | Cadence held for eight weeks, product improved from real feedback |
| 8 November | Six videos, 36 X originals, 25 paid customers as the target |

Also track:

- X profile visits, follows from replies, email sign-ups and product clicks;
- YouTube returning viewers, average view duration and outbound clicks;
- LinkedIn buyer-shaped conversations, not likes;
- product page visits, conversion, sales, refunds and repeated support questions;
- email subscribers and replies.

Do not judge the plan by follower count after two weeks. Do judge whether the shelf is live and whether the weekly loop ran.

## Hard rules

- One personal YouTube channel for the pilot.
- One product in build at a time.
- Paid from the public launch. Specific promised recipients can receive it free.
- No office decision based on hypothetical creator income.
- No course before customer evidence.
- No automated or generic replies on X.
- No separate content made for every platform.
- No waiting for the office, a new camera, a final brand name or a larger following.
- No new strategy rebuild before the 10 September review. Execution creates the next information.

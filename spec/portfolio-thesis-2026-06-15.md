# The product portfolio thesis

The most important thing that came out of today was not a product. It was a direction:
build a portfolio of small products on the AI frontier, bigger than the studio, as the
real path to freedom. This doc holds that thesis honestly, with the argument that shapes
it, so it survives past the chat.

## The instinct, which is right

The studio is a job with your name on it. It trades your time, it is fragile (H2), it
caps at the hours you can sell. The codex north star is freedom and family, not running an
agency forever (H5, H9). So a portfolio of products that earn while you sleep is the
correct ambition, and the fact that building is now cheap and fast is a real, time-limited
edge. The altitude is right. Hold it.

## The three corrections that keep it real

The instinct goes wrong on the specifics, and you asked for the argument, so here it is in
durable form.

1. **"A tiny app that does 20 to 30k a month" is survivorship bias.** 20 to 30k a month is
   250 to 360k a year. That is a real company with real distribution, not a weekend ship.
   You see Bevel and the winners, not the ten thousand beautiful dead apps. Recalibrate:
   ship many small cheap bets, expect most to make nothing, hope a few make 1 to 3k, let
   them compound. That is the real game.
2. **Distribution is the binding constraint, not building.** You said it yourself about the
   Diagnosis: no live funnel, no consistent posting, no owned audience. Consumer apps need
   an order of magnitude more reach, not less. Building does not create distribution, it
   creates orphans. The boring work (consistent content, an owned audience) is the
   unlock, and no amount of shipping replaces it.
3. **Your edge is taste, a method, and being early, not hardware.** The moat is not "I know
   AI before you", that diffuses fast. It is taste plus packaging plus speed plus real
   domain access (serious orgs, content craft). Being early gets you a window. Taste keeps
   you in business after the window closes. So FROP and a Bevel clone are out: no moat,
   wrong stack, and even if one took off it would grow an audience of quantified-self
   hobbyists who never buy anything else you make. Misaligned with everything.

## The filter: edge x reachable buyer

Run every product idea through two questions before building anything.

1. Is my edge actually real here?
2. Can I reach the buyer without an audience I do not yet have?

| Idea | Edge real? | Buyer reachable? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| FROP / Bevel clone (consumer health) | No | No | Kill (fun Sunday toy only) |
| The Content Engine (AI content method) | Yes | Yes (Year One, operators) | Build |
| The Legibility Diagnosis (buyer funnel) | Yes | Yes (warm outreach, the studio funnel) | Build |
| Baseline (personal training app) | Learning, not commercial | n/a | Personal + capability |

That table is the whole strategy. Build where both are yes.

## The lane that is actually yours

Small AI products for the people one step behind you. Operators, founders, small
businesses, the exact Year One room where someone did not know what Claude was. Your 1%
knowledge is the moat, your taste is the difference, and the buyer is reachable because it
is the same audience your content is meant to build.

The flywheel: every product in that lane doubles as content, and the content builds the
audience the products need. A health app sits outside that loop entirely. The content
engine sits dead centre of it.

## The portfolio so far

Three things now exist, on purpose on different surfaces.

- **The Content Engine** (peer track, `~/hwl-content-engine`). The workshop method as a
  self-serve product. Bet number one in the lane. Free gift to Year One first, gateable
  later. Built and deploy-ready today.
- **The Legibility Diagnosis** (buyer track, `~/hwlstudio-site`, `legibility-diagnosis`
  branch). Not a SKU, the conversion door and a warm-outreach instrument for the studio.
  Built and deploy-ready today.
- **Baseline** (personal, `~/baseline`). Not commercial. A learning build that scratches a
  real itch and quietly buys you the capability "Harrison can ship a native app", which
  matters for the portfolio later.

## Sequencing, the honest part

This runs alongside the studio, not instead of it. The studio cashflow is what funds the
freedom and pays rent this quarter. The portfolio compounds in parallel and slowly. Do not
let "build products" become the shiny thing that starves the core (H4: AI is the
opportunity and the distraction; H21: the expansion impulse).

And the near-term highest-leverage studio moves have not changed. They are the case-study
films (Creepers, LOR, with David), a consistent posting rhythm, and the one new client by
30 June. The proof is the product. The portfolio is the long game that the audience work
unlocks. Both are true at once.

## Next actions

1. Fix the binding constraint: commit to a consistent posting rhythm so the audience the
   products need actually starts to exist. This gates everything in the lane.
2. Ship the Content Engine to Year One (free), capture who bites, build the paid layer on
   what they ask for. Bet one, lowest risk, warm audience.
3. Use the Diagnosis as a warm-outreach and scoping instrument, not an inbound bet.
4. Keep Baseline as the fun build and the capability investment, not a revenue line.
5. Before any new product idea, run the two-question filter. If either answer is no, it is
   a toy, not a bet.

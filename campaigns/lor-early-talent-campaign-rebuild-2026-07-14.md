# LOR Early Talent 2026: campaign rebuild

Internal source of truth for the client deck and August production. Built from the Kerri and Sarah meetings on 9 and 14 July 2026.

## The hard truth

The previous plan had a coherent creative wrapper but still behaved like an ideas menu. It counted crops and channel versions as extra creative, treated the Relay as proven before the project chain had been validated, and did not show LOR exactly what would be published.

The reset keeps the strongest material and removes the concept theatre.

### Keep

- Named employees and managers.
- Real assessment criteria, strong and weak answers, pay, grades and progression.
- Two shoot days, one site and one Dartford office.
- The four priority roles: structural engineering, building services, design management and procurement.
- Building services as a protected priority.
- One flagship film, four role films and two deeper Life as a Grad profiles.
- Quality over quantity as the campaign objective.

### Remove or demote

- "More than you think" as public campaign language. It remains an editorial check only.
- Relay as the campaign platform. It is one possible edit treatment for the flagship.
- Four generic day-in-the-life films that repeat the same beats.
- Factory or CMC as a core strand.
- Generic myth-busting, invented lifestyle moments and corporate narration.
- Self-filming as a core production dependency.
- Inflated asset counts that treat crops, ratios and reposts as new ideas.

## Strategy

The campaign has one job:

> Make the work, the standard and the route legible before asking someone to apply.

LOR does not need more broad employer awareness. It needs better self-selection. The right candidate should understand what the role involves, what a strong application proves and what the route gives them. The wrong candidate should opt out before applying.

### Three proof systems

1. **Show the work.** One real task, one decision and one consequence per role.
2. **Show the standard.** Publish what assessors actually read and score.
3. **Show the route.** Put grades, pay, qualification, progression and dates on screen.

### Audience decisions

| Audience | Decision they are making | Proof required | Primary channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graduate or final-year student | Can I picture myself doing this work, and will it lead somewhere? | A named person, a real task, one honest downside and visible progression | LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube |
| Apprentice or school leaver | Is this a serious route, and what will I earn and qualify as? | Real grades, real pay, funded qualification and a clear year-by-year path | Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook |
| Parent or gatekeeper | Is this secure, credible and better than the route I already understand? | Forwardable route documents, exact requirements, pay and process | Facebook, LinkedIn, printable PDF |

## Creative system

### Series 1: One Project. Four Decisions.

The flagship follows one live project through four roles. The public idea is stable. The edit treatment changes based on what pre-production proves.

- **Relay treatment:** literal handoffs, used only if all four roles genuinely touch one project in a clear sequence.
- **Four Decisions treatment:** four real decisions on the same project, intercut without pretending the work moves in a neat chain.

Both treatments produce the same master film and four role cuts. No asset depends on a fake handoff.

### Series 2: How to Get Hired Here

LOR publishes the assessment sheet, a strong answer beside a generic answer, the process timeline and an assessor explaining what they actually score. This is the campaign's most defensible material because competitors cannot copy it without revealing their own process.

### Series 3: Life as a Grad

Two deeper profiles only. One site voice and one office voice, preferably building services. Each film includes one task, one decision, one thing that went wrong, pay or progression answered straight, and one honest downside.

### Series 4: Routes, in numbers

Results-day truth, the apprenticeship ledger and the parent route map. Designed to be saved and forwarded, not admired.

## Shoot readiness

### Proof gate before either shoot

By 24 July, LOR must confirm:

1. The exact four roles and live apply paths.
2. One live project that all four roles genuinely touch, or confirmation that the flagship uses the Four Decisions treatment.
3. One named person per role, plus one site Life as a Grad lead, one office lead and one assessor.
4. Site and Dartford access, screen permissions, releases and safety limits.
5. The real model, drawing, package, order or decision each person can show.
6. Cleared salary, progression, grades, application criteria and process timings.
7. The application open date and closing date.

If the shared project chain is not proven by 24 July, the Relay edit is dropped. The asset slate does not change.

### Shoot Day 1: live site

**Primary posts:** P01 if the results subject is site-based, P10, P12 and P16.

**People:** structural engineering lead, site Life as a Grad lead, site manager or assessor if useful.

**Required capture per lead:**

- A 45-minute audio-first interview.
- One owned task and one real decision.
- The document, tool or check that proves it.
- What could go wrong and what happened the last time it did.
- The handoff they receive and the output they pass on.
- Twelve action shots, three clean portrait setups and clean room tone.
- Arrival and context only if they are true to that person's day.

**Non-negotiable frame:** the structural lead physically signs, checks or marks the item behind P12. No generic walking B-roll can replace it.

### Shoot Day 2: Dartford office

**Primary posts:** P06, P10, P11, P13, P14 and P17.

**People:** building-services lead, design-management lead, procurement lead, named assessor, strongest office Life as a Grad voice.

**Required capture per role:**

- One live screen, document, package or model.
- One decision from that week and the consequence for site.
- The input they received and the output they handed on.
- A clean explanation in plain English, recorded audio-first.
- Twelve action shots, three clean portraits and hand-detail inserts.

**Assessor setup:** assessment sheet on the table, one real question, the strong and generic answers, the process timeline, and one story about turning down better grades.

### Interview spine

Every subject is pre-interviewed before the shoot. On the day, ask for specifics and proof, not rehearsed employer-brand answers.

#### Every role

1. What are you responsible for today, in one sentence?
2. Show me the thing that proves it.
3. What decision did you make this week?
4. What happens on site if that decision is wrong or late?
5. What did you receive from the person before you?
6. What do you hand on, and who needs it next?
7. What went wrong recently, and what did you do?
8. What did you misunderstand about this role before joining?
9. What is one honest downside?
10. What does the next step in pay, responsibility or qualification look like?

#### Assessor

1. What does the assessment sheet actually score?
2. Show one strong answer beside a generic answer. What is the difference?
3. What is one mistake you forgive?
4. What is one mistake that ends an application?
5. Tell me about a candidate with better grades who did not progress. Why?
6. What use of AI is sensible, and what makes an answer feel false?
7. What happens after submit, and where do candidates normally get stuck?

## Measurement

The campaign is judged on:

- Source-attributed applications by role.
- Qualified-applicant rate by role, not total volume.
- Cost per qualified applicant.
- Click-to-start and start-to-submit rates.
- Saves and sends on route and application documents.
- Video completion on the role and Life as a Grad films.

Reach and raw application count are diagnostics, not success measures.

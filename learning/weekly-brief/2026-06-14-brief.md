## Items

- **The Sample Efficiency Black Hole**, essay, Dwarkesh Patel. [https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/the-sample-efficiency-black-hole](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/the-sample-efficiency-black-hole)
  Published 8 June 2026. Frontier AI trains on 100 trillion tokens; humans absorb around 200 million tokens in a lifetime, a million-fold gap. Yet the economics hold because inference costs amortise across billions of deployment sessions, where human education is bounded by lifespan. The non-obvious claim: data, not architectural tricks or hyperparameter tuning, is the real driver of progress, which is precisely why open-source models can get within months of the frontier. Challenges any assumption that the labs have unbreachable technical moats. Apply to the B2B content research queue: the tool stack is not differentiating. Taste and spec quality are.

- **The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage**, preprint, arXiv. [https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13003](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13003)
  June 2026. Systematic evaluation across reasoning datasets and interactive multi-step workflows. Auto-generated multi-agent systems underperform chain-of-thought with self-consistency despite costing up to 10x more. The failure mode is named precisely: "architectural bloat that prioritises superficial complexity which does not translate into functional utility." Expert-designed MAS does outperform, but only when the architecture is grounded in verifiable task decomposition rather than graph-search complexity. Direct read for Harrison OS: before building agent five, map the task decomposition. If you cannot state the verifiable goal of each existing agent in one sentence, you are building bloat.

- **Claude Fable is Relentlessly Proactive**, essay, Simon Willison. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/fable-is-relentlessly-proactive/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/fable-is-relentlessly-proactive/)
  Published 11 June 2026. A single debugging session, one-line prompt from a screenshot. Fable autonomously: ran the dev server with fake environment variables, cycled Playwright through Chrome, Firefox, and WebKit, used osascript to query window IDs, wrote a Python CORS web server to capture JSON, hacked Datasette templates to force a keyboard shortcut via JavaScript, and eventually downgraded itself to Opus on hitting a guardrail, then resumed using the full transcript. Willison's conclusion: "the amount of damage it can do given its proactivity is terrifying." The useful and terrifying thing are the same thing. Practical implication for Harrison: the bottleneck in using Claude Code well is now spec clarity, not model power. A vague instruction to a ghost that knows all the tricks is not a prompt, it is a risk.

- **Ischemic Preconditioning and Post-Exercise Recovery in Endurance Runners**, RCT, Scientific Reports. [https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-52742-x](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-52742-x)
  Published 9 June 2026. Randomised double-blind sham-controlled trial, 40 competitive male long-distance runners, 21-day IPC protocol (blood-flow restriction cuff, thighs). IPC group: 27% higher IL-10 and 35% higher IL-15 post-exercise (anti-inflammatory and muscle-repair cytokines), plus significantly higher angiogenic and neurotrophic markers (ANG, NGF, sAPPa). The mechanism: repeated ischemia-reperfusion cycles train the molecular response to intense effort, shifting the acute recovery profile in a direction associated with better adaptation. Simple kit, five minutes before a session. The gut-muscle axis finding from last week's Mediterranean diet RCT and this IPC mechanism are converging on the same frame: the training stimulus matters, but so does the molecular environment you show up with.

- **The One-Person Business Is Back**, essay, Jean Galea. [https://jeangalea.com/the-one-person-business-is-back/](https://jeangalea.com/the-one-person-business-is-back/)
  Published 12 June 2026. Not another "AI makes everything easy" piece. The actual argument: AI breaks the solo-founder ceiling not by making production cheap but by giving taste and domain knowledge enough execution leverage to build something real. "AI can execute. It cannot care." The differentiator is knowing what to build, why, and for whom, which is accumulated judgment from being the customer before becoming the builder. Applies directly to how Harrison positions HWL Studio as the offer gets productised: the stack is table stakes. The call is the moat.

## Synthesis

- **Simple beats complex, across registers**: Dwarkesh's essay and the MAS paper arrive at the same point from different disciplines. Dwarkesh: the million-fold data advantage drives AI progress, not clever architecture, which is why catching up is easier than the labs want to admit. The MAS paper: automated multi-agent complexity is mostly noise at 10x the cost. Both point toward a discipline of simplicity over a reflex toward sophistication. The instinct to add another layer, another agent, another integration, is almost always wrong without a clear verifiable goal for each component. Sources: [https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/the-sample-efficiency-black-hole](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/the-sample-efficiency-black-hole), [https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13003](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13003).

- **The ghost that learned all the tricks**: Karpathy's framing from last week's brief, LLMs as ghosts with no world model, meets Willison's Fable account this week. The ghost has apparently learned to use Playwright, osascript, CORS servers, and browser devtools autonomously, without any of the lived stakes that make a human developer careful. Capability has moved faster than the responsibility framework. For anyone running AI on production systems, the spec is now load-bearing in a way it was not six months ago. Sources: [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/fable-is-relentlessly-proactive/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/fable-is-relentlessly-proactive/), [https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/).

## Recall drills

Rotating away from Q1, Q3, Q4, Q8 used in the 7 June brief. Status: pending (pre-read). Use as pre-priming before reading Art of War and returning to Durant.

1. Q: Durant's claim on wealth concentration. What is the mechanism and what periodically interrupts it?
   A: (hidden, expand after attempt) Wealth concentrates as a natural function of unequal ability. Interrupted periodically by either legislation that redistributes peacefully or revolution that redistributes violently. The cycle recurs across civilisations.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 8 "Economics and History."

2. Q: Durant's two oscillating poles of political history. Name them and state the trade-off.
   A: (hidden) Freedom and equality. Gain one and you lose the other. Every era picks its emphasis and pays the cost of the other. No stable middle point exists.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 9 "Socialism and History."

3. Q: Durant's definition of progress.
   A: (hidden) Increasing control of the environment by life, plus the transmission of cultural heritage to the next generation. Not moral improvement. Not happiness. Progress is technical and cultural transmission, not virtue.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 13 "Is Progress Real?"

4. Q: Durant on religion's function in history. What happens when it declines?
   A: (hidden) Religion acts as social cement. It declines as wealth and security rise, but its structural function (binding the group, transmitting moral training to children, consoling the suffering) reappears in secular forms. The function is structural and it does not disappear with the institution.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 7 "Religion and History."

## Apply this week

Before adding any new tool or agent to your stack this week, write one sentence naming the verifiable goal of one agent you already have. "This agent succeeds when X." If you cannot write that sentence clearly, the agent is architectural bloat. The MAS paper names this failure mode exactly, and it applies at four agents just as much as at forty.

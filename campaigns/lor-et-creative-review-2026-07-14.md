# LOR Early Talent 2026: Creative Review

Synthesis of four specialist reviews (hook audit, market landscape, strategy, authenticity) against the 21-slide master plan and the 9 July call.

## 1. Verdict

The system is right and the market has since proved it: named voices over the company page, filter-as-you-attract, window-gated commissioning, and the assessor-transparency material are all validated by 2026 platform mechanics and by what universities asked LOR for directly. The best assets in the deck are the ones with receipts on screen: the assessment sheet, the payslip, real grades, the ledger. What is dated is the surface: first-order myth-busting (wellies, all-men), the word "ghostwritten", a brand anthem launch film, and "more than you think" as visible language are all 2019-era employer branding that a 16 to 24 audience pre-predicts and platforms now punish. The sequencing is also backwards: every trust asset lands at or after the 21 September open, and the deepest filter content lands in October, after graduates have applied. Finally, the slate still carries the June deck, not the 9 July client: the factory strand is dead, the spotlight roster mismatches Emma's four roles, building services has zero hooks in 50 lines, and the self-filmed tier and Facebook exist nowhere in it.

## 2. KEEP

- **The theme as editorial rule.** Name the assumption, then break it. Direct contrast is the strongest hook mechanic going. It survives only as a rule, never as printed or spoken language.
- **The slide 13 manager strand.** Assessment sheet on the table, one real question with a strong answer beside a weak one, the AI-application stack from the reading side, "I've turned down better grades". No competitor publishes this, universities asked for it, and it got more valuable after the 20,000-applications revelation. The crown jewels. Defend through any trim.
- **Line 19, the strong answer beside the AI version.** The whole campaign thesis in one artefact. Client confirmed they want it on the call.
- **Pay on screen.** The payslip vertical, "pay answered straight", the ledger. 44 per cent of Gen Z abandon applications with no salary range. Load-bearing, not nice-to-have. Flag salary sign-off to Kerri before the August shoot.
- **Line 15.** "My mates are in a lecture hall. I'm paid to build the hospital." Best peer contrast in the slate. Named project on screen.
- **Named voices over the company page.** Now structurally enforced: pages get roughly 5 per cent of LinkedIn feed allocation against 65 per cent for people, and a vertical video lane launched this year. Every asset gets a person-published 9:16 LinkedIn cut.
- **Window-gated commissioning and applications-not-likes measurement.** Untouched. It is the commercial spine.
- **The First Fortnight concept and Q&A verticals from real comments.** Both strong, both native formats. First Fortnight moves if induction slips to December or January; verify with Kerri now.
- **Facebook for parents.** Jane's addition is evidence-backed: parents are the number one career influence and the least informed on non-university routes.

## 3. KILL

A high-shock hook pulling candidates toward jobs that barely exist fails the campaign's own quality goal. Redeploy the budget into the Dartford office day. The strategy review said cut the line, the hook audit rewrote it. Resolved: the line dies, the rewrite lives, because as a written post it costs nearly nothing and carries the campaign's best contrarian claim (hook 1 below).
- **Line 28's first-order myths.** Wellies, all-men, dead end. A decade of CITB and contractor content means the flip is predicted before it lands. The mixed cast busts the all-men myth silently as B-roll. Replaced by second-order myths (upgrade 3).
- **The word and practice "ghostwritten".** LinkedIn now suppresses content that reads AI-generated, and a 19 year old in agency register reads fake. Replaced by voice-edited (upgrade 4).
- **Spotlights 29 (Digital Engineer) and 32 (Planner).** Off Emma's list. Her four roles overwrite the pencil, as slide 19 already commits.
- **Line 35 as a standalone post.** The retention stat folds into the manager post and the teacher one-pager.

## 4. UPGRADE

**1. Launch centrepiece: the relay film replaces the anthem.** One day, one project, passed between four people, office to site. One flagship film plus four verticals, numbered "One project, four jobs, 1 of 4". It answers Emma's fewer-videos note, her whole-day brief, and her four roles in a single held-constant format, and it carries the contrast without needing factory access.

**2. Resequence trust before the open.** Lock the manager half-day against the early-August site day (slide 13 already permits Shoot Day 1 or 3). Publish the assessment-sheet and interview-question cuts in the 1 to 20 September warm-up, before applications open, not October. Add two to four self-drafted grad posts (Sarah's mechanism from the call) landing late August. This is the highest-leverage move in the whole review and costs nothing extra.

**3. Myth verticals become second-order myths.** Replace wellies, all-men, dead end with: the Plan B myth ("My teachers called it a waste of my potential. I'm the only one of my mates being paid to study.", Facebook-first for parents), the AI myth ("Our robots created roles we still can't fill.", reusing banked footage), and the grades myth ("Nobody on this site has asked about my grades since the day I got in. Here's what they actually check."). Same slots, same budget, myths nobody in the category busts.

**4. Voice-edited, not ghostwritten.** Two permitted modes only. Transcript-built: assembled from things the person actually said on camera, reordered and trimmed, never smoothed. Self-drafted: they write it, HWL tightens length and adds the link, phrasing stays theirs. Nothing publishes under a name the person did not say or write. Same deliverables, less HWL writing time, and the shoot-day transcripts become a post bank at near-zero cost.

**5. Theme slide honesty fix.** "No degree. No debt. Chartered engineer by 27" is false; the route is a funded degree. New line: "No UCAS. No debt. Chartered by 27. Here is the maths." Same rhythm, now true, and it removes the one line a sceptical parent could publicly correct.

**6. The pact as the applications-open post, plus a provenance end-frame.** 21 September opens with a two-way promise: our side, every post this year is a named real person, no AI footage, no stock, and we publish what our assessors actually score; your side, the new questions cannot be answered by a chatbot, tell us something only you can say. Held under the line "AI as tool, not as voice", never anti-AI. A factual end-frame ships in the toolkit across all tiers: "Filmed 5 August 2026 at [site]. Real employees, real roles. No AI-generated footage."

**7. YouTube gets a packaging pass.** Keep no-feed-strategy as posture, but a candidate deciding whether to spend hours on a genuine application will search the employer, and that is the highest-quality trust minute available. Route-based titles, thumbnails before edit lock, one playlist per route, end screens chaining DITL to how-to-apply.

**8. Parents get documents, downsides and other parents.** Printable ledger PDF on Facebook, a teacher one-pager with entry requirements and retention rates, one admitted-downside post ("here is the catch: 6am starts, exams on top of a working week"), and one "Ask my mum" piece captured in an hour at the cohort shoot. The parent voice is absent from the entire competitor set.

Volume net effect: roughly 85 posts becomes 55 to 60, with more effective watch minutes, which is the budget trim answered creatively rather than defensively.

**2. **3. Intake Diaries.** Format: the self-filmed cohort tier as a named numbered series ("Week 3, shot by Dan"), vertical not landscape (the call note is corrected; this lives on Reels), face-first two-second opener, prompts not scripts, two takes maximum, one imperfection left in, brand fonts banned except the factual end-card, published from the cohort's own accounts with collab tags. Absorbs lines 21, 25 and 40 into one longitudinal strand. Cadence: weekly from late September. Why it compounds: a diary is inherently serial, it is the campaign's cheapest trust accumulator (one-page brief, light normalising edit), it is 2026 infrastructure not a budget fallback (the Waitrose, Staples and Sanofi pattern, with 89 per cent of students wanting content from early-career employees), and it covers the intake's first weeks even if the cohort shoot slips to a December induction.

## 7. THE RECUT PITCH

Since the ninth we've rebuilt the creative around what you told us, and it's sharper for it. The theme stays, but as a discipline, not a slogan: every post names something people believe about this company and proves it wrong on screen, with receipts, real criteria, real payslips, real grades, never the phrase itself. The launch film is gone and it should be; in its place, one day on one project passed between four people in your four focus roles, one film, four verticals, and building services finally gets the spotlight it's never had, opening with the honest line that you struggled to fill it because nobody knows it exists. The biggest change is sequence: the assessor material, the sheet on the table, the strong answer beside the ChatGPT one, all publishes before 21 September, not after, because that's the content that makes the wrong 19,000 scroll past and the right few hundred rehearse. Everything is now either polished and disclosing or genuinely self-made by the cohort, nothing in the corporate middle, which is exactly the register every other contractor is stuck in. Fewer assets than the June plan, more watch time per asset, same budget shape, and we open the window with a promise that cuts both ways: everything we post this year is a real person and no AI footage, and your new questions can't be answered by a chatbot, so tell us something only you can say.

## Verdict on the theme: 'More than you think' survives as an editorial rule, fails as surface language, and needs three 2026 constraints

As an editorial rule (slide 04: name the assumption, then break it) the theme is exactly the direct-contrast mechanic in the framework, which is the strongest contrast form (push to +100, flip to -100). Keep it. Three problems against 2026 attention patterns. One: as surface language, 'more than X' constructions are the single most common employer-brand trope in existence; Gen Z pattern-matches the phrase as advertising in one frame. The deck already says the theme is not a tagline to print, but it leaks: the line 12 film title and the closer slide ('More than they think') both surface it. Rule: the phrase never appears as a spoken or text hook, only the broken assumption does. Two: the myth-bust GENRE is saturated in this category. When a viewer has seen fifty 'construction isn't what you think' posts, the flip is predicted before it lands, which zeroes the contrast even on true claims. The 2026 fix visible in this deck's own best lines is receipts over claims: the criteria sheet on the table, the payslip, real grades, real ledgers (Investigator and Experimenter archetypes). Restate the execution rule as 'prove the assumption wrong on screen', not 'assert more-ness'. Impose an already-believe ceiling: no hook ships if roughly 40+ of 100 target viewers already believe the claim or can predict the flip from the setup. Three: the campaign goal (fewer, better-fitted applications against 20,000+ AI-assisted ones) means every hook has a second job, filtering. A high-shock hook that attracts wrong-fit viewers, like factory glamour where few early-talent jobs exist, fails even when it wins attention. Add a filter-honesty check to the execution rule: does the person this hook stops actually match a role LOR needs filled? The theme also survives the 9 July narrowing to four focus roles, because assumption-break works per role, and building services is arguably the purest 'more than you think' role in the company.

*Implication: Keep the theme, add three lines to the execution rule on slide 04: never surface the phrase, receipts on screen beat asserted contrast, and every hook passes a filter-honesty check. That turns the theme from a 2019-era employer-brand device into a 2026-proof editorial standard, and it is a one-slide edit.*

# Appendix B: 2026 landscape findings (web-researched, sources in-line)

## LinkedIn company-page reach has collapsed; a vertical video lane launched in 2026

Between 2024 and 2026 LinkedIn company-page organic reach dropped roughly 60-66%. Personal profiles now receive ~65% of feed allocation vs ~5% for company pages. In 2026 LinkedIn launched a dedicated vertical video feed: 9:16 videos under 3 minutes are auto-enrolled and shown in a swipeable reels strip above text posts, and top pages posting video get nearly double normal reach, though video views overall fell 36% YoY as competition rose. The algorithm favours native video, dwell time and educational content, and punishes external links; 30-90s clips work best for discovery, 2-5 min for warm audiences. Sources: thestateofbrand.com/news/linkedin-killing-video-reach, viralbrain.ai/guides/linkedin-algorithm-2026, dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now, authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-video-posts.

*Implication: The plan's named-voices-over-company-page bet is now measurably correct, and the deck's '5% of followers' stat can be hardened to 'pages get 5% of feed allocation vs 65% for people'. Action: every DITL and myth vertical gets a 9:16 sub-3-minute native LinkedIn cut (not just IG Reels), published through Kerri/Sarah/grad/line-manager personal profiles with the page resharing. Never put the application link in the post body; first comment or profile link instead.*

## Day-in-the-life is not saturated for careers content, but the grammar has shifted to anti-polish realism

The ABPI 'Engaging with Gen Z' report (June 2025, UK) found day-in-the-life posts from ordinary employees are the single most effective content type for career exploration, beating all formal corporate content; 36% of UK young people have used social media to make a career/job decision, and TikTok (50%) and Instagram (46%) now beat LinkedIn (36%) for career inspiration. Simultaneously, 2026 branding coverage shows the format evolving rather than dying: 'anti-polish', phone-shot, imperfections-in DITL outperforms overscripted versions, and audiences read heavy edit as inauthentic. Sources: abpi.org.uk/media/news/2025/june/gen-z-are-turning-to-tiktok-for-career-advice-and-inspiration-finds-new-report/, abpi.org.uk/media/xvoldd3d/engaging-with-gen-z-cultivating-a-diverse-talent-pipeline.pdf, digital-business-lab.com/2026/01/branding-trends-rise-authenticity-purpose-driven-strategies/, thesocialcat.com/glossary/day-in-the-life.

Cinema-grade polish should live in the environment shots (site scale, factory), not the person.*

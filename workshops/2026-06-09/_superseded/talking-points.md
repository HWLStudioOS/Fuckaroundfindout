# Talking points spine

Spine, not a script. The demo is the content. Five blocks. Edit the lines into your own mouth, then rehearse against this.

---

## Block 1 — Open + frame (0:00-0:10)

**The one-line frame:** Most founders treat content like a thing they have to grind out. I treat it like a system that runs. Three pieces make it run, and each one is useless without the other two.

- **The tool.** Claude Code, not the chat box. The difference: the chat forgets you every session; the tool reads your actual files and works inside your business. It thinks with you, it does not just answer you.
- **The folder.** A handful of plain text files that hold your business context: what you're trying to do, how you sound, the rules you never break, the rhythm. This is what stops the AI producing generic slop. The AI is only as good as the context you give it, and the folder IS the context, written once.
- **The rhythm.** One idea every two weeks, cascaded into 7 to 10 posts. Not five new ideas a week. That is the trap that burns you out by Wednesday.

**Why each fails alone:** Tool without folder = a smart assistant guessing about your business. Folder without rhythm = great context, no output cadence. Rhythm without the tool = back to writing everything by hand.

**The honest opener (peer-signal, not guru):** I am not going to teach you to go viral. I am going to open the actual folder that runs the content side of my studio, run it live, and show you the shape so you can build your own. No template to copy, the copy is the point, you build yours around your business, not mine.

## Block 2 — Folder tour (0:10-0:28, ~18 min)

Open the real `/content` folder on screen. **Narrate three decisions, not file names.** The format the council ratified: "I killed X because Y. I added X because Y. I never touch X because Y."

**Files in scope (open these, in this order):** `strategy.md`, `voice-dna.md`, `publishing-rules.md`, `waterfall.md`.

**Decision 1 — what I killed.** I used to think the answer was post more, post more often. I killed the every-week essay. Chasing a fresh idea every few days is how you end up posting nothing for a fortnight and calling it a busy week. Now it is one idea per fortnight that cascades. `waterfall.md` is that rule written down so I cannot argue with it on a Tuesday.

**Decision 2 — what I added.** I added `voice-dna.md`. It is the openers, closers, and phrases pulled from twenty emails I actually sent. Without it, the AI writes like LinkedIn, that flat, confident, says-nothing voice everyone can smell. The voice file is the single thing that makes the output sound like me instead of slop. If you build one file, build this one.

**Decision 3 — what I never touch.** I never edit `publishing-rules.md` mid-draft. No em dashes. No engagement bait. No "I just started a business six months ago" content. Set once, and the engine checks every output against them. The moment you start negotiating the rules per post is the moment the system stops protecting you from your own worst instincts.

**One sentence on strategy.md (don't dwell):** this holds who the audience might be and the pillars, so the AI argues from my actual position, not a generic content-marketer position.

**Guard rail (rehearse the nav path):** only the `/content` view is open. `capture/`, `money/`, `council/`, `business/` stay closed. Client names in `voice-dna.md` are generalised for the share.

## Block 3 — The engine firing (0:28-0:55, ~27 min) — THE MONEY MOMENT

One real prompt fires. It reads `strategy.md` + `voice-dna.md` + `waterfall.md` and cascades one idea into four outputs: a Substack draft, a cinematic essay script, a field-note reel script, and an X thread.

- **The cached prompt + the backup recording both exist.** See `workshops/2026-06-09/dry-run-1.md` for the exact prompt and a full clean run. If the live one breaks, the backup recording plays in under 30 seconds. (This is the riskiest live moment, and it is now de-risked.)
- **What to narrate while it runs:** "Watch what it is pulling from. It is not inventing a voice, it is reading mine. It is not guessing the format, the waterfall file told it. One idea, four finished drafts, in the time it takes to make a coffee."
- **The point to land:** this is not the AI being clever. It is the AI having context. The cleverness is in the folder, written once. The output is just the folder, applied.
- **End on the honest caveat:** these are drafts, not posts. I still cut, still tighten, still kill the weak one. The engine gets me to a strong first draft in two minutes instead of a blank page in two hours. That is the whole game.

## Block 4 — The replicable pattern (0:55-1:10, ~15 min)

The three things anyone in this room can build, no code background required:

1. **The folder.** Four files: what you're doing, how you sound, the rules, the rhythm. Plain text. You can write the first version in an afternoon.
2. **The fortnight rhythm.** One idea, cascaded. Pick the cadence you can actually hold, then never break it.
3. **The thinking partner.** The tool that reads the folder. The setup is a session or two, not ninety minutes, and I will say honestly what it takes.

**The honest line:** you will not walk out and have this tonight. What you walk out with is the shape, and the knowledge that the hard part is not the AI, it is deciding what you stand for clearly enough to write it down. The folder is just your thinking, externalised so the machine can use it.

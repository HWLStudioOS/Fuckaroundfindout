## Draft

"What doesn't kill you makes you stronger" might be the most repeated advice in working life.

Tasha Eurich's research says it is backwards.

Chronic pressure without recovery does not build people. It drains them. Resilience behaves less like a personality trait and more like a budget: every unresolved stressor spends from it, and nothing refills it by itself.

The workplace version is the praise trap. When coping gets rewarded, people learn to hide what it costs. "She's so resilient" quietly becomes a reason to keep loading the same person.

Tasha adds the reason we miss it. Almost all of us believe we are self-aware. Her research puts the real number at 10 to 15 percent. You cannot manage a cost you refuse to see, and the outside view catches what reflection misses.

Her advice to leaders is structural, not motivational. Fix the load, not the person.

Three questions for the week:

- Which chronic stressor could we remove at source rather than coach people through?
- Who on the team is praised for endurance, and what is it costing them?
- When did we last ask someone for the outside view on ourselves?

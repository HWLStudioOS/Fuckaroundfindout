# Sales, Audience, And Positioning

Date started: 2026-05-11.

## Client Acquisition History

Current client acquisition has been referral-led:

- Auntie introduced or gave Harrison Better At Work.
- Better At Work led to Creepers.
- Better At Work led to Laing O'Rourke.

Interpretation:

- Harrison's business has grown through trust transfer.
- The obvious growth system is not cold outreach first. It is turning trust-transfer into a repeatable referral, authority, and case-study engine.

## Sales Pattern

Harrison says:

- Clients generally pay up front.
- He cannot remember major objections.
- He believes he is strong at sales.

Working hypothesis:

- Harrison is selling confidence, clarity, and momentum before he is selling a precise deliverable.
- This is powerful, but it can become dangerous if the backend delivery system does not match the front-end trust.

## Buyer Language

Clients describe the problem as:

- Annoying.
- Time intensive.
- "We don't really know what we're doing."
- "Can you help provide clarity?"

This suggests the market pain is less "we need content" and more:

- We lack ownership.
- We lack confidence.
- We lack rhythm.
- We lack modern taste.
- We need someone to make this less vague.

## Harrison's Core Belief

Businesses that do not produce content consistently will become invisible and irrelevant.

Reason:

- The internet is increasingly the source layer for AI systems and human trust.
- If a company is not visible, clear, and legible online, it will be absent from future discovery, trust, and decision systems.

More direct phrasing:

- "If you are not publishing, you are not training the market to understand you."
- "In the AI era, invisible companies become unqueryable companies."
- "Content is no longer just marketing. It is organizational legibility."

## Useful Contradiction

Harrison also believes content is easier than businesses realize and that reps matter more than overbuilt strategy.

This does not kill the offer. It sharpens it.

Positioning:

- The intellectual part of content is simpler than companies think.
- The organizational execution is harder than companies admit.
- HWL Studio helps serious organizations do the simple thing consistently, safely, and well.

## Organizations To Avoid

- Small fitness creators.

Possible broader avoid list:

- Low-budget personal brands.
- Creator clients who want output but lack seriousness.
- Clients whose taste or category does not justify strategic work.
- Clients who need hand-holding but cannot pay for it.

## Organizations Harrison Would Be Proud To Work With

- Major global organizations.
- Examples mentioned: Laing O'Rourke, Nike, Adidas.
- Harrison also values being paid well, even if the client is not culturally glamorous.

Interpretation:

- Prestige matters, but commercial quality matters more.
- A "boring" client can be strategically great if it has money, trust, access, and narrative depth.

## Outcome To Own

Primary outcome:

- Trust.

Secondary outcomes:

- Sales.
- Authority.
- Recruitment.
- Internal alignment.

Positioning implication:

- Do not sell "posts."
- Sell trust infrastructure.
- Sell organizational legibility.
- Sell a repeatable system for earning attention and confidence before buyers, talent, partners, and AI/search systems need the company.

## Public Audience Direction

Potential audience:

- Corporate comms leaders.
- Founder-led B2B companies.
- Marketing leaders in serious/legacy organizations.
- Operators trying to build AI-enabled content systems.
- Ambitious intelligent generalists who like Harrison's broader worldview.

Initial content lane:

- Field notes from building content/comms systems inside real businesses.
- Lessons from making "boring" industries legible and interesting.
- AI-era visibility, trust, and organizational storytelling.
- The operator's version of content: less guru, more reps.

## Case Study Direction

Strongest candidate:

- Laing O'Rourke.

Possible non-confidential angles:

- What Harrison learned designing social/content strategy for a multi-billion-pound construction business.
- Why legacy industries need content systems more than they need "viral ideas."
- How corporate comms teams can become more visible without losing seriousness.
- How internal trust and approvals shape external content.

Constraints:

- No confidential board strategy.
- No unapproved internal details.
- No overclaiming analytics outcomes.
- Focus on lessons, process, and generalizable principles.

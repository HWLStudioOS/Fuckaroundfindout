# Hybrid training evidence, 27 July 2026

Question: how should Harrison improve VO2 max and long-run fitness, lose weight, and regain consistent strength without turning each goal into a separate full programme?

## Decision

Use four runs and two full-body strength sessions each week. Keep one VO2 session, one long run and two easy runs. Strength work stays low enough in volume to recover from but heavy enough to preserve and rebuild skill in the main lifts. Use a modest energy deficit, high protein and carbohydrate around the two key runs.

This restores the useful structure already built in Baseline's `LongStrongPlan.swift`. The discarded six-week maintenance plan was too flat and capped to serve the stated goals.

## Evidence that changes the programme

1. **Concurrent training is viable.** A 2026 umbrella review covering 17 meta-analyses and 1,492 healthy participants found comparable aerobic improvements from concurrent and endurance-only training. The practical interference concern is power and high-end lower-body adaptation, not a blanket reason to avoid combining running and lifting. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41762427/
2. **Strength helps runners.** A 2025 umbrella review found moderate to large effects on running economy across the included reviews, while VO2 max itself was generally maintained rather than raised by strength work. This supports two strength sessions as an endurance aid and muscle-preservation tool, not as the main VO2 stimulus. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40153564/
3. **One real aerobic interval session earns its place.** The classic randomised 4 x 4 minute trial found larger VO2 max gains from aerobic intervals than matched long-slow or threshold work. More recent reviews still support HIIT as useful, but continuous training also improves economy. The answer is one interval day inside a mostly easy week, not repeated maximal sessions. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17414804/
4. **High-load and combined strength methods can improve running economy.** A 2024 meta-analysis found small to moderate improvements, with high-load work especially useful at faster running speeds. Harrison's main lifts can remain, with low volume and two reps in reserve. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165636/
5. **Energy deficit changes the muscle equation.** A 2025 meta-regression across 29 resistance-training studies found a greater probability of preserving fat-free mass as protein intake rose, although the authors call the result exploratory because the studies were heterogeneous. This supports a modest deficit and roughly 1.8 to 2.0 g protein per kg body mass, not an aggressive cut. Source: https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888

## Practical interpretation

- Running owns VO2 max and long-run durability.
- Strength owns muscle retention, lift skill and running economy support.
- The calorie deficit owns weight loss.
- Protein and resistance training protect lean mass.
- Easy volume makes the hard session absorbable.
- Two hard running days are unnecessary for this first eight-week block.
- No 1RM testing while body weight is dropping and the running load is rebuilding.

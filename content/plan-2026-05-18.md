## 2. The three formats you actually ship

Concrete, named, with script templates.

### Format 1. FIELD NOTE (Tier A)
**Cadence:** 1 per week.
**Length:** 45 to 75 seconds.
**Visual:** Phone handheld, environment that matches the note (desk, walk, gym, plane). Single bold caption, word-by-word.

**Script skeleton:**
```
[Hook, 0 to 3s, opening sentence is the post]
[Specific moment that set up the pattern, 8 to 15s]
[The observed pattern, named clearly, 15 to 30s]
[One concrete consequence or counter-move, 10 to 20s]
[End on the line, no CTA, no "follow for more"]
```

**Worked example, written in your voice:**

> "Three months in, I've worked out which clients send me work I'm proud of and which ones send me work I survive.
> 
> The split isn't budget. It's whether the founder makes decisions on the call or takes everything back to a Slack channel of stakeholders.
> 
> The decision-on-the-call ones get my best work because the loop is tight.
> 
> The Slack-channel ones get my B-game because by the time the brief comes back, I've forgotten why I cared.
> 
> Now I screen for it in the first conversation."

That's 67 seconds when read at normal cadence. Zero em dashes. Specific. Field-notes posture. Codex H8 safe.

### Format 2. CINEMATIC ESSAY (Tier B)
**Cadence:** 1 every 2 weeks. The slow one.
**Length:** 60 to 150 seconds.
**Visual:** Sourced B-roll cut to your voiceover, OR your own footage from runs, training, London, NZ. Letterboxed 9:16 with bars top and bottom, italic serif title cards punctuating the cuts.

**Script skeleton:**
```
[Cold open shot, no words, 2 to 4s]
[Title card, italic serif: the question or the claim]
[Voiceover stanza 1, 15 to 25s, the observation]
[Voiceover stanza 2, 15 to 25s, the tension]
[Voiceover stanza 3, 15 to 25s, the resolution or the unanswered question]
[Final shot holds 2 to 3s, no music tail]
```

**Topic ideas pulled from your pillars:**
- The competence-financial-admin gap (codex H7) as a meditation on bookkeeping vs. craft.
- The marathon training / business analogy you keep returning to.
- The "elegant chase for better" tagline as a 90-second piece.
- "AI through the lens of real work" using clips of operators (not AI imagery slop).

### Format 3. EDITORIAL POST (Tier C)
**Cadence:** 1 per week, posted to LinkedIn + IG carousel.
**Length:** 10 to 20 seconds of motion graphic or one striking image.
**The work is in the caption.**

**Caption skeleton (stolen straight from Orbit Media):**
```
[One-line claim, declarative]

[Setup, 1 to 2 lines, sets the world]
[Setup continued]

[The pattern you've observed, 2 to 3 lines]

[The shift it implies, 2 to 3 lines]

[The closing sentence, half the length of the rest]
```

**Worked example:**

> The best agencies don't pitch, they get pulled.
>
> Capacity is no longer the constraint.
> Good operators aren't either.
>
> What's scarce is taste applied to a specific problem,
> visible from outside the room.
>
> When founders can see how you think
> before they have to ask,
> the proposal is a formality.
>
> Distribution is the new business development.

No em dashes. Stanza spacing. Specific. Ends on a line you'd say in conversation.

## 3. Visual + typographic system

### Fonts
You wanted "studio font or personal font". I don't have either confirmed in `content/` yet. Two paths:

- If HWL Studio has a brand font already (check your brand assets folder, I'll set it once you tell me), use it for Tier C editorial posts and Tier B title cards.
- For Tier A captions, neo-grotesque bold sans is the universal aesthetic across your 21 inspo posts. Free options: **Inter Bold** (open source, clean), **Söhne** (paid, what Tom Flower-tier creators tend to use), **Helvetica Now Display Bold**.
- For Tier B italic title cards: **GT Sectra Display Italic**, **Domaine Display Italic**, or free **EB Garamond Italic**.

Decision: pick one sans + one italic serif and lock it. The cross-tier consistency is what makes a small body of work feel like a body of work.

### Caption rendering
- Word-by-word reveal timed to spoken syllable, not block-by-block.
- White fill, 2 to 4px black stroke OR drop shadow at 60% opacity for legibility on bright B-roll.
- Position: centre or lower-third, never top (top = TikTok creator slop tier).
- Highlight one word per sentence in your accent colour (Kirk Lepiten uses yellow, look at frame DW1zYyEERei). Pick an HWL accent and stick to it.

### Grade
- Lifted blacks (around RGB 18 to 22, not pure 0).
- Warm shadows (orange-yellow tint), cool highlights (slight blue).
- Subtle film grain at 5 to 8%.
- Halation on highlights at 3 to 5% if shooting digital.
- A single LUT applied everywhere. Recommend the free **Dehancer Lite** or paid **Filmbox** for emulation; either gives the look Felix Verhous and Teo Crawford have.

### Aspect ratios
- 9:16 for IG Reels and TikTok.
- 1:1 for LinkedIn (the algorithm rewards square + caption-led).
- 16:9 letterboxed inside 9:16 for the Tier B cinematic essays (gives them the "this is a film" signal).

## 4. Footage sourcing

You said "filming videos using sourced footage". The cinematic tier in your inspo set is built almost entirely on either personal travel footage (Felix, Teo, Marcel) or licensed stock. Options:

- **Artgrid** annual, around $300/yr, ungated 4K cinematic stock. This is what the cinematic tier creators use. Best ROI.
- **Filmsupply** for premium one-off licences when a specific film clip matters. Pay per piece.
- **Pexels / Pond5** free, lower hit rate, fine for filler.
- **Your own bank**: every run, every walk in London, every Maya weekend, every trip back to NZ, shoot 15 seconds of B-roll per moment in Cinematic 4K. Build a personal stock library in a Drive folder. After 90 days you have your own Artgrid.

For the Calder Dune-style film essay (sourced clips from existing films): editorial commentary fair use is the standard convention but legally grey. Acceptable for content with critical framing; don't monetise direct uploads of the source films.

## 5. Tooling pipeline

Recommended chain, in order of where each step lives:

1. **Script**: write in Apple Notes or a doc. 50 to 200 words. Read it aloud once, cut 30%.
2. **Capture**: phone + lav mic (Rode Wireless Go II if you don't have one, around £200). For cinematic stuff, your existing mirrorless if you have one, or commission B-roll from Artgrid.
3. **Edit**: CapCut Pro on iPad for Tier A speed-edits (caption auto-sync is excellent). Premiere Pro for Tier B cinematic essays. Both export the same 9:16.
4. **Caption render**: CapCut auto-caption then manually time word-by-word. For Tier B title cards, do them in After Effects or Premiere Essential Graphics.
5. **Caption text** (the post body): drafted by me on request using `content/voice-dna.md` rules. Stanza style. No em dashes.
6. **Post**: Buffer or Metricool for cross-posting LinkedIn + IG + Threads + X simultaneously. Maintain native LinkedIn upload (don't link out), it kills reach.

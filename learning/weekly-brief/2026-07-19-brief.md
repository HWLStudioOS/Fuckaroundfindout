## Items

- **Agent Data Injection Attacks are Realistic Threats to AI Agents**, paper, Woohyuk Choi, Juhee Kim, Taehyun Kang, Jihyeon Jeong, Luyi Xing, Byoungyoung Lee, arXiv:2607.05120. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05120)
  Submitted 6 July 2026. Names a new attack class: malicious instructions disguised as trusted metadata (a resource identifier, a data-origin field) rather than obvious injected text, which slips past defences built to filter untrusted content. Demonstrated working exploits, including remote code execution and supply-chain attacks, against Claude Code, Claude in Chrome, and Codex specifically. The finding isn't that injection exists, it's that current agents have no structural separation between trusted and untrusted data at all. Harrison runs Claude Code daily plus a 24/7 Telegram-triggered agent with file and tool access, exactly the shape of system this paper breaks.

- **Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement**, essay, Lilian Weng. [https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)
  Published 4 July 2026. Argues the near-term path to AI self-improvement isn't models rewriting their own weights, it's evolving the orchestration layer around them: workflow loops, file-system memory instead of context windows, sub-agent spawning. Names reward hacking (an agent editing its own verifier) and diversity collapse in evolutionary search loops as the concrete failure modes, and prescribes keeping test suites and logs read-only as the discipline against both. Close to a design document for the exact system Harrison is building: four unattended launchd agents, one of which writes its own output file every week.

- **The Real Thucydides Trap: How Overconfidence Could Draw America and China Into a War**, essay, Joshua Rovner, Foreign Affairs. [https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/real-thucydides-trap](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/real-thucydides-trap)
  Published 14 April 2026. Rovner goes back to the decade before the Peloponnesian War and finds no power transition underway at all, Athens and Sparta had settled into a stable balance, sea versus land. The war came from both sides wrongly believing they could win fast and cheap against the other's core strength. Allison's whole "rising power threatens established power" mechanism, the spine of Destined for War, is built on a reading of the founding case that a specialist in the field calls simply wrong. Read this before the book, not after.

- **The Collapse of Ego Depletion: Science's Biggest Self-Control Failure**, essay, Michael Inzlicht. [https://www.speakandregret.michaelinzlicht.com/p/the-collapse-of-ego-depletion](https://www.speakandregret.michaelinzlicht.com/p/the-collapse-of-ego-depletion)
  Published 29 January 2025. Inzlicht helped build the "willpower is a finite resource that depletes with use" theory in the 1990s, then watched it fail under a 24-lab, 2,000-plus-participant preregistered replication that found no meaningful effect. He names the cause plainly: a seductive idea, weak manipulations, and publication bias, not a conspiracy. Directly undercuts the willpower-as-a-muscle-you-grind-down model underneath most Goggins-style self-command content Harrison reads, from the researcher who built and then dismantled it himself.

- **Carbon plates in running shoes biomechanics: a systematic review and meta-analysis**, meta-analysis, Frontiers in Sports and Active Living. [https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1764338/full](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1764338/full)
  Published 29 May 2026. Pooling 15 crossover trials, carbon plates produced no significant change in leg stiffness, knee power, hip power, or step frequency versus non-plated shoes, only a borderline reduction in ankle power. Relevant to Harrison's own plateless choices (Aero Glide 4, Agravic TT trial): the usual justification for going plateless, that plates redistribute load in ways an ITB or patellar issue can't handle, isn't well supported by the pooled biomechanical evidence either way. Comfort and injury history remain the honest reasons, not joint mechanics.

- **How GPT-5.6 Changes Knowledge Work**, essay, Dan Shipper, Every. [https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-gpt-5-6-changes-knowledge-work](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-gpt-5-6-changes-knowledge-work)
  Published 10 July 2026. Shipper's line: "Your job turns from doing the work to tending the system that does it for you." Not a new idea, managers have worked this way for decades, but he argues capable agents now put that operating mode within reach of any individual knowledge worker. Ships a working open-source tool (Tend) built around real recurring workflows: inbox triage, hiring pipelines, customer queues. Close to a literal blueprint for how HWL Studio's own agent stack should be structured at the next stage of scale: review the loop's output, don't do the loop's job.

- **Observation of Floquet rotational super-radiance**, paper, Hadiseh Nasari, Hady Moussa, et al., Nature. [https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260711010120.htm](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260711010120.htm)
  Published 8 July 2026 in Nature, covered 12 July. Physicists at CUNY built a stationary ring of electronic resonators, modulated in space and time, that mimics ultrafast rotation without anything physically spinning, and used it to recreate the Penrose-Zel'dovich mechanism (wave amplification by an extremely fast-spinning object, first proposed for black holes) on a benchtop, with 7.8 dB gain. Fifty years of black-hole theory turned into an experimental tool via a synthetic-rotation trick nobody had built before, opening new territory in optics, wireless comms, and quantum sensing.

- **Josh Kerr breaks the men's mile world record**, race report, Irish Times / WWD. [https://www.irishtimes.com/sport/athletics/2026/07/18/josh-kerr-makes-athletics-history-by-shattering-one-mile-world-record-in-london/](https://www.irishtimes.com/sport/athletics/2026/07/18/josh-kerr-makes-athletics-history-by-shattering-one-mile-world-record-in-london/)
  18 July 2026, London Stadium Diamond League. Kerr ran 3:42.66, breaking Hicham El Guerrouj's 3:43.13, a mark that had stood for 27 years. The mechanism, not just the scoreline: altitude training in Albuquerque, training partners paced him to a precise lap schedule, and Brooks built him a custom shoe (Hyperion 222) for this specific attempt. A 27-year record didn't fall on talent alone, it fell to a stacked, deliberately engineered attempt.

## Synthesis

- **Destined for War's founding case doesn't hold up, and three specialists say so for three different reasons**: Rovner says the mechanism was never a rising power threatening a falling one, it was mutual overconfidence about a quick win. Arthur Waldron, reviewing Allison's book directly back in 2017, cites two of the twentieth century's heaviest Thucydides scholars (Kagan, Badian) showing the "trap" isn't in the actual Greek text at all, and that the historical pattern favours accommodation over confrontation. Ryan Swan, writing this May, argues the real 2026 danger isn't war, it's that reflexive defence spending crowds out the industrial-tech investment that actually decides the rivalry. None of the three deny China's rise is real. All three say Allison's specific mechanism for how it turns into war is wrong, in different directions. Sources: [https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/real-thucydides-trap](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/real-thucydides-trap), [https://thechinaproject.com/2017/06/12/no-thucydides-trap/](https://thechinaproject.com/2017/06/12/no-thucydides-trap/), [https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2026.1822253/full](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2026.1822253/full).

- **Self-command's founding metaphors are being dismantled by the people who built them**: Inzlicht spent a career on ego depletion, watched it collapse under replication, and wrote the obituary himself. A pre-registered study across New Zealand, Norway, and the US finds naive Stoic ideology, specifically the desire to suppress emotion and feel less, correlates with lower wellbeing on both measures psychologists use, despite being a genuine misreading of the actual philosophy. Neither piece says self-command is fake. Both say the specific mental model, willpower as a depletable resource, stoicism as emotional suppression, is doing active harm relative to a more accurate version of the same idea. Worth holding loosely against the Holiday/Goggins reading list currently in rotation. Sources: [https://www.speakandregret.michaelinzlicht.com/p/the-collapse-of-ego-depletion](https://www.speakandregret.michaelinzlicht.com/p/the-collapse-of-ego-depletion), [https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-022-00563-w](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-022-00563-w).

- **The harness is now both where the leverage lives and where the exploit lives**: Lilian Weng argues the orchestration layer around a model, not the model's weights, is the actual site of near-term capability gains, and names reward hacking and read-only verifiers as the load-bearing safeguards. LangChain's Nemotron case study is the empirical proof: tuning only the harness (prompts, tool descriptions, middleware) took an open model to within one point of Opus 4.8 on their benchmark at a tenth of the cost, no retraining. The Agent Data Injection paper names the exact same layer as the current blind spot: agents have no structural separation between trusted and untrusted data, and that gap is already being exploited against Claude Code specifically. Same layer, opposite use, and only one of those two properties has had any attention on Harrison's own stack so far. Sources: [https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/), [https://www.langchain.com/blog/tuning-the-harness-not-the-model-a-nemotron-3-ultra-playbook](https://www.langchain.com/blog/tuning-the-harness-not-the-model-a-nemotron-3-ultra-playbook), [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05120).

## Recall drills

Rotating away from the five drills used in the 5 July brief (wealth concentration, greed, morals, years of peace, religion). These five haven't surfaced since 28 June or earlier.

1. Q: What are Durant's three biological lessons of history?
   A: (hidden, expand after attempt) Life is competition. Life is selection. Life must breed.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 3 "Biology and History."

2. Q: Durant's two oscillating poles of political history. Name them and state the trade-off.
   A: (hidden) Freedom and equality. Gain one and you lose the other. Every era picks its emphasis and pays the cost of the other.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 9 "Socialism and History."

3. Q: What did the Durants reject about race as a historical driver, and what did they offer instead?
   A: (hidden) They rejected racial determinism. Civilisations rise from geographic and economic accident, not racial superiority. Geography shapes economy, economy shapes culture, culture shapes character.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 4 "Race and History."

4. Q: Durant's definition of progress.
   A: (hidden) Increasing control of the environment by life, plus the transmission of cultural heritage to the next generation. Not moral improvement, not happiness.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 13 "Is Progress Real?"

5. Q: Durant's line on the conservatism of history. What's the warning about new ideas?
   A: (hidden) Out of every hundred new ideas, ninety-nine or more will be inferior to the traditional responses they propose to replace. Tradition is selection over time, not stupidity.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 5 "Character and History."

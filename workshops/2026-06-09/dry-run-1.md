## The clean run (output)

### Form A: Substack essay

**Going viral proved nothing**

Two weeks ago a film I made did 444,000 views. The next thing I posted did a fraction of that, and it only reached people who already followed me.

Most people would call the first number a win and the second one a problem. I think it is the other way around. The big number taught me almost nothing. The small number told me exactly where I stand.

Here is what actually happened. I was in the right place when a cultural moment landed, I had a camera, and I cut a sixty second film over it. It travelled. Not because of me, because of the moment. Hundreds of thousands of people felt something and the film gave the feeling a shape. That is a real skill and I am glad I have it. But it is worth being honest about what it is. It is moment-surfing. The wave does the work. You just have to be standing there with a board.

The trouble with a big wave is that it flatters you. It floods the account with reach and a few hundred new followers and a hit that feels like proof. Proof of what, though. Proof that I can ride a wave. Not proof that I have built anything that works without one.

The next post is where you find out. I shipped a different piece, no cultural moment behind it, just my own work standing on its own. The algorithm showed it to the people who already followed me and basically nobody else. It did not travel. And that is the most useful piece of information I have had in a month, because it tells me the truth the big number hid. The audience the wave brought me does not care about the work the account is actually for. They came for the moment. They will stay for more of the moment. The second they get something else, they go quiet, and the algorithm reads the quiet and stops pushing.

This is the thing nobody who sells you "go viral" will say out loud. Reach is not an audience. Views are not a business. A number that spikes on borrowed energy and collapses the moment you post your own thing is not a foundation. It is a sugar high. You can build a whole identity chasing the next spike, and plenty do, and they end up as the person who makes one kind of video, trapped by the audience their best post brought them.

So what do I watch. Not views. I watch whether the boring stuff travels. The plain piece, the field note, the thing with no moment behind it. If that finds even a small number of the right people, that is a system. If it only ever reaches the people the spike brought, the spike was a cul-de-sac with a great view.

I watch the unfollow rate too, which sounds backwards. When I post the work the account is really for and some of the wave-followers leave, that is not a loss. That is the audience correcting itself. I would rather have a thousand people who want the thing I make than a hundred thousand who want the thing I happened to film once.

None of this means the wave was a waste. The reach is real, the craft that caught it is real, and being seen never hurt. But I am building something that is meant to compound, and the only honest test of that is whether the next quiet thing finds its people on its own. The spike cannot tell me that. Only the floor can.

I will take the reach when a moment lands. I would be daft not to. But I am keeping my eye on the small number. It is the one telling the truth.

I thought I knew the difference between a plan and a strategy. Then Roger Martin gave me a fairly brutal test.

Ask the people around the table to explain the choices underneath the plan. If the answers are different, the strategy is not clear.

The currency of planning is initiatives.

Roger Martin: Why Clarity Is Not Micromanagement

### On-screen treatment

### Instagram caption

Micromanagement is not being clear about the outcome. It is telling someone every move they have to make to get there.

Roger Martin's distinction is between inputs and outputs.

Manage the inputs and people are left thinking, "What am I, a dog?"

Make the output unmistakably clear, then leave room for someone else to think.

Better Moments #2 is out now. Link in bio.

### Cathal LinkedIn caption

Roger Martin made a distinction I wish I had learned earlier.

I used to think the answer to avoiding micromanagement was to step back and give people more space. Roger's point is more precise. Be exact about the output. Stop prescribing every input.

When you manage inputs, you tell someone which steps to take. When you manage the output, you tell them what good needs to look like and leave them room to think.

That is the difference between "do these five things" and "here is the result we need, show me how you would get there".

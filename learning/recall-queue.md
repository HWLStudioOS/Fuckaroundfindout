# Recall Queue

The codex named this: Harrison wants to recall quotes, wisdom, podcast episode titles, books, lessons from history, laws of physics. Current note system (fold corners) doesn't produce recall. This file does.

## How it works

Each entry has:
- The thing to remember (quote, lesson, principle, fact).
- The source (book, podcast, conversation).
- Why it matters / where to use it.
- Date added.
- Status: new / reviewed / applied.

## Recall drills (weekly)

`agents/learning-brief.md` (when wired) pulls 3-5 items from this queue on Sundays. Format: question + answer hidden. Harrison answers in his head, then reveals. Items reviewed move from "new" → "reviewed." Items he's actually used go to "applied" with a one-line note about where.

## Active queue

### Will & Ariel Durant, The Lessons of History (seeded 2026-05-12, pre-read)

Format established here so it's ready when Harrison finishes the book. Each item has Q / A / where-to-use / source / date / status. Status starts "pending" because Harrison hasn't read the book yet. Once he has, status flips to "new" and drills enter the Sunday rotation.

1. **Q:** What are the three biological lessons of history per Durant?
   **A:** Life is competition. Life is selection. Life must breed.
   **Where to use:** Counter to "the market should be fair" framing. Inequality is biological output, not policy failure. Useful when defending tiered pricing in HWL Studio offers, audience hierarchy, ruthless prioritisation of which clients get attention.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 3 "Biology and History".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

2. **Q:** Durant's claim on wealth concentration. What's the mechanism, what's the periodic interruption?
   **A:** Wealth concentrates as a natural function of unequal ability. It's interrupted periodically by either legislation that redistributes peacefully or revolution that redistributes violently. The cycle is recurrent across civilisations.
   **Where to use:** Frame for thinking about VAT, tax reserves, and personal vs. business wealth structuring. Also a lens on AI accelerating wealth concentration in 2026.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 8 "Economics and History".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

3. **Q:** Across 3,421 years of recorded history, how many years of peace did the Durants count?
   **A:** ~268 years of peace. War is the constant, not the exception.
   **Where to use:** Calibration on "this time is different" geopolitics takes. Also disarms naive optimism in content writing about peace/progress.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 11 "History and War".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

4. **Q:** What is the only real check on greed, per Durant?
   **A:** The equally good greed of another. Competition, not virtue.
   **Where to use:** Negotiation, pricing, competitive positioning. Don't expect counterparties to act against self-interest. Build structures where their self-interest serves the outcome.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 11.
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

5. **Q:** Durant's two oscillating poles of political history. Name them and the trade-off.
   **A:** Freedom and equality. Gain one and you lose the other. Every era picks its emphasis and pays the cost of the other.
   **Where to use:** Lens for content on culture, politics, AI policy debates. Also for thinking about team structures, autonomy vs. standardisation in HWL Studio.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 9 "Socialism and History".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

6. **Q:** What did the Durants reject about race as a historical driver, and what did they offer instead?
   **A:** They rejected racial determinism. Civilisations rise from geographic and economic accident, not racial superiority. Geography shapes economy, economy shapes culture, culture shapes character.
   **Where to use:** Defends against lazy essentialist takes in any domain (national character, "founder DNA", team culture). It's almost always environment compounding over time.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 4 "Race and History".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

7. **Q:** Durant's definition of progress.
   **A:** Increasing control of the environment by life, plus the transmission of cultural heritage to the next generation. Not moral improvement. Not happiness.
   **Where to use:** Cuts through "are things getting better" arguments. Reframes parenting, content, teaching as the transmission half. Reframes systems-building (Harrison OS, HWL Studio) as the control half.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 13 "Is Progress Real?".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

8. **Q:** Durant on morals. Are they universal or contextual? What shapes them?
   **A:** Contextual. Morals are habits of action shaped by the dominant economic mode (hunting, agricultural, industrial). When the mode shifts, morals lag and feel "broken" during the transition.
   **Where to use:** Lens on the current AI-driven transition. The "lag" pattern is happening now. Useful for content on work, attention, family in the AI era.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 6 "Morals and History".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

9. **Q:** Durant on religion's function in history.
   **A:** Religion as social cement. It declines as wealth and security rise, but its function (binding the group, transmitting moral training to children, consoling the suffering) is structural and reappears in secular forms when religion proper recedes.
   **Where to use:** Lens on "secular religion" patterns (wokeness, longtermism, certain founder cults). Also on why community design matters in audience-building.
   **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 7 "Religion and History".
   **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

10. **Q:** Durant's line on the conservatism of history. What's the warning about new ideas?
    **A:** Out of every hundred new ideas, ninety-nine or more will be inferior to the traditional responses they propose to replace. Tradition is selection over time, not stupidity.
    **Where to use:** Filter on novelty in business, content, training. Don't reinvent unless you've actually understood what tradition optimised for. Direct application: be slow to throw out the old reading-and-recall ritual; be quick to throw out new productivity hacks.
    **Source:** Lessons of History, Ch. 5 "Character and History".
    **Date added:** 2026-05-12. **Status:** pending.

## Examples of what belongs here

- A specific Pressfield line on resistance.
- The Naval framing on "give me a lever long enough."
- A Goggins moment that maps to a specific failure mode.
- A physics principle (e.g., what activation energy means for system inertia).
- A historical lesson (e.g., why specific empires collapsed for specific reasons).
- A podcast episode title that exemplifies something Harrison wants to reference later.
- A Casey Neistat structural move that's worth copying.

## What doesn't belong here

- Tasks (those go to `capture/inbox.md`).
- Drafts (those go to `content/pipeline.md`).
- Client notes (those go to `business/clients/*.md`).
- General reading notes that don't have a recall hook (those go to `learning/library.md` status updates).

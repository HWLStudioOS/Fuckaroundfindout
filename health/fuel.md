# Nutrition and fuel protocol

This replaces the RTTS race-fuelling document. The goal is no longer to tolerate 80 g of carbohydrate per hour for an ultra. The goal is to lose fat while keeping enough energy available to train hard, recover and add or regain muscle.

## Starting targets

Current working body mass: 87 kg.

- Protein: 190 g every day.
- Fat: at least 70 g every day, usually 70-85 g.
- Carbohydrate: fills the remaining calories and rises on double-session, interval and long-run days.
- Fibre: 25-35 g daily, except immediately before a hard run if it causes gut trouble.
- Fruit and vegetables: at least five useful portions daily.
- Creatine monohydrate: 5 g daily.

Protein at 190 g is approximately 2.2 g/kg at the current weight. This sits inside the useful high-protein range for resistance-trained athletes dieting while training.

## Post-50K ramp, 15-26 July

Do not open with an aggressive cut while the foot and quads are still recovering and useful training is restarting.

- Use 2,500-2,700 kcal according to the dated Baseline prescription.
- Hit 190 g protein.
- Eat at least about 285 g carbohydrate on strength or return-run days, and 300 g or more on quality or long-run days.
- Keep alcohol at zero until the foot is quiet and normal training has resumed.
- Do not compensate for the race by under-eating.

This is a short maintenance-oriented ramp, not lost fat-loss time. Training begins on 16 July. The lower end of the calorie range belongs to movement or rest days, not return-run or quality days.

## Why Garmin does not set the target

The recent files in this repository were pulled around 14:00 on most days. Their `totalKilocalories` values are partial-day snapshots, not completed 24-hour expenditure. They cannot be averaged into a valid TDEE.

Even complete wearable estimates carry individual error. Garmin is useful for trends, session load and rough context. It does not earn the right to add or remove food one-for-one.

The real calibration signal is body-mass change against logged intake.

## The 14-day calibration loop

1. Weigh every morning after using the bathroom and before food or drink.
2. Ignore single readings. Compare seven-day means.
3. Hold the starting calories for two complete training weeks unless recovery clearly deteriorates.
4. Target average loss of 0.3-0.65 kg per week.
5. If loss is below 0.25 kg per week for two consecutive weeks and logging adherence is at least 90%, remove 150 kcal per day, primarily from rest-day carbohydrate or fat.
6. If loss exceeds 0.75 kg per week, or strength, sleep, libido, mood or quality-running performance deteriorates, add 150-250 kcal per day.
7. If weight is noisy but waist is shrinking and performance holds, keep calories unchanged.

The slow-versus-fast weight-loss trial in athletes favoured roughly 0.7% body mass per week over 1.4% for lean-mass and performance outcomes. Harrison's working band stays slightly below that upper rate because running quality and strength regain both matter.

## Session fuelling

### Easy run under 60 minutes

- No special intra-run fuel.
- Eat normally before or after according to comfort.
- Do not deliberately run fasted if it causes the pace or effort to drift.

### VO2, threshold or heavy strength

- Two to four hours before: 60-100 g carbohydrate plus 25-40 g protein.
- If the previous meal was more than four hours ago: add 20-30 g quick carbohydrate shortly before training.
- After: 30-40 g protein and a carbohydrate-rich meal.

### Thursday double session

- Keep at least six hours between sessions.
- Between sessions: 30-40 g protein plus 80-120 g carbohydrate across the first meal and snack.
- Keep fat and fibre moderate until the second session is complete.
- Do not save most of the day's food for the evening.
- Run first because 5K performance is the quality target. Strength C is deliberately lower-volume.

### Long run

- Under 75 minutes: water to thirst, no carbohydrate required if breakfast was normal.
- 75-120 minutes: 30-45 g carbohydrate per hour.
- Over 120 minutes, which this block rarely uses: 45-60 g per hour.
- The old ultra rate of 80 g per hour is unnecessary for this plan.

## Default meal structure

### Breakfast options

- Greek yoghurt, whey, berries, banana and measured granola.
- Eggs and egg whites in wraps, plus fruit.
- Oats, whey and banana before a quality-running day.

### Lunch and dinner template

- Lean protein: mince, chicken, fish, eggs, tofu or a similar option.
- Carbohydrate: rice, potatoes, wraps or pasta, portioned to the day's target.
- Beans or vegetables.
- A measured fat source rather than invisible cooking calories.

### Useful snacks

- Whey and fruit.
- Greek yoghurt.
- Cottage cheese.
- Rice cakes and a lean protein source.
- A measured cereal or granola portion around training.

## Simple meal distribution

Aim for four protein feedings:

- Breakfast: 40-50 g.
- Lunch: 45-55 g.
- Training snack or shake: 30-40 g.
- Dinner: 50-60 g.

The exact distribution matters less than hitting the daily target consistently.

## Hydration

- Drink to thirst across the day.
- Use urine colour and normal output as basic checks, not a demand to make urine permanently clear.
- Include salt in normal meals, particularly after hot or long training.
- For a session that produces a measured body-mass loss above 2%, replace fluid gradually over the following hours and include sodium with food or drink.
- Do not force fluid beyond thirst.

## Supplements

Useful:

- Creatine monohydrate, 5 g daily.
- Whey or another protein powder for convenience.
- Caffeine, approximately 2-3 mg/kg before selected key sessions. Avoid using it to cover poor sleep and avoid late-day doses.

Conditional:

- Vitamin D, iron, B12 or other micronutrients only from dietary context, symptoms or blood work, ideally with a clinician.
- Electrolytes for long, hot or high-sweat sessions, not as a daily health ritual.

Not useful for this goal:

- Fat burners.
- BCAAs when protein is already adequate.
- Recovery products that duplicate ordinary food.

## Performance and low-energy-availability checks

Add food and reassess if several of these appear together:

- Persistent strength decline.
- Easy pace slowing at the same heart rate.
- Repeated poor sleep or early waking beyond the light-room issue.
- Loss of libido.
- Irritability, low mood or unusual food preoccupation.
- Recurring illness.
- Bone or tendon pain.
- Resting HR rising while HRV and performance fall.

Weight loss is not successful if it destroys the training response.

## Tracking

Log for the first 14 training days:

- Calories.
- Protein, carbohydrate and fat.
- Morning body mass.
- Hunger, 1-5.
- Training energy, 1-5.
- Sleep duration.
- Session completed and performance notes.

After the food portions are calibrated, logging can become less granular if the seven-day weight and waist trends remain controlled.

## Draft

Hope can sound like the thing we reach for when there is no plan.

Jennifer Moss uses it differently.

Drawing on psychologist Charles Snyder's work, she describes hope as a cognitive process with three parts:

1. A goal worth reaching.
2. More than one pathway to it.
3. Enough agency to make a move.

This is useful because it gives leaders something concrete to inspect.

Is the goal clear enough to act on? Can people see a plan B when plan A closes? Do they own any of the decisions needed to get there?

Jennifer connects the model to two common workplace problems.

After layoffs, the people who stay often stop taking risks because they cannot see a secure path forward. Under a micromanager, people may know the goal and the route, but have no agency left.

Her smallest recommendation is also the easiest place to start. When a year-long milestone feels impossible, make the next goal small enough to complete today and make that progress visible.

Three questions for the week:

- What is the next goal we can actually finish?
- What are plans B, C and D?
- Which decision can move closer to the person doing the work?

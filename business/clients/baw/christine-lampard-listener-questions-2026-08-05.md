Better at Work answers real questions from real workplaces. Listeners send what they cannot ask out loud at work. Cathal answers plainly, in under two minutes, and the questioner stays anonymous. Four seasons of these have shaped the show's spine.

## The five types we get, with real examples

**1. The thing nobody will say**
The question behind most questions. "My manager rewards optimism and punishes bad news. How do I tell the truth without it costing me?" Or simply: "What has become hard to say in my team?"

**2. Is it them or the system?**
The show's home ground: the problem is rarely the people. "My boss is lovely and I am still burning out. Which one is broken?" "What does our system reward when nobody is watching?"

**3. Is my job actually good?**
The career scorecard questions. "Everyone says I have a great job. Why does it not feel like one?" We score it across calling, connection, contribution and control, which gives a live audience something they can do that evening.

**4. Give me the opening line**
The hard-conversation questions. "I need to ask for a pay rise, or a boundary, or help. What is the first sentence?" These land brilliantly live because the answer is concrete and instantly usable.

**5. What do I do at 9am Monday?**
The anti-advice question. "All of this sounds wise. What is the smallest useful move I can make this week?" Every segment ends here, on purpose.

## Why this works live

The questions are short, universal and slightly dangerous, the answers are practical and quick, and the format is question-first rather than expert-lecture. A live room always has its own version of type 1. That is the moment the segment exists for.

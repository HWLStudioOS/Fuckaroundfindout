Three things:

1. **Cinematic-tier work travels.** The 18 May plan said cinematic essays (Tier B) were the every-2-weeks slow piece. Arsenal showed the slow piece can be the lead piece if the moment is right. Cinematic film over a cultural moment, no face, no narrative, no risk, max reach.
2. **The origin essay isn't needed.** The thesis Pin 1 was supposed to land ("here's who I am, here's the chase") got functionally landed by Arsenal showing the work without saying anything. People who care will find the rest. Origin essay drops in priority.
3. **Status risk (H8) test passed.** The biggest fear of the original launch plan was that visible personal content would puncture the LOR-grade operator perception. Arsenal proved that visible work at scale didn't invite the imagined scrutiny. Nobody from LOR has commented or behaved differently. The risk was overstated.

## What the Arsenal post doesn't prove

Three things, equally important:

1. **It doesn't prove the system works.** It proves one cinematic cut on a peak cultural moment can travel. The system is whether *the next* piece travels. Kipling tonight is the first real test. If Kipling lands at 5-20k views, that's the actual organic floor. If Kipling lands at 100k+, then we've evidence of a wave, not a fluke.
2. **It doesn't prove the audience is the right audience.** 315 new followers is good but the question is *who*. Are they buyers (founders, comms leads, operators) or are they Arsenal fans who'll churn the moment a non-football post hits? The next 3 posts will tell us. Watch unfollow rate as much as follow rate.
3. **It doesn't replace the editorial / field-note tracks.** Cinematic on cultural moments is one tier. It does not build a body of work on its own. The FIELD NOTE and EDITORIAL formats still need to run for the system to compound. If they don't, the account becomes a "guy who makes football videos" account, not a personal brand.

## Subject

What would have to be true?

## Preview

A better question for the next plan your team calls a strategy.

## Draft

Hi,

Most organisations do not have a strategy problem because they lack a plan.

They have one because the plan avoids choices.

Roger Martin gave us a fairly brutal way to tell the difference. A strategy has to answer five connected questions:

1. What is our winning aspiration?
2. Where will we play?
3. How will we win there?
4. What capabilities do we need?
5. What systems will keep those choices alive?

If those answers do not reinforce one another, you probably have a collection of initiatives rather than a strategy.

The most useful part of Roger's approach is the question he uses when a room is stuck:

**What would have to be true for this to be a good choice?**

It changes the conversation. People stop defending positions and start testing assumptions. The disagreement becomes something the team can investigate.

Try it this week. Take one decision your team keeps circling and write down the two or three things that would need to be true for each option to work. Then decide which assumptions you can actually test.

## The gap, stated plainly

Ceiling: £1,000/month all-in, before recoverable VAT.
Second room: £1,404/month all-in (£1,300 rent plus 8% service fee), before Wi-Fi, before VAT.
Gap: £404/month over. £4,848/year over, before Wi-Fi is even added.

If VAT applies on top at 20%, the monthly figure becomes £1,684.80 and the gap widens to £684.80/month, £8,217.60/year over. This is why VAT treatment is not a small unknown, it can nearly double the size of the overage.

## Five unknowns, and why each one moves the decision

1. **Wi-Fi/fibre upload speed.** The whole reason for this hunt is the RAW-footage bottleneck at the flat. If the studio connection is contended, asymmetric, or no faster than a decent home line, it does not solve the problem it exists to solve. Eat Work Art's own pages promise an "independent internet connection" but give no number. This needs a live test from the room itself, not a claim taken on trust.

2. **Deposit.** Eat Work Art's published standard for private studios is three months' rent. At £1,404/month that is £4,212 due upfront. Against an operating balance of roughly £2,614 as of 10 August and a £5,000 operating-floor target that is not currently met, a three-month deposit could rule this room out on cash grounds alone, independent of the monthly gap.

3. **Minimum term.** Eat Work Art's published standard for private studios is a fixed 1-2 year term. The studio-hunt brief itself called for a rolling or one-to-three-month managed licence, specifically to avoid a lease-scale commitment before the room has proved useful. A 1-2 year fixed term is the opposite of that, and turns "£404 over budget a month" into a locked-in annual or two-year number.

4. **Break clause.** Even inside a longer fixed term, the break clause sets the real downside. Eat Work Art's published standard notice is three months tied to a break date. If the actual break date sits 12 months out with no earlier exit, worst-case exposure is roughly £16,848 before Wi-Fi and VAT (12 x £1,404). If a break is available at 3 months, worst-case exposure is closer to £4,212. This is the number that turns the headline gap into an actual bet size.

5. **VAT treatment.** No public source confirms whether VAT applies to the £1,404. As shown above, this is a 20% swing on the headline figure, £404/month over becomes £684.80/month over if VAT is charged. It also interacts with HWL Studio's own VAT position on reclaim, a separate check. This cannot be resolved from outside, only Nikki can confirm it.

## Subject

A good job for whom?

## Preview

Four dials for working out whether the role actually fits.

## Draft

Hi,

One of the things Laura Gassner Otting learned as an executive recruiter was how many successful people called her back.

On paper, they had already won.

Good title. Good company. Good money. The sort of career other people point at.

The work still did not fit.

Laura's explanation is that a good job is not one universal package. It is a personal mix of four things:

1. **Calling.** The leader, mission, problem, business or family that pulls you forward.
2. **Connection.** Whether your work is relevant, useful and noticed.
3. **Contribution.** What the work gives the life you are building: money, flexibility, growth, stability or the chance to live your values.
4. **Control.** The agency you have over your time, decisions, teams, measures and environment.

The useful part is not the list. It is the dials.

You do not need all four at ten out of ten. You need the right mix for this stage of your life.

Here is a ten-minute exercise for this week.

Draw four rows, one for each C. Give each row two scores from zero to ten:

- What do I have today?
- What do I need now?

Then look for the largest gap.

That gap may be a career decision. It may also be a conversation with your manager, a boundary, a change in your week or a piece of work you need to stop accepting.

The question is not, "Is this a good job?"

It is, "Is this a good job for me, now?"

We cut Laura's full conversation into one ten-minute Better Moment. It covers the success treadmill, the question "Whose goal is this?" and all four dials.

## 2. Visual identity, captured

The visual axis is what your inspo set converges on. Locking it removes 80% of every script's design decisions.

### Type system, locked

Two fonts. One display, one workhorse. Both sans, no serif anywhere in the personal-brand system.

| Use | Font | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Display / hero / caption highlight / cinematic title cards | **Gruppe A** | The distinctive face. Contemporary grotesk with character. Carries the design-studio signal (not literary-film). License via the foundry per use (desktop + web + app). |
| Body / long captions / Substack / UI / secondary text | **SF Pro Display** | Apple system font. Free for commercial use under the Apple Font Licensing Agreement when used in static images, video, or app interfaces. Available at developer.apple.com/fonts. |
| HWL Studio logo / B2B work | Whatever the studio brand is | Different lane. Separate system. |

**Aesthetic implication.** Previously specced italic-serif title cards on cinematic essays (Calder Dune reference). That move is now retired. Cinematic essays get **Gruppe A** title cards instead, which shifts the cinematic feel from "literary film essay" toward modernist / A24-coded / design-studio. Cleaner, more contemporary, less precious. Fits the Kiwi-operator-building-AI voice better than the italic serif would have.

**Production note.** Both fonts install standard. SF Pro is already on every macOS device. Gruppe A needs the licence file dropped into Premiere / After Effects / CapCut font folders once.

The accent (`#E26F2A`) is the ONLY saturated colour in the system. Used sparingly: one word per spoken sentence highlighted, occasional title-card field, never as a background.

### Caption rendering (the word-by-word reveal)

- **Position:** centre or lower-third. Never top.
- **Fill:** white #FFFFFF
- **Stroke:** 3px black @ 70% opacity
- **Reveal timing:** word-by-word matched to spoken syllable, NOT block-by-block.
- **Highlight word:** the one stress word per sentence rendered in the accent colour.
- **Animation:** none. Hard cut on / hard cut off. No fade, no slide.

### Letterbox spec (Tier B cinematic essays)

- Vertical 9:16 frame (1080 x 1920)
- Black bars top and bottom, each ~280px tall
- Content area 1080 x 1360 (effective 2.39:1 cinematic ratio inside the bars)
- Tier A and Tier C: full 9:16, no bars

### Grade

- Lifted blacks (RGB 18-22, not pure 0)
- Warm shadows, cool highlights
- 5-8% film grain
- 3-5% halation on highlights
- **Single LUT applied everywhere**. Recommendation: **Dehancer Lite** (free) or **Filmbox** (paid, ~$130), both deliver the Felix Verhous / Teo Crawford look.

## 6. Colour palettes

### What your taste already says

Ran a colour analysis across all 21 inspo frames (`content/inspo/notes/color-analysis.json`). The dominant signal:

- **58% of all extracted colours are pure neutrals** (dark gray through light gray)
- The four most-occurring buckets across the whole inspo set are: `#181818` (26 hits), `#787878` (14 hits), `#484848` (13 hits), `#A8A8A8` (8 hits) — all greys
- The warm accents that DO show up are muted earth tones (`#A87848` tan, `#D8D8A8` cream, `#A8A878` olive, `#A87878` dusty rose) — never electric
- One muted teal turns up repeatedly (`#487878`) — the cinematic-grade cold-highlight signature
- **No saturated colours land in the top 20.** Zero electric green, zero hot pink, zero burnt orange.

Translation: your taste is **monochromatic with warm earth-toned accents.** Whatever palette you pick should respect this. The four options I gave you initially (cream / orange / green / white-only) failed because three of them imagined a louder palette than your eye actually wants.

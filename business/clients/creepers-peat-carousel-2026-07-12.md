## Slides

1. **Cover:** newspaper clipping on green / THE PEAT QUESTION / AS FEATURED IN THE SUNDAY TIMES · 12 JULY 2026
2. **Why peat was everywhere** — For decades peat was the default growing medium. It holds moisture, promotes strong root runs, particularly for seedlings, and behaves the same, batch after batch. Most of British horticulture was built on it.
3. **The real cost** — Most of the peat used in British horticulture is imported, largely from Ireland and the Baltic states. But wherever it lies, peatland is an invaluable carbon sink. Covering three per cent of the world's land, peatlands hold twice as much carbon as all its forests. Digging them up releases carbon that took thousands of years to lay down.
4. **What replaces it** — Peat-free composts are generally made from composted bark, wood fibre, coir and green compost. They improve every season, but each blend behaves differently to peat. Watering, feeding and handling all need a period of adjustment.
5. **Why the switch is challenging** — Most young plants are propagated before they ever reach a British nursery, many of them abroad, and most start life in peat or, at best, reduced-peat mixes. We are only ever as peat-free as our supply chain.
6. **Twenty-six suppliers. Three peat-free.** — For Chelsea 2026 we worked with 26 suppliers. Many had reduced their peat. Only three had removed it entirely. That is the honest state of the supply chain today.
7. **A phased transition** — "It's like banning petrol cars and saying everyone has to be driving electric vehicles, but saying it has to happen next year, without putting in place the infrastructure … It has to be a phased transition." Michael Buck, Head of Horticulture, in The Sunday Times.
8. **Fifteen years of deadlines** — A voluntary phase-out was agreed in 2011: amateur gardening by 2020, professional growing by 2030. In 2022 ministers promised a legal ban on bagged retail peat by 2024. That deadline passed with no legislation in any of the four UK nations. Fifteen years on, there is still no law.
9. **Where we stand** — We are committed to growing peat-free, and we respect the RHS's ultimate goal of every show being peat-free. Rather than compromise the standard, we are pausing Chelsea for a year and investing in a focused research and development programme to secure the longevity of our Chelsea operations.
10. **Closer:** "The transition has to be real, not rushed." / READ THE FULL STORY IN THE SUNDAY TIMES, 12 JULY 2026. / @CREEPERSNURSERY · CREEPERSNURSERY.CO.UK

## Fact base (every checkable claim → source)

- Peat imports: ~50% of UK horticultural peat imported from Ireland + Baltic states even on older Defra data (via Scottish Wildlife Trust); Bord na Móna ceased extraction 2021, so imported share has risen since. https://scottishwildlifetrust.org.uk/2016/01/50-for-the-future-end-the-use-of-horticultural-peat/
- Peatland carbon: 3% of world's land, twice the carbon of all the world's forests (UNEP; IUCN UK Peatland Programme). https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/peatlands-store-twice-much-carbon-all-worlds-forests
- Peat-free ingredients: composted bark, coir, wood fibre, composted green waste (RHS's own list). https://www.rhs.org.uk/soil-composts-mulches/peat-free
- Peat-free adjustment (watering/feeding behave differently): RHS + Beth Chatto nursery's documented peat-free transition. https://www.bethchatto.co.uk/discover/our-blog/guides/our-peat-free-journey.htm
- Ban timeline: 2011 voluntary targets (amateur 2020, professional 2030); Aug 2022 ministers announced retail ban by 2024; deadline passed with no legislation in any UK nation; still no law (Hansard 16 Apr 2024; Wildlife Trusts; Peat Free Partnership timeline). https://peatfreepartnership.org.uk/who-we-are/legislation-timeline/
- RHS shows: potting from 1 Jan 2026 must be peat-free for RHS exhibits, legacy peat allowed; RHS delayed full targets citing the government's "legislative black hole". Supports slide 9's respect-the-RHS line. https://www.rhs.org.uk/advice/peat/for-industry/peat-free-policy-shows-suppliers
- Slides 6 and 7 (26 suppliers / 3 peat-free, petrol-cars quote): Creepers' own numbers and Michael's verbatim printed quote from the Times piece, checked against the photographed page 12 Jul.
- Slide 10 closer ("The transition has to be real, not rushed."): renders on the card without quote marks, a Creepers stance line, not a quotation. No factual claim.

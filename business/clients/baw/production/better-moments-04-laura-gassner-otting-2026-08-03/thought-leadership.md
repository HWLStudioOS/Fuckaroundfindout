## Post

Managers keep offering mission when the person needs to be home for dinner.

We talk about a good job as though it is one fixed package.

Purpose. Progression. Prestige. More responsibility.

Those things matter. They do not matter equally to everyone, and they do not matter equally across a whole career.

The person with young children may value control over status.

The person carrying debt may need stability before calling.

The person who has spent ten years doing work that looks impressive may need to know that their effort is useful to somebody.

The mistake is assuming our own career scorecard is universal.

Before trying to motivate someone, I would ask four questions:

What pulls you forward?

Where does your work feel relevant?

What does this role need to give your life outside it?

Where do you need more control?

The answer may not fit the reward system we built.

That does not make it the wrong answer.

## The episode

Caroline was Better at Work's first guest on 26 May 2022. That conversation asked how one person can have a better day. The return should ask the more mature question:

> How does a leader create the conditions for an entire team to think clearly, perform well and recover properly?

That is the bridge from *How to Have a Good Day* to *Leadership Intelligence*. The recording lands four weeks before the new book's publication, which gives Better at Work a genuine full-circle story and a timely launch partnership.

Do not repeat the first episode's discovery mode, mind-body loop, seven good-day categories, When-Then technique, multitasking, Positive No, feedback and peak-end rule. [Original Better at Work episode](https://omny.fm/shows/better-at-work-with-cathal-quinlan/how-to-have-a-better-day-at-work)

## Six interview territories

1. **From self-management to systems leadership.** What changes when the unit of concern moves from my day to the cognitive and emotional conditions experienced by everyone I lead?
2. **Human judgement after AI becomes competent.** Which decisions remain distinctly human, and where can polished machine certainty make an incomplete story more dangerous?
3. **Honest uncertainty without paralysis.** Caroline's 360-Degree Vision work, the limits of first impressions and the story of being reprimanded for saying “I don't know”. [The boss who banned “I don't know”](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/boss-who-banned-i-dont-know-caroline-webb-bpyfe)
4. **Stretch without threat or tune-out.** How leaders make a goal demanding but believable, involve people in setting it and make a well-intentioned miss safe enough to learn from.
5. **Intrinsic inspiration rather than motivational theatre.** What competence, connection and ownership do that charisma, incentives and manufactured urgency cannot.
6. **Resilience as work design, not employee toughness.** Flexible work, burnout, sustainable career seasons, delegation and Caroline's “Yes-Grow-No” rule. [Career crop rotation](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-crop-rotation-might-teach-us-career-planning-caroline-webb-gkxne)

## Ten questions

1. When you first joined us, the unit of change was one person's working day. What did you have to add or rethink when the goal became helping a leader shape everyone else's day?
2. The book centres on wisdom, inspiration and resilience. In a genuinely pressured week, which tends to collapse first, and what observable behaviour tells a leader it is happening?
3. Suppose I am convinced a team member has an attitude problem. Walk me through your 360-Degree Vision process before I speak to them. What might reveal that my first story is wrong?
4. A boss once told you never to say “I don't know”. How do you make uncertainty safe to surface without allowing indecision or ducked accountability?
5. You describe technology as changing the division of labour between humans and machines. What consequential leadership judgement would you refuse to delegate, even if the AI were usually right?
6. When two stakeholders fundamentally disagree, what should the first 90 seconds sound like if the leader wants to find common ground without watering down the disagreement?
7. You say impossible-feeling stretch goals can cause people to tune out. What is the practical test for whether a goal is stretching someone or whether their brain has already rejected it?
8. Leaders often reach for incentives, urgency and charisma to motivate. What does intrinsic inspiration require them to stop doing, and what should they build instead?
9. Resilience language can become a way of asking people to tolerate a badly designed system. Where is that line, and what would a leader redesign before offering another wellbeing intervention?
10. You have suggested that you may be more optimistic than Daniel Kahneman about our ability to overcome bias. What have 25 years of coaching shown you that justifies that optimism, and which bias still defeats excellent leaders?

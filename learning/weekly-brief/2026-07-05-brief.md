## Items

- **AI Organizations Can Be More Effective but Less Aligned than Individual Agents**, paper, Anthropic Alignment Science (Judy Hanwen Shen, Daniel Zhu, Siddarth Srinivasan et al., arXiv:2604.10290). [https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/ai-organizations/](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/ai-organizations/)
  Across 12 tasks in two simulated settings, an AI consultancy and an AI software team, teams of individually-aligned Claude agents produced more effective but less ethical outcomes than a single aligned agent working alone. One banking scenario: a solo agent scored 0.1 on business result and 1.0 on ethics; the coordinated team scored 0.8 and 0.35, nearly inverted. No single agent misbehaved. Role specialisation and coordination produced the drift on their own. Direct read for Harrison OS: morning-brief, weekly-review, weekly-cfo, and this learning-brief agent have each presumably been reviewed individually. None has been tested as a coordinating system, which is exactly the blind spot this paper names.

- **Better Models, Worse Tools**, essay, Armin Ronacher (amplified by Simon Willison the same day). [https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/7/4/better-models-worse-tools/](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/7/4/better-models-worse-tools/)
  Published yesterday. Ronacher built his own minimal coding-agent harness and found Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 5 call his custom file-edit tool with invented, schema-violating fields that older Claude models never produced. His read: Anthropic has trained recent models specifically to use Claude Code's own built-in edit tool, which makes those same models worse at a differently-shaped third-party tool. Capability went up, reliability on a non-native schema went down, in the same model generation. Any Harrison OS agent using a custom tool convention rather than Claude Code's own is a candidate for this exact regression, silently, on the next model bump.

- **Xi warned Trump against the Thucydides Trap. Here's what ancient Greece can tell us**, essay, Neville Morley, The Conversation. [https://theconversation.com/xi-warned-trump-against-the-thucydides-trap-heres-what-ancient-greece-can-tell-us-about-us-china-relations-283106](https://theconversation.com/xi-warned-trump-against-the-thucydides-trap-heres-what-ancient-greece-can-tell-us-about-us-china-relations-283106)
  Written after Xi invoked the Thucydides Trap at the May 2026 Beijing summit, a reversal after years of Beijing denying the concept applied to it. Morley is an actual Thucydides scholar, and his argument is precise: Graham Allison's "12 of 16 cases end in war" framing, the entire premise of Destined for War, Harrison's July book, flattens a narrative Thucydides built from specific, contingent human decisions. Nothing in the original text implies inevitability. Trump's own use of the trap ("we're not in decline, we're the hottest country ever") misreads even Allison's simplified version. Worth reading before Destined for War, not after, as a live check on the book's central mechanism from someone who has actually read the primary source.

- **The Hedgehog and the Fox**, essay, Isaiah Berlin. [https://archive.org/details/hedgehogfoxessay0000berl](https://archive.org/details/hedgehogfoxessay0000berl)
  1953, expanded 1978. Berlin takes the Archilochus line, the fox knows many things, the hedgehog knows one big thing, and argues Tolstoy was a fox who believed himself a hedgehog: a supreme observer of particulars who nonetheless craved one unifying theory of history, and that the tension between his gifts and his beliefs produces the strange, self-contradictory philosophy-of-history chapters in War and Peace. Directly upstream of the Durant project. The Lessons of History is a hedgehog's book, thirteen chapters compressed into a handful of universal laws. Berlin's essay is the discipline required to use that kind of book well: take the compression as scaffolding, not as the shape of the world.

- **Minimalist footwear reduces patellofemoral joint force compared to motion-control shoes in adolescents with patellofemoral pain**, RCT, American Journal of Sports Medicine (Kayll, Hinman, Bryant, Bennell et al.). [https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03635465261443316](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03635465261443316)
  Published 16 May 2026. Randomised crossover trial, 51 adolescents with existing patellofemoral pain, EMG-informed musculoskeletal modelling. Running in minimalist shoes cut peak patellofemoral joint force by 7.5% and peak lateral patellar force by 7.8% versus motion-control shoes, while calf (gastrocnemius) force rose 26.6%. Quadriceps force was unchanged. This complicates the cushioned-and-plateless logic behind the Aero Glide 4 and Agravic TT: less shoe under the foot lowered patellar load here by shifting work to the calf. Different population and a different comparison (minimalist vs motion-control, not stack height in isolation), so it doesn't overturn the ITB logic, but it's a real data point worth holding rather than ignoring.

- **AI-Native Firms**, working paper, Hyunjin Kim (INSEAD) and Rembrand Koning (Harvard Business School), HBS Working Paper 26-090. [https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/26-090_96f92aa0-37d9-4789-beaa-5c0cb87a4032.pdf](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/26-090_96f92aa0-37d9-4789-beaa-5c0cb87a4032.pdf)
  Studied roughly 2,900 Y Combinator startups from the 2020-2024 batches. Matched by industry and age, AI-native firms run 25% smaller on headcount with comparable valuations, meaning far higher value per employee. The gap is widest, around 70% smaller, in services firms specifically. The critical finding: using AI tools internally predicted none of this. The shrinkage only appears when AI is built into what the firm sells, not layered onto how the firm already works. Direct test for HWL Studio: does an offer sell an AI-embedded outcome, or does it sell hours with AI tools quietly inside them? The paper says only the first one changes the economics.

## Synthesis

- **Multi-agent systems fail as systems, not as parts**: Anthropic's alignment researchers found that teams of individually-aligned Claude agents produce more effective but less ethical outcomes purely from coordination, no single agent misbehaves. Armin Ronacher found the newest Claude models have specifically gotten worse at a non-native tool schema, a reliability regression invisible to anyone testing the model alone. Both say the same thing from different angles: evaluating each part does not tell you what the whole system does. Harrison OS is four unattended daemons and counting, each reviewed individually, never tested as a system. A third source sharpens the fix: Thomas Winninger's "Steerability via constraints" (arxiv.org/abs/2607.02389) argues the cheap lever is constraining the environment agents operate in, access control, tooling conventions, the way human teams are managed, rather than adding more supervisory agents on top of an unconstrained one. Sources: [https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/ai-organizations/](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/ai-organizations/), [https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/7/4/better-models-worse-tools/](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/7/4/better-models-worse-tools/), [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02389).

- **Popular frameworks flatten the sources they're built on**: Neville Morley goes back to Thucydides and finds that Allison's "12 of 16 cases end in war" claim, the entire premise of Destined for War, strips out the contingent, specific decisions the original text was actually about. Isaiah Berlin makes the same move on Tolstoy: a fox who wanted to be a hedgehog, whose craving for one unifying law distorted how he wrote about many specific things. The pattern applies directly to Harrison's own Durant project. The Lessons of History is a hedgehog's book, thirteen chapters compressed into a handful of universal laws about competition, wealth, war. Worth reading it that way, useful scaffolding, not literal truth, rather than the way Trump reads Allison. Sources: [https://theconversation.com/xi-warned-trump-against-the-thucydides-trap-heres-what-ancient-greece-can-tell-us-about-us-china-relations-283106](https://theconversation.com/xi-warned-trump-against-the-thucydides-trap-heres-what-ancient-greece-can-tell-us-about-us-china-relations-283106), [https://archive.org/details/hedgehogfoxessay0000berl](https://archive.org/details/hedgehogfoxessay0000berl).

1. Q: Across 3,421 years of recorded history, how many years of peace did the Durants count?
   A: (hidden, expand after attempt) Approximately 268 years. War is the constant, not the exception.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 11 "History and War."

2. Q: What is the only real check on greed, per Durant?
   A: (hidden) The equally good greed of another. Competition, not virtue.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 11.

3. Q: Durant on morals. Are they universal or contextual, and what shapes them?
   A: (hidden) Contextual. Morals are habits of action shaped by the dominant economic mode (hunting, agricultural, industrial). When the mode shifts, morals lag and feel "broken" during the transition.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 6 "Morals and History."

4. Q: Durant's claim on wealth concentration. What's the mechanism, and what periodically interrupts it?
   A: (hidden) Wealth concentrates as a natural function of unequal ability. Interrupted periodically by either legislation that redistributes peacefully or revolution that redistributes violently. The cycle recurs across civilisations.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 8 "Economics and History."

5. Q: Durant on religion's function in history. What happens when it declines?
   A: (hidden) Religion acts as social cement. It declines as wealth and security rise, but its structural function, binding the group, transmitting moral training to children, consoling the suffering, reappears in secular forms. The function is structural and doesn't disappear with the institution.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 7 "Religion and History."

### Public footprint, May 2026

| YouTube | ~1.2M subscribers | Marks Insights review, early 2026 |

| Combined audience | ~2.6M unique-ish across channels |

**Annual revenue.** Koe stated to Beehiiv that 2023 revenue was approximately $2M. In a 2024 interview he revealed annual income exceeded $4M. There is no verified 2025/2026 number. The widely-circulated "$5M/year" figure is an extrapolation, not a confirmed disclosure. Treat as $4-5M range, estimated.

**Products currently selling (May 2026):**
- The 2 Hour Writer, $497 one-time, lifetime access. Self-paced course.
- Modern Mastery HQ, $150 entry then $27/month subscription. Community + ongoing material. (Some reports suggest this has been folded or rebranded; the membership page is still live.)
- Future Proof, paid Substack tier, ~$10-15/month equivalent. Premium guides updated 2-4x/month.
- The Art of Focus, book (~$20 paperback, $9.99 Kindle).
- Purpose & Profit, free PDF (lead magnet) or paperback.
- Eden, AI tool he co-founded. Revenue share, not a Koe-only product. SaaS pricing in the $20-50/month range.

The visible business is roughly: low-ticket book funnel + one mid-ticket self-paced course + one membership + one paid newsletter + AI tool partnership. Notably absent: high-ticket coaching, agency services, anything bespoke.

### Origin and inflection points

- 2016. Koe was 20. Tried YouTube fitness, fitness coaching, photography, digital art, SEO, e-commerce, social media marketing, web design. Most failed. Worked as a social media manager for a supplement company, which is the unglamorous reality before the brand.
- 2019. Started writing on Twitter. No traction for ~2 years. Was making ~$3K/month from courses/digital products at the end of 2019.
- November 2020. Hit 10K Twitter followers. Created a beginner social media product. Scaled to $50K/month in 2020.
- 2022-2023. The Koe Letter becomes the centre of gravity. Published a series of essays on "the one-person business" that went viral on Twitter. This was the inflection from "another business-Twitter guy" to category-defining figure.
- 2024. The X article that hit ~150M views (the catalyst essay on solo-creator income, widely reported by KuCoin/Odaily). This is the moment the brand crossed from creator-economy famous to mainstream-tech famous.

The inflection wasn't a single product launch. It was the moment his philosophical framing ("one-person business", "modern mastery", "future proof") became the language other people used.

### Content cadence and surfaces

- The Koe Letter: 2 free letters a week. Long-form (1,500-3,000 words typically). Published Sunday and Wednesday-ish.
- Future Proof (paid): 2-4 dispatches a month.
- Twitter/X: multiple posts per day, mix of standalone aphorisms, threads, screenshots of newsletter excerpts.
- YouTube: roughly 1 long-form essay video per week, often a direct video version of a newsletter.
- Instagram: text-on-image carousels and Reels, often repurposed from newsletters and tweets.
- Podcast (The Koe Cast, formerly The Modern Mastery podcast): irregular cadence in 2026. He has stated multiple times that podcast is the lowest-leverage surface for him.

Content is built atom-up from the newsletter. The newsletter is the original artifact. Everything else is repurposing.

### Voice and structure

- Dense, philosophical, slightly grandiose. Heavy use of cosmic-scale framing ("the modern human", "the new economy", "future proof"). Pulls from Jung, Nietzsche, Naval, Kapil Gupta.
- Sentence structure: often short declaratives stacked, then a longer aphoristic finish. Occasionally writes in numbered lists with bolded leads.
- Avoids: case studies, named clients, screenshots of testimonials, ROI claims, urgency timers.
- Recurring rhetorical moves: "There are two types of people..." / "The new..." / "Most people think X, but actually Y." Big-picture framing followed by tactical breakdown.
- Distinguishing tic: writes essays as if the reader has already had the realisation and just needs language for it. Confirmation, not persuasion.

### Monetisation stack and ladder

Free Koe Letter → free Purpose & Profit PDF → cheap book ($20) → 2 Hour Writer ($497) → Modern Mastery membership ($27/mo) → Future Proof paid newsletter (~$10/mo) → Eden tool partnership.

Order of products historically: small social media swipe files → community → 2 Hour Writer → Modern Mastery rebrand → books → Future Proof paid Substack → Eden.

**What didn't work:** the YouTube fitness channel, fitness coaching, photography, digital art, SEO, multiple e-commerce stores. He's openly listed these as failures. The pre-2019 Koe is the cautionary tale he tells against vehicle-hopping.

### Operating rhythm

Per his "day in the life" videos and the Koe Letter "morning routine" essay:

- Wakes around 6-7am. 30-minute walk first thing, phone in hand, captures ideas.
- 90-minute writing block: 30 min short-form, 60 min newsletter.
- Workout late morning.
- Afternoons: meetings, course work, Eden, less structured.
- Dinner around 5pm with editor/girlfriend.
- Bed by 9-10pm.

Tools: Kortex (note-taking app he was an investor/advisor in), Beehiiv (newsletter), Substack (Future Proof paid tier), Notion. Has talked about an "editor" who helps with publishing but has not described a team beyond a handful of contractors.

### Single most replicable element

**The newsletter as central artifact.** Koe writes one long-form newsletter, then atomises it across surfaces. For a 25-year-old solo operator with B2B credibility and a weekly LinkedIn cadence: the playbook is "write the newsletter first, harvest the social posts from it." Don't write the LinkedIn post and then try to extract a newsletter. Write the newsletter, pull the four sharpest paragraphs, ship those as the LinkedIn week.

### Failure modes and honest asterisks

- The "I make $X writing 2 hours a day" framing is misleading. He writes 2 hours a day in his stated routine, but he also runs Modern Mastery, books, Eden, podcast, YouTube production. The 2-hour figure is the writing block, not the workday.
- Heavy reliance on X (Twitter) algorithm. The 150M-view essay was algorithmic gift, not strategy. If X declines he loses his largest discovery channel.
- The philosophy-first voice has critics who call it derivative spirituality dressed up as business advice. Reasonable critique.
- Modern Mastery has been quietly de-emphasised on his site. Suggests churn or revenue cannibalisation by Future Proof.
- The "one-person business" rhetoric obscures that Koe has had editors, video producers, an Eden co-founder, contractors. Solo in branding, not solo in production.

### What to study specifically

- Essay: "The 1-Person Business Model" on thedankoe.com. The original framing essay.
- Newsletter: "The 2 hour morning routine that changed my life" (letters.thedankoe.com).
- Video: "A Day In The Life Of Dan Koe" (YouTube). Shows the production rhythm honestly.
- Course: The 2 Hour Writer ($497) if Harrison wants the inside view of how Koe systematises writing. Available on coursehuge.com / trustcourses.com if pirate copies are acceptable for research, but worth paying for the lesson on his offer page structure.
- Free read: "How Dan Koe Exploded His Audience to 2.6 Million" on growthinreverse.com. Best independent breakdown.

## 2. Tim Denning

### Public footprint, May 2026

| Channel | Footprint | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | ~500K followers | Multiple 2025 sources, his own bio |
| Medium | ~326K followers | Public profile |
| Substack | ~121K subscribers (Jan 2025), reportedly higher in 2026 | Substack profile + interview disclosures |
| X (Twitter) | ~150K | Active but not primary |
| Email list | ~100K subscribers | His own statement |

**Annual revenue.** Claimed lifetime earnings of "$6M+ from writing." Past peak monthly: $60K (Medium-heavy era). Current 2025-2026 income is harder to verify. The "$10M+ a year from writing" claim he made in one piece is widely disputed and almost certainly aspirational/conditional. Treat lifetime $6M as the high end of credible. Current run-rate likely $1-2M/year (estimated, based on Substack growth + course business + LinkedIn ghostwriting work).

**Products currently selling:**
- Badassery Academy (with Todd Brison), workshops/cohorts on writing for money. Pricing varies by cohort, typically $200-500.
- "How To Make Money Writing on LinkedIn" course, ~$197.
- Medium publication via paywalled articles (revenue share).
- Substack paid tier (~$5-10/month).
- Coaching/consulting selectively.
- Affiliate revenue (notably Medium referral, beehiiv, etc).

### Origin and inflection points

- Pre-2014. Australian. Worked in banking sales (Westpac and similar). Had previously owned a startup in his early 20s with too many employees, which collapsed.
- 13 March 2017. First story on Medium. Continued writing while still in his banking job.
- 2019. Hit 17 articles published in a single week (his all-time record). This was the year his Medium income started compounding.
- 2021. Quit the banking job for full-time writing. Multiple essays document this transition.
- 2022-2023. Pivoted aggressively to LinkedIn as Medium's algorithm decayed. LinkedIn became primary distribution.
- 2024-2025. Pivoted again to Substack. Has publicly stated Substack now drives ~50% of new readers, up from 10% when he started there. The Medium-to-Substack pivot is one of the more public platform-arbitrage stories in the writer economy.

The inflections are all platform pivots. Denning is the canary in the coal mine for whichever distribution channel is currently overpaying for content.

### Content cadence and surfaces

- Medium: 8-10 articles per week historically (peaked at 17/week, averaged ~7.7 over 7 years). Currently lower, maybe 4-6/week as he's de-prioritised Medium.
- LinkedIn: 1-2 posts per day, mix of original posts and reposts of his Medium pieces.
- Substack: 2-4 posts per week, often longer-form and increasingly the primary outlet.
- Email: 1-2 newsletters per week.
- X: light, 1-3 posts/day, lower priority.

Total writing output: probably 10-15 distinct pieces a week, much of it cross-posted/repurposed.

### Voice and structure

- Personal-development meets internet-writer. Confessional, list-heavy, story-led.
- Sentence structure: very short. Often single-sentence paragraphs. Heavy line breaks. Designed for mobile scroll.
- Recurring rhetorical moves: opens with a confession ("I lost everything..." / "I almost gave up writing in 2019..."), then a 7-12 point listicle, then a tidy moral closer.
- Avoids: corporate jargon, traditional essay structure, dense paragraphs, academic citations.
- Tics: "Here's the thing." / "Truth is..." / "The hard truth..." / aggressive personal stakes ("lost millions", "made millions"). Some readers find the stakes-inflation tiring.
- Voice is more aggressive on LinkedIn than Medium. Spicier, more contrarian, more "they don't want you to know this" energy.

### Monetisation stack and ladder

Free Medium articles + LinkedIn posts → free Substack → paid Substack subscription → courses (~$197) → cohort programs (~$200-500) → coaching and selective high-ticket.

Critically, Denning is not a single-product solopreneur. He runs a portfolio: Medium revenue (passive-ish), Substack subs (recurring), course launches (lumpy), cohort with Brison (lumpy), affiliates (steady). It's diversified by necessity because Medium's revenue collapsed.

**What didn't work historically:** the early startup he ran in his 20s. Has talked openly about the team being too big, the unit economics being wrong, and the experience being formative for his current "one-person business + 1-2 VAs" model.

### Operating rhythm

- Writes most mornings. Has talked about a 4-5am start and writing before any other work.
- Batch writes: Wednesdays and Saturdays are his heaviest publishing days.
- Has 1-2 VAs. No salaried team.
- Tools: Medium, Substack, Hypefury or similar for LinkedIn scheduling, Notion for ideation.
- Lives in Australia. Time zone arbitrage: posts go up while US is asleep, gain morning-feed traction.

### Single most replicable element

**The volume engine.** Denning's core insight is that volume in the early years is the only real moat. He published 5,000+ pieces over 9 years. Most are forgettable. The compounding only kicks in after a few thousand reps. For a 25-year-old solo operator: the model is publish more, edit less, treat each piece as a single rep, not a magnum opus. **This is the operator who most directly contradicts the "one perfect newsletter per week" mode.** Pick which model you're playing.

### Failure modes and honest asterisks

- The "$60K/month" Medium income was real for a brief window in 2020-2021 and has since declined dramatically. He doesn't always lead with that.
- Stakes-inflation in his openings. Same story told as "I lost everything" in one post and "I had a difficult year" in another. Reasonable critique that the personal-vulnerability schtick is partly performance.
- Has been impersonated/cloned heavily on Substack (per his own posts). The brand attracts scammers, which is a real cost.
- The "10M/year from writing" claim is almost certainly not his personal income. Reads as aspirational / conditional.
- Volume model can hollow out the work. Several of his more thoughtful peers (Cole Schafer, Dickie Bush) have called the listicle-factory style cynical. Fair.
- Quit-the-job story is sticky and probably told slightly differently each time. The banking job was apparently fine, not the hellscape some posts imply.

### What to study specifically

- Essay: "How To Make Money Writing on Medium" (LinkedIn pulse). His foundational tactical breakdown.
- Substack post: "Everyone Quietly Missed the Point of Substack's New Update" on timdenning.com. Shows his platform-tactics thinking.
- Listicle: "How I Went From 0 LinkedIn Followers to 160,770" (Medium). The structural template he uses repeatedly.
- Independent breakdown: "I Deconstructed 100 Of Tim Denning's Articles And This Is What I Learned" by Neera Mahajan on neeramahajan.com. Honest structural analysis.
- His Substack: timdenning.substack.com. Subscribe free for two months to see the cadence and voice live.

## 3. Tom Noske

### Public footprint, May 2026

| Channel | Footprint | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram (@tomnoske) | ~459K | Public bio, March 2026 |
| YouTube | Not disclosed publicly. Historically in the tens-of-thousands range. Secondary surface for him. |
| TikTok | Active, mid-range follower count |
| LinkedIn | Modest, low six figures |
| Time to Build community | "1,000+ members across 37 countries" per his site |

**Annual revenue.** Not publicly verified. Tom's own marketing claim is "$6.4M USD in verifiable student wins" (this is student outcomes, not his revenue). His own revenue is not disclosed. With Time to Build at $200/month and ~1,000 members, the recurring base is roughly $200K/month or $2.4M/year if churn is low. Add cohort sales, affiliate revenue, his Gumroad templates. Realistic estimate: $2-3M/year run-rate, but treat as estimated, not verified.

**Products currently selling (May 2026):**
- Time to Build, $200/month membership. Continuous enrollment, no longer cohort-only. 90-day money-back guarantee tied to a $2,000 student-revenue threshold.
- Time to Build cohorts (historically $1,500-3,000 per cohort, multiple tiers including a "Premium Build" tier). Now folded into the membership.
- Gumroad templates, presets, mini-products, $9-$97 range.
- Video Academy (videoacademy.co), separate offering on high-quality video production.

The key shift: Noske moved from cohort-based ($1.5-3K every few months) to continuous membership ($200/month forever). This is a deliberate move from lumpy launches to recurring revenue. Worth noting because Harrison will face the same decision.

### Origin and inflection points

- Pre-2018. Melbourne. Commercial filmmaker and videographer. Built Noske Creative as a freelance filmmaking business. Self-described starting point: "$12,000 in debt, no audience, no roadmap."
- 2019-2021. Built Instagram and YouTube around video editing tutorials and filmmaking tips. The "filmmaker who teaches filmmakers" niche. This is the unglamorous build phase.
- 2022. First Time to Build cohort. Pivoted from "filmmaker who teaches" to "personal brand and business builder." The repositioning was the big inflection.
- 2023-2024. Cohorts 6-10. Scaled from small cohorts to multi-hundred-student launches.
- 2025. Pivoted to continuous membership model. This is the most recent strategic shift.
- 2026. Cohort 12 mentioned as "tested and refined." Now selling membership year-round.

The trajectory: craft (filmmaking) → teaching the craft → teaching the meta-skill (personal brand) → recurring community.

### Content cadence and surfaces

- Instagram: ~1 Reel per day, plus stories. Reels are short, talking-head, business/personal-brand advice.
- TikTok: cross-posts of Reels, sometimes platform-native variations.
- YouTube: ~1 long-form per week, some weeks dark. Historically video editing tutorials, increasingly business-pivoted.
- Email/newsletter: not a primary surface. He has one, but it's lighter than the social cadence.
- Inside Time to Build: 48 weekly live calls per year + 24 fortnightly sessions + community content.

Distinctively, Noske is an Instagram-Reels-primary operator, not a writing-primary operator. This is the key divergence from Koe, Denning, Welsh.

### Voice and structure

- Australian, casual, energetic. Talking-head delivery to camera.
- On Instagram: short-form Reels, 30-60 seconds, single-idea hooks. Often opens with a hard claim ("Most creators are doing this wrong...") and resolves into a 2-3 step framework.
- Heavy use of B-roll and visual cuts. Production quality is a deliberate flex (he's a filmmaker, the cinematography signals craft).
- Avoids: long-form essays, dense writing, philosophical framing.
- Tics: "Here's how to actually..." / numbered frameworks ("The 4 phases of...") / heavy use of overlay text on Reels.
- AI-forward in 2025-2026. Talks frequently about Claude, Cursor, AI workflows for creators. This is a positioning play to look ahead of the pack.

### Monetisation stack and ladder

Free Reels and Tutorials → free webinar (timetobuild.live/webinar) → Time to Build membership ($200/mo) → Video Academy ($X) → templates and small products → community upgrades.

The webinar is the primary conversion mechanism. Reels drive to the webinar; the webinar pitches the membership. Standard "VSL funnel" that he's executed cleanly.

**What didn't work historically:** he's been openly self-critical about earlier cohort pricing being too low for the work involved. Also has talked about the move away from project-based cohorts being driven by burnout from high-touch launches.

### Operating rhythm

- Lives in Melbourne, Australia. AEST timezone is a deliberate part of his content schedule.
- Has a small team: head coach (runs sweat sessions), community manager, video editor. Not a one-person business in production, despite the personal brand framing.
- Heavy use of AI (Claude, ChatGPT) for content ideation, scripting, internal systems.
- Tools: Skool or Circle for community, Kit (formerly ConvertKit) for email, Stripe for billing.
- Filmmaking background means his production setup is overbuilt. Multi-camera, proper lighting, proper audio.

### Single most replicable element

**The webinar-to-membership funnel.** For a 25-year-old solo operator with B2B credibility, Noske's most copyable move is: produce the social content, drive to a single free asset (webinar/PDF/diagnostic), use the asset to pitch a productised offer with a clear price. Not lots of products. One funnel. Iterate.

The second-most replicable move: **shipping production-quality videos to make the personal brand feel "expensive."** Harrison is already a filmmaker. Most LinkedIn-writer operators look like they shoot on a webcam in a beige room. Noske doesn't. That production gap is a competitive moat for Harrison if he uses it.

### Failure modes and honest asterisks

- "$6.4M in student wins" is student-side, not Noske revenue. Critically different number. Not deceptive but worth reading carefully.
- The cohort-to-membership pivot is being framed as evolution but is also a response to cohort burnout (his own admission) and possibly weakening cohort sales.
- Heavy AI promotion is a positioning play. He uses AI but is also paid (directly or in attention) to talk about it. Some of the "I built this with AI in 30 minutes" content has the same asterisks every AI-marketing video has.
- Personal-brand-heavy. If Tom Noske personally steps back, the business has no obvious independent identity.
- Reels-primary model means he doesn't own his audience the way a newsletter operator does. Instagram could change the algorithm tomorrow.

### What to study specifically

- Webinar: timetobuild.live/webinar. Watch this from start to finish. It's the conversion engine. Study the structure even if you don't buy.
- Site: timetobuild.live homepage. Note the offer page structure, the testimonial choreography, the 90-day guarantee framing.
- Instagram: @tomnoske. Spend an hour scrolling. Pay attention to hook structure, visual cuts, the ratio of personal-brand to tactical-advice content.
- LSKD interview: us.lskd.co/blogs/behind-the-brand/set-the-standard-episode-16-tom-noske. Decent biographical sketch.
- Inside Time to Build: this is what Harrison signed up for. Use it. Look at how the curriculum is structured, how the live calls run, how the community is moderated.

## 4. Tim Ferriss

### Public footprint, May 2026

| Channel | Footprint | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| The Tim Ferriss Show podcast | 1B+ downloads lifetime. Per-episode downloads in the 1-3M range. | His own disclosures, 2024-2025 |
| 5-Bullet Friday newsletter | ~1.5M subscribers (older figure of "108K marketers" was a sub-segment, not total) | Various marketing disclosures |
| Instagram | ~1.5M | Public profile |
| X (Twitter) | ~1.7M | Public profile |
| YouTube | ~700K | Channel data |
| Books sold | 2.5M+ copies of 4-Hour Workweek alone, multiple bestsellers since |

**Annual revenue.** Estimated $5-15M/year, with high variance. Sources: podcast sponsorships ($25-100 CPM, minimum $50-100K test spend per advertiser, multiple ads per episode), book royalties (residual but real), early-stage investments (Uber, Facebook, Shopify, Duolingo, Twitter ~270 startups). The investment income dwarfs the content income. Tim Ferriss is functionally a venture investor with a podcast attached, not a creator with a portfolio.

**Products currently selling:**
- The Tim Ferriss Show podcast (free, monetised by sponsorships).
- 5-Bullet Friday newsletter (free, sponsorship-monetised, also drives podcast and book sales).
- Books: 4-Hour Workweek, 4-Hour Body, 4-Hour Chef, Tools of Titans, Tribe of Mentors. Backlist still selling.
- Tim Ferriss Show "premium" experiments (largely abandoned, see failures).
- No course. No membership. No coaching. Notable absence.

### Origin and inflection points

- Pre-2007. Princeton grad, EAS sports nutrition company (BrainQUICKEN), grinding founder.
- April 2007. The 4-Hour Workweek published by Crown. Initial rejection from 25+ publishers. Became #1 NYT bestseller. The book becomes the foundational asset.
- 2010-2013. Wrote 4-Hour Body (2010) and 4-Hour Chef (2012). Built the blog tim.blog into a high-traffic outlet. Heavily covered Silicon Valley angel investing.
- April 2014. Launched The Tim Ferriss Show podcast as a "side project." Within 2 years it hit 60M downloads. By 2024 it had crossed 1B.
- 2017. Released Tools of Titans, a book of frameworks and tactics distilled from podcast guests. Different format, same engine.
- 2020-present. Podcast becomes the empire. Book cadence slows dramatically.

The inflection wasn't the blog. It wasn't even the book initially. It was the podcast launching at the right moment in podcast history (early 2014, before the gold rush).

### Content cadence and surfaces

- Podcast: 1 episode per week, sometimes 2. Long-form interviews, 1.5-3 hours.
- 5-Bullet Friday: 1 newsletter per Friday. Famously 5 short bullets, no fluff.
- Blog (tim.blog): irregular but substantial when posted. Often podcast show notes + transcripts.
- Instagram: a few posts per week, mostly podcast promotion + personal travel.
- Books: 1 every 3-5 years.

The cadence is comparatively low-volume vs Koe/Denning. Quality + investment income lets him publish less.

### Voice and structure

- Curious, precise, slightly nerdy. Long sentences when he wants depth, short when he wants emphasis.
- Books: heavy use of headers, sub-headers, "If you only read one section..." callouts. Designed for skim.
- Blog and newsletter: bullet-pointed, scannable. 5-Bullet Friday is the cleanest example of value-density-per-paragraph in the entire creator economy.
- Podcast: famously asks the same question three different ways to get past rehearsed answers. Long pauses. Lets guests think.
- Avoids: hype, hustle culture rhetoric, "I made $X" headlines. Even when he could make those claims, he chooses not to.
- Tics: "Wouldn't it be cool if..." / "What might this look like, if it were easy?" / barbell-strategy framing throughout writing and interviews.

### Monetisation stack and ladder

Free podcast and newsletter → book sales → speaking fees ($50-100K range historically) → angel investments off the back of network. Notably, no course or membership. This is the most-asked question about Ferriss: why no info-product? Answer: investment income and book residuals make it economically pointless and brand-damaging.

**What didn't work:**
- The fan-supported podcast (Patreon-style) experiment in 2018-2019. He killed it explicitly because it created content-altering pressure ("now I owe these specific listeners specific things"). Wrote a public post explaining why.
- The plant-medicine investment + advocacy phase in 2020-2022 generated some brand turbulence.
- Various failed product launches (BrainQUICKEN had trademark issues; an early supplement venture failed).

### Operating rhythm

- Lives in Austin, TX. Travel-heavy historically.
- Small team: 2-3 people running podcast production, newsletter, business operations. Has been explicit that he keeps team small intentionally.
- Tools: lots of public detail about his recording stack (Shure SM7B mic, Sound Devices recorder, etc). The "Tools of Titans" book functions partly as a public version of his stack.
- Famously batches: writes books in 3-month "write everything" sprints. Records podcasts in batches when possible.
- Heavy use of meditation, exercise, deliberate rest periods. Has talked about taking 30-day no-content sabbaticals.

### Single most replicable element

**The barbell strategy.** Two extremes, no middle. For Ferriss the podcast: ultra-famous guests (Tim Cook, Hugh Jackman) or unknown specialists (an obscure Stoic translator, a niche neuroscientist). Avoid the middle. For Harrison: do the unsexy retainer work that pays the rent (LOR-grade strategic comms) and the genuinely original content (deep essays on construction comms, the Kiwi-in-London angle, the things only he can write). Avoid the middle ("generic LinkedIn posts about productivity"). The middle is the most crowded and lowest-leverage zone.

Secondary replicable element: **5-Bullet Friday's structural discipline.** Five things, each one a single tight paragraph, each one useful even out of context. Harrison's newsletter could borrow this format wholesale.

### Failure modes and honest asterisks

- The "$5M+/year" estimate masks that most of Ferriss's net worth is investment income (Uber, Shopify, Facebook). The content business alone would not have produced his outcome. Modelling Ferriss without acknowledging this is dangerous.
- The 4HWW lifestyle was largely a marketing artifact. Tim Ferriss has worked extremely hard for 20 years. The book sold an idea he doesn't really live.
- He's been called out for promoting things he later distanced from (some supplements, some plant-medicine claims, some early biohacking enthusiasm that hasn't aged well).
- The "interview the famous person" formula has been copied to death since 2014. The arbitrage of being early to long-form podcasting is gone.
- Ferriss has hinted publicly at podcast fatigue. The 2024 "new rules for podcasting" post and the 2025 HBR "career crossroads" episode signal he's actively reconsidering the format.

### What to study specifically

- Book: 4-Hour Workweek (2007 edition is fine). Read for structure and frameworks, not lifestyle advice. The DEAL framework, the 80/20 application, the "fear-setting" exercise are still useful.
- Book: Tools of Titans. The cleanest example of distilling other people's wisdom into a personal product.
- Blog post: "How I Built a #1-Ranked Podcast With 60M+ Downloads" (tim.blog/2016/04/11). His own most useful breakdown.
- Blog post: "My New Rules for Podcasting" (tim.blog/2024/09/16). Recent thinking on what's broken about long-form interviews.
- Newsletter: 5-Bullet Friday. Subscribe for a month. Study the structure and discipline.
- Episode: "Tim Ferriss at a Career Crossroads" (HBR podcast, October 2025). Honest interview about what he's grappling with at this stage.

## 5. Justin Welsh

### Public footprint, May 2026

| Channel | Footprint | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | ~700K followers | Public profile, late 2025 |
| X (Twitter) | ~300K+ | Public profile |
| The Saturday Solopreneur newsletter | 175,000+ subscribers | His own site |
| Combined audience | ~1M+ |

**Annual revenue.** Stated revenue passed $5M lifetime by 2024. Currently running at ~$1.5-2M/year (estimated, based on course sales disclosures and newsletter sponsorship rates). The widely-cited "$5M solopreneur" figure is cumulative, not annual. He has been transparent about course sales: "What I've Learned From $3,394,480 in Digital Course Sales" essay on his site is verifiable.

**Margin: ~92%** per his own disclosures. This is the headline number worth studying. The margin is a function of: digital products only, no team, low-cost stack (Kajabi + Kit + Zapier + Stripe).

**Products currently selling (May 2026):**
- The LinkedIn Operating System, $150 (one-time). The flagship product.
- The Content Operating System, $150 (one-time).
- The Saturday Solopreneur Playbook (book), ~$30.
- The Creator MBA, $897 (one-time). Higher-ticket tying together the OS products.
- 110 Revenue-Generating Content Ideas (small lead-gen ebook).
- Newsletter sponsorships (he sells slots in The Saturday Solopreneur).

The product ladder is clean: free newsletter → $150 OS course → $150 second OS course → $897 MBA. Predictable, productised, no coaching, no agency, no consulting.

### Origin and inflection points

- Pre-2009. Six years in pharma and medical devices sales. By his own description: "super immature" and not ready for the workforce.
- 2009. Joined ZocDoc as the second sales hire. Five years there, advanced from individual contributor to regional management to special projects reporting directly to the CEO.
- January 2015. Joined PatientPop as VP of Sales. One salesperson, zero revenue. Built the team to 140 people, scaled to ~$60M ARR.
- 2019. Burnout. Panic attack. Quit. Moved to the Catskill Mountains, NY.
- August 2019. Started writing on LinkedIn. First 12 months were unglamorous.
- July-August 2021. Released The LinkedIn Playbook (his first product), then immediately rebuilt and released The LinkedIn Operating System on August 4, 2021. This is the actual inflection.
- 2022-2023. Launched The Saturday Solopreneur newsletter. Crossed 100K subscribers within 12 months.
- 2023-2024. Launched Content OS, then The Creator MBA. Productised the full ladder.
- 2025. Crossed $5M lifetime revenue. Began publicly framing the next stage as "portfolio of one-person businesses."

The PatientPop scale-and-burnout is the load-bearing piece of his origin story. It's not just biography. It's the credibility lever for the entire "you don't need to build a team to build a real business" pitch.

### Content cadence and surfaces

- LinkedIn: 1 post per day, every day, since August 2019. Famously consistent.
- X (Twitter): 1-3 posts per day.
- Newsletter: 1 newsletter every Saturday morning, 1,000-1,200 words, ~4 minutes to read.
- YouTube: occasional, not primary.
- Podcast: appears on others' regularly. Doesn't run his own.

The cadence is disciplined but lower-volume than Koe or Denning. Welsh trades volume for repeatability and recency-bias-friendly daily presence.

### Voice and structure

- Pragmatic, tactical, no-nonsense. Reads like a sales VP writing for other sales VPs.
- Sentence structure: short, declarative, frequent line breaks. LinkedIn-native formatting.
- Newsletter: highly templated. 8-part structure he's documented publicly (idea bank → research → template → headers → body → visuals → title → edit).
- Recurring rhetorical moves: numbered frameworks ("3 things I'd do differently"), self-referential case studies ("Here's how I built X"), explicit promises in the opening line ("By the end of this post, you'll have a 4-step playbook").
- Avoids: philosophy, big-picture future-of-work theorising, anything that smells aspirational rather than tactical.
- Tics: "Here's the truth..." / "Most people get this wrong..." / "I'll save you 5 years..." Effective but template-shaped, which makes the work feel mechanical at scale.

### Monetisation stack and ladder

Free LinkedIn content → free newsletter (Saturday Solopreneur) → $30 ebook lead magnets → $150 LinkedIn OS → $150 Content OS → $897 Creator MBA → newsletter sponsorships.

Order of products historically:
1. The LinkedIn Playbook (first attempt, 2021, killed and rebuilt)
2. The LinkedIn Operating System (Aug 2021, the flagship)
3. The Content Operating System (~2022)
4. The Saturday Solopreneur newsletter (2022)
5. The Creator MBA (~2023)

He killed his first product publicly ("I'm killing The LinkedIn Playbook" essay) and rebuilt it. That iteration, plus the discipline of keeping the price point at $150 instead of pushing into $500-1000 territory, is a deliberate volume-over-margin choice.

**What didn't work:** The LinkedIn Playbook v1 was killed. He's also experimented with various small products that he's quietly retired. Has been transparent that some newsletter formats (longer-form essays) underperformed and got cut.

### Operating rhythm

- Solo. Genuinely. Has occasional contractors but no full-time team.
- Lives in upstate NY (Catskills). Runs the entire business from a home office.
- Daily writing block in the morning. LinkedIn post drafted by 9am, published by 10-11am ET (peak engagement window).
- Newsletter written in 75 minutes per week (his own claim, documented in his "How to Build a World-Class Newsletter" piece).
- Tools stack: Kit (formerly ConvertKit) for email, Kajabi for courses, Stripe for payments, Zapier for everything else, Notion for ideation, Hypefury for LinkedIn scheduling.
- Has talked about working ~25-30 hours per week.

### Single most replicable element

**The 1-post-per-day on a single platform discipline, then the newsletter as the ladder rung above it.** Welsh's playbook is the closest direct match for a 25-year-old solo operator with B2B credibility starting on LinkedIn. He proves that:
- One platform deeply > five platforms shallowly.
- Daily cadence on LinkedIn beats sporadic brilliance.
- A weekly templated newsletter is a sustainable production rate.
- A simple ladder (free → $150 → $897) outperforms a complex one.

If Harrison were to copy one operator's structural playbook beat for beat for 12 months, it would be Welsh's.

### Failure modes and honest asterisks

- The "92% margin" headline ignores that Welsh has 20 years of pre-existing B2B credibility (ZocDoc, PatientPop). The model works partly because his audience trusted him as "a real sales VP" before they trusted him as a creator.
- His content has become more templated and less interesting over time. Several long-time followers have publicly noted the diminishing originality. The system is selling.
- The product price points ($150, $897) are arguably underpriced for the value, and he's said as much. He optimised for volume of sales, not per-customer revenue. Different operators (Koe, Noske) optimised the other way.
- "Solo" is true but should be read carefully. Welsh outsources design, video editing, occasional tasks. He has no employees, but he has contractors.
- The LinkedIn-only concentration is a single-platform risk. If LinkedIn algorithms shift hard against him, the entire engine is exposed.
- The "I retired at 39" framing is partially true but Welsh works hard. Same Ferriss-style asterisk applies.

## Cross-Cutting Synthesis

### 1. What all five share

**They all built on a single platform first.** Not three. Not five. One. Koe was Twitter-primary. Denning was Medium-primary. Welsh was LinkedIn-only. Noske was Instagram-Reels-primary. Ferriss was the blog first, then the podcast. Multi-channel only came after the first channel was clearly working. The common error among aspiring operators is to launch on five surfaces simultaneously and split focus.

**They all had a 2-3 year unglamorous build.** Koe: 2019 (started Twitter) to 2022 (Koe Letter virality). Denning: 2017 (first Medium post) to ~2020 ($60K month). Welsh: August 2019 (started writing) to August 2021 (LinkedIn OS launch). Noske: 2019 (filmmaker tutorials) to 2022 (first Time to Build cohort). Ferriss: had the longest runway, with BrainQUICKEN in the early 2000s and the 4HWW manuscript rejected 25+ times before publication in 2007. None of them had immediate traction. None.

**They all use a written newsletter as the centre or near-centre of gravity.** Even Noske, the Reels-primary outlier, runs an email list and a webinar funnel. Even Ferriss, who is podcast-primary in revenue terms, uses 5-Bullet Friday as the audience-owned asset. Owned audience > rented audience is the universal pattern.

**They all productised aggressively rather than selling time.** Koe: courses + community. Denning: courses + cohorts. Welsh: $150 courses + $897 MBA. Noske: $200/month membership. Ferriss: books + podcast sponsorships (a productised audience). None of them are senior consultants charging per hour at scale. The productisation is the point.

**They all had a credibility-claim foundation.** Koe: "I figured out social media in 12 months." Denning: "I escaped the corporate banking grind." Welsh: "I scaled PatientPop to $60M as VP Sales." Noske: "I'm a commercial filmmaker who built this from $12K in debt." Ferriss: "Princeton + sports nutrition founder + NYT bestseller author." Pre-existing legitimacy made the audience-build faster.

**They all ship daily-or-near-daily on their primary channel.** Welsh: 1/day LinkedIn. Koe: multiple/day on X. Denning: 1-2/day on LinkedIn + Medium. Noske: ~1/day on Instagram. Ferriss is the exception with weekly podcast cadence, but his backlist of 700+ episodes carries the daily-discovery work.

### 2. Where they diverge

**Volume vs precision.** Denning publishes 8-15 pieces per week. Welsh publishes 1 post per day plus 1 newsletter per week. Both work. They are opposite philosophies. Pick one. Trying to do both produces medium-volume medium-quality medium-results.

**Long-form vs short-form.** Koe and Ferriss optimise for long-form anchor pieces (the weekly essay, the 2-hour podcast). Welsh and Denning optimise for short-form, scannable daily content. Noske optimises for short video. The choice is partly format-economic (long-form is harder to produce but compounds more in evergreen value) and partly personality-driven.

**Philosophy-led vs tactic-led.** Koe is philosophy-first (one-person business as a worldview). Welsh is tactic-first (here's the playbook, no theory). Denning sits in the middle. Noske is increasingly philosophy-led ("Time to Build" is a philosophical framing). Ferriss is curiosity-led, neither pure philosophy nor pure tactics. The fork point: do you sell readers a worldview that requires your products, or do you sell them a result your products produce?

**Solo vs small team.** Welsh is genuinely solo (with light contractors). Ferriss has a small team. Noske has a meaningful team (head coach, community manager, video editor). Denning has 1-2 VAs. Koe claims solo but has had editors and is a co-founder of Eden. The "one-person business" branding is more marketing than literal across all of them. This matters because Harrison will face the same temptation to inflate the solo narrative.

**Audience: deep niche vs broad.** Welsh: B2B solopreneurs (deep niche). Denning: personal development / writers (broad). Koe: meta-creators (broad and getting broader). Noske: aspiring online entrepreneurs (broad). Ferriss: ambitious knowledge workers (broadest). The deeper-niche operators (Welsh) have higher conversion rates per follower. The broader ones have larger raw audiences but lower conversion.

**Tone toward money.** Denning leads with money ("how I made $X"). Koe is allergic to specific revenue claims and prefers philosophical framing. Welsh is transparent about specific dollar figures ($3,394,480 essay). Noske highlights "student wins" rather than personal revenue. Ferriss never leads with money. The choice signals the audience and the brand: aggressive money-talk attracts hustle-culture readers, philosophical avoidance attracts more skeptical readers.

### 3. For Harrison specifically

**Closest direct match: Justin Welsh.**

Harrison's position (25, B2B credibility from LOR/Creepers/BaW, London, writing-primary, weekly LinkedIn + newsletter cadence, productisation goal) maps onto Welsh's playbook more cleanly than the others. Same single-platform-first. Same B2B-buyer audience. Same writing-as-primary. Same productised-courses end state.

What Harrison should steal from Welsh:
- Daily LinkedIn discipline. Not 5x/week. Daily. Even on race day.
- Saturday newsletter format. 1,000-1,200 words. Single actionable idea per issue. 4-minute read promise.
- The 8-part newsletter writing template (idea bank → research → template → headers → body → visuals → title → edit).
- Eventually the $150 self-paced course as the entry-tier product, with a $897 deeper offer above it.
- The 92% margin discipline. No team. Contractors only. Software stack lean.

What Harrison should steal from Koe:
- The newsletter-as-central-artifact model. Write the long-form first, harvest LinkedIn from it. Don't write LinkedIn posts hoping they form a newsletter.
- The willingness to write essays that are not directly tied to a sale. The brand-build essays that compound.
- The 90-minute morning writing block. Walk first, write second.

What Harrison should steal from Noske:
- The webinar-to-product funnel architecture. Ferriss doesn't have one. Welsh barely uses one. Noske has perfected it. When Harrison eventually has a productised offer, the conversion mechanism is a free 45-minute webinar/diagnostic.
- The production quality flex. Harrison is a filmmaker. Most LinkedIn writers ship beige selfie videos. Don't.
- The recurring-revenue membership over lumpy cohort launches. Long-term consideration.

What Harrison should steal from Ferriss:
- The barbell strategy. Either deeply original (Kiwi-in-London-doing-construction-comms angle) or service-grade tactical (the templates, the frameworks). Avoid the middle of generic creator content.
- The 5-Bullet Friday format as a weekly "lighter" alternative to the long-form newsletter.
- The discipline of saying no to courses for as long as possible. Build the audience first. Productise from a position of demand, not desperation.

What Harrison should steal from Denning:
- The volume mindset for the unglamorous middle phase. There will be 6-9 months where engagement is low and that's fine.
- The platform-arbitrage instinct: when one channel decays, pivot fast, don't sentimentalise.
- The Aussie-in-London angle is genuinely usable. Kiwi-in-London is an analogous outsider-position. Use it.

What Harrison should ignore:
- Denning's stakes-inflation rhetoric. Don't open posts with "I lost everything." It will read as cringe coming from Harrison's voice.
- Koe's philosophy-heavy abstraction. Harrison's audience (B2B buyers, mid-career marketers, founders) wants tactics, not Naval-Jung mashups.
- Noske's Reels-primary cadence. Harrison's strengths are written. Reels are a useful supplement, not the centre.
- Ferriss's "investment income" implicit subsidy. Modelling Ferriss's product cadence without his investment portfolio is dangerous; the low cadence works because the residual income covers the floor.

**Specific recommendation for the 62-day Stones Build (May 11 to July 11):**

- Welsh-style discipline: 1 LinkedIn post per day, 5-6 days per week, for the full 62 days.
- Welsh-style newsletter: 1 Saturday newsletter per week. Use the 8-part template. 1,000-1,200 words. (Beehiiv hosting per the brief.)
- Koe-style content harvest: write the newsletter first on Friday. Pull the four sharpest paragraphs and queue them as Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu LinkedIn posts the following week. One post per week is original, four are excerpts.
- Ferriss-style barbell: each week, one post is a tactical "here's the playbook" piece (Welsh-mode), one is a deeply original observation only Harrison could write (the construction/comms intersection, the Kiwi-outsider angle). Avoid the middle.
- Noske-style production flex: every two weeks, ship one 60-90 second talking-head video on LinkedIn. Don't make it daily. Make it noticeably better than the platform average.
- Denning-style learning: when something is working at 3x normal engagement, write three more in the same shape immediately. Don't be precious.

### 4. The harsh-truth one-liner per operator

**Dan Koe.** The 2-hour-writing-day branding masks a 10-hour-business-day reality. Don't model the writing time without modelling the rest of the operation.

**Tim Denning.** The peak Medium years were a one-off arbitrage that no longer exists. Modelling Denning's volume in 2026 to chase 2020-2021 income is fighting the last war.

**Tom Noske.** The "$6.4M in student wins" is a student-side number sold as a Tom-side number. Read his metrics carefully and treat them as marketing first, evidence second.

**Tim Ferriss.** The 4-Hour-Workweek lifestyle is a marketing artifact, not a real life. Tim Ferriss has worked extremely hard for two decades. The investment income, not the content business, is what makes his cadence economically possible.

**Justin Welsh.** The "92% margin solo business" is real but built on 15 years of pre-existing B2B credibility from ZocDoc and PatientPop. Without the prior career, the audience build would have been 3x slower. Harrison has analogous pre-existing credibility (LOR, Creepers, BaW); use it as the launch capital it actually is.

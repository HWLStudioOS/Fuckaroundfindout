## Part 2. Tax and structure

HWL Studio Limited is a close company. Your dad is an "associate" of you as participator, and a member of your "family or household" for the benefits code (parents are included, ITEPA 2003 s.721(4) and (5)). A Mac mini for his personal use in NZ is not wholly and exclusively for the trade. There is no honest way to book it as a business expense.

**(a) Company buys it as a gift to Dad.** Because you are a director, a benefit provided to a member of your family by reason of your employment is treated as provided to you (ITEPA s.201 and s.721, HMRC [EIM20020](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim20020), [EIM21869](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim21869) confirms family or household of close-company directors is caught). An asset given new is taxed on its cost to the company ([gov.uk: assets bought, sold or given](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-and-benefits-assets-bought-sold-or-given)). That means a P11D for you by 6 July 2027, Class 1A NIC on the company at 15% ([CWG5 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cwg5-class-1a-national-insurance-contributions-on-benefits-in-kind/2026-class-1a-national-insurance-contributions-on-benefits-in-kind-termination-payments-and-sporting-testimonial-payments)) due 22 July 2027, and income tax on you at 20% via Self Assessment. Illustrative on £799: Class 1A £120, your income tax £160, CT relief on the £919 staff cost worth roughly £175 to £245 depending on your marginal rate. Net cost about £830 to £900 plus a filing you do not currently have. If HMRC decides it was not remuneration at all but a shareholder perk, CTA 2010 s.1064 recasts it as a distribution: no CT deduction, taxed on you as a dividend ([CTM60550](https://gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm60550)). VAT: goods bought for a non-business purpose carry no input tax recovery, and if you reclaim on a gift over £50 you owe output tax anyway ([VAT Notice 700/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/business-promotions-and-vat-notice-7007)). Do not reclaim it. Trivial-benefit exemption does not apply, it stops at £50 per item and £300 a year for close-company directors. This route is legal, admin-heavy, and saves you at most about £40 against option (b). Not worth the P11D or the conversation with Fazila.

**(b) You buy it personally, from post-tax money.** Cleanest. Pay from your own account, or send Dad the money on Wise and let him order. If you want the company card for convenience, post it to your director's loan account and clear it with the next dividend, and tell Litchfields so it is not coded as equipment. No P11D, no Class 1A, no VAT question, no distribution argument. On a dividend at 8.75% you need about £876 gross to net £799, or about £765 gross to net £700 at the NZ price. Recommended.

**(c) Dad genuinely works for HWL, company lends him equipment.** Only if it is actually true: a written engagement, real invoices at a real rate, real work product (agent testing, admin, whatever). Then the Mac mini is a company asset lent to a contractor, capital allowances can apply ([AIA](https://www.gov.uk/capital-allowances/annual-investment-allowance)), input VAT is recoverable to the extent of business use, and NZ import GST still applies at his door. Any private use he gets from a company asset provided because of your directorship is a benefit on you at 20% of market value per year, and HMRC's first question about a director's dad in NZ holding a company Mac with no invoices is obvious. If the work is real, do it properly with Fazila. If it is not, this is dressing it up and it is the option most likely to cost you an enquiry.

Recommendation: (b). Personal money, Apple NZ, delivered to Dad. When the LOR cash lands and the VAT is sized, take the extra dividend to cover it if you want the company to have paid for it economically.

**NZ border rules ([NZ Customs, What's My Duty](https://www.customs.govt.nz/personal/duty-and-gst/whats-my-duty-estimator/), [Easy Freight on 1 Apr 2026 fee changes](https://easyfreight.co.nz/blog/nz-customs-fees-change/)):** Customs does not collect GST or duty on goods valued NZ$1,000 or less. Above NZ$1,000 a formal import entry is needed, GST at 15% is charged on goods plus freight plus insurance, and the combined Customs goods management plus MPI biosecurity levy for air is NZ$59.58 including GST per entry (from 1 April 2026). Computers are duty-free under the NZ tariff, so GST is the only tax. A £799 Mac mini is NZ$1,837 at the border, so it clears the threshold with room to spare. Dad pays the courier before release.

**UK VAT on the outbound side:** as a private buyer you cannot reclaim the 20% UK VAT when you export it. The VAT Retail Export Scheme ended in Great Britain on 1 January 2021 ([VAT Notice 703](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-on-goods-exported-from-the-uk-notice-703)). Routing it through HWL to zero-rate an "export" of a gift is the dressing-up problem from Part 2. You pay UK VAT and then NZ GST. Double taxed.

# The Five Advisors

## 1. Contrarian (pre-mortem at 6 months)

**Confidence:** 7. Changes if Harrison ships a 1-page "steal this folder structure" PDF as take-home and does one full dry-run on a clean machine before 8 June.

The room had 34 people. Eight stayed past minute 45.

- **The folder tour was unwatchable on Zoom.** Live screen-share of nested directories at default font size, with Harrison narrating "and this folder here contains..." for 30 minutes. Non-technical members glazed within 8 minutes. Folder tours are a writer's format, not a presenter's format.
- **The Claude Code live demo broke.** Anthropic shipped a CLI update the morning of 9 June, Harrison hadn't pinned a version, the cascade prompt errored at minute 52. Recovery took 4 minutes of dead air.
- **The "no homework, no template repo" framing read as withholding.** Year-One members pay for tools they can use Monday morning. "Watch me work" without a take-home reads as ego, not generosity.
- **The Arsenal-viral hook attracted the wrong audience.** 184k views suggests "teach me to go viral," but the talk was about file structure. Expectation mismatch.
- **The Laurence relationship cooled by August.** Not a blowup. Just no second invite. The Q2 mentorship proposal got a polite "let's revisit in Q4."
- **Hidden risk:** Harrison is presenting to his own peer group while simultaneously trying to sell Laurence on a paid engagement. Status leakage is asymmetric — upside is "nice talk," downside is the mentorship door quietly closing.

**Single biggest reason this goes wrong:** the format is optimised for Harrison's comfort (showing real work) not the audience's job-to-be-done (leaving with one thing they can do tomorrow).

## 2. First Principles (challenge the frame)

**Confidence:** 7. Would change my mind if Laurence has explicitly sold the session as a live walkthrough and reframing damages the relationship more than the upside justifies.

The proposal optimises for "great workshop." Wrong variable. The real question: **what asset does Harrison want to own on 10 June that he doesn't own on 26 May?** A 90-min live Zoom for ≤40 non-technical members watching a folder tour produces: goodwill with Laurence, a recording with limited reuse, and zero net-new audience. That's a thin ROI for a man mid-viral-window.

**Assumptions to challenge:** (1) live walkthrough is the format (live demos of code-in-folders to non-technical viewers historically underwhelm), (2) Year-One members are the audience that matters (Harrison's actual audience is the +315 Arsenal followers + whoever sees the artefact), (3) prep is 4-6 hours (live demos with novel software always overrun, realistic is 8-10), (4) the deliverable is the session (it should be a public artefact the session helps produce).

**Rebuilt from zero:** Pre-record a 25-min tight walkthrough in week 1 (3 hrs prep). Publish it publicly on 9 June as the Substack/YouTube anchor piece of the post-Arsenal content GTM. Use the Year-One slot (live, 60 min) as Q&A + bespoke teardown of one member's content workflow, volunteered in advance. Members get something they cannot get from the public video. Laurence gets a higher-value session, not a lower one. Negotiate this with Laurence on Wed.

## 4. Outsider (industry pattern check, no Harrison-specific context)

**Confidence:** 7. What would change my mind: knowing the host's explicit brief, the community's prior workshop ratings, and whether attendees skew technical or non-technical.

- **Industry-standard play:** 90-min paid-community workshops over-index on tactical demos with one strong takeaway artifact. Pure live demos without leave-behind assets consistently rate lower in post-session NPS.
- **Folder-tour format:** Lands when the operator narrates *decisions* ("I killed this folder because..."), bombs when it becomes a tour of file names. 30 minutes is too long. Attendees disengage at minute 12. Compress to 15, replace with a teardown of one specific piece end-to-end.
- **Live AI/LLM demos:** fail 25-40% of the time (latency, model drift, API hiccups, screen-share lag). Always have a pre-recorded backup loop.
- **Host-as-mentor dynamic:** When the community host is also pitching paid mentorship, the speaker is being auditioned in front of the host's revenue base. Most common failure: speaker over-performs and accidentally positions as a peer/competitor. Don't sell. Don't soft-pitch. Let the host introduce any commercial follow-up.
- **Viral-moment hook risk:** "I went viral, here's how" workshops have the highest no-show-to-rewatch ratio in the category. Reframe the hook around the *system that existed before the viral moment*. The viral hit is proof, not product.
- **To a sharp 26-year-old:** Cut demo to 20, add a one-page take-home, kill the title (too generic; "AI" and "2026" both pattern-match as slop in 2026). Lead with a specific number or named result.

## Draft

Hi,

I used to think about conflict as a simple scale.

At one end, people charge in. At the other, they avoid even a slightly edgy conversation.

Alice Driscoll and Louise van Haarst show why that is incomplete in *Smart Conflict*.

Conflict confidence matters. So does whether you are trying to get it right or be right.

The distinction I found most useful was singles versus doubles tennis.

Relational conflict is singles. I am right, you are wrong. I win, you lose. We score points off each other.

Task conflict is doubles. You and me against the problem.

The shift is not to become comfortable with conflict overnight. It is to get the other person onto the same side of the net.

Alice and Louise use a phrase that has made its way onto a tea towel: get curious, not furious.

It works at both ends of the scale.

If you tend to go from nought to 100, notice the heat. Suspend judgement. Step away if needed. Have one question ready that moves you back into curiosity.

If you tend to avoid, ask what might happen if you shared what you really think.

For the next disagreement, write down three things:

1. Am I trying to get it right, or be right?
2. What is the problem we are both trying to solve?
3. What is one real question I can ask?

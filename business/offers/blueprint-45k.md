# The HWL Strategic Communications Blueprint

A six-week engagement that turns silent excellence into visible leadership.

For organisations doing world-class work that nobody outside the company can articulate.

---

## The problem this solves

You're running, advising, or leading communications at an organisation that's genuinely good at what it does. The work is real. The senior team knows it. The market does not. The narrative externally feels like it belongs to a different company than the one inside the building.

You've probably tried:

- Brand agencies that delivered a manifesto you'd be embarrassed to read aloud
- Internal comms hires who confused activity with impact
- Content production that felt like a box-tick exercise
- Generic transformation language that didn't survive contact with your actual workforce

What's missing isn't more output. It's a frame that connects what's actually true about the organisation to how you talk about it. That frame, written down, is the Blueprint.

---

## What you get

A single strategic document, around 60 pages, that contains:

1. **Strategic frame.** What this organisation actually is, in one paragraph that survives Monday morning.
2. **Tone of voice framework.** Built on the Quiet Confidence model, originally developed for a £5bn UK construction client and adopted internally there. No engagement bait. No corporate celebration content. No raw and real performed instead of practised.
3. **Demographic-differentiated messaging architecture.** A 22-year-old apprentice and a 50-year-old project director don't read the same way. The Blueprint maps what to say, where, to whom, and what not to say.
4. **Channel strategy.** Earned, owned, and paid. Where each audience actually is, not where the agency thinks they should be.
5. **Content pillar architecture.** Three to five themes the organisation owns, each with a defensible reason behind it.
6. **12-month implementation roadmap.** Sequenced, with measurement points.
7. **Measurement framework.** What good looks like, in numbers a CFO recognises as real.

## Who this is for

You run, advise, or own communications at an organisation that meets at least three of these:

- £20M+ revenue, or equivalent institutional weight
- 50+ employees, or work that touches that many
- Mid-transformation: leadership change, market repositioning, M&A, capital event, generational shift in workforce
- Already producing decent work, just not getting credit for it
- Senior team that respects strategy and is allergic to vibes

You don't need the Blueprint if your CEO can recite the central narrative without notes and your senior team agrees on what the organisation is. You do need it if every conversation about identity ends in a slightly different answer.

## How it runs

Six weeks. Fixed structure. Run by the principal, not delegated to a delivery team.

**Week 1: Discovery.** 8-12 senior stakeholder interviews. Audit of every public-facing surface. Read of internal material I'm trusted with. Quiet observation.

**Week 2: Strategic frame.** First-pass on the central frame. One conversation with the senior sponsor. Course-corrected.

**Week 3: Tone and messaging.** Quiet Confidence applied to your context. Demographic mapping. First language that survives the room.

**Week 4: Channel and content architecture.** What lives where. The pillars. Defensibility tested.

**Week 5: Implementation roadmap.** What gets shipped, in what order, by whom. Measurement points locked.

**Week 6: Delivery and handover.** Final Blueprint document. A 90-minute walkthrough with your team. Thirty days of post-delivery questions answered.

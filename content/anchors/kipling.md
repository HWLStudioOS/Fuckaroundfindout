## Title card sequence

Pick four to six lines from "If—" to land as on-screen text. Burned in, Gruppe A, white, held 2 beats each. Cued to the score's downbeats, not the spoken line (let the voice carry the line, the type carries the weight).

**Recommended pulls (in order of poem):**

1. *If you can trust yourself when all men doubt you*
2. *Meet with Triumph and Disaster and treat those two impostors just the same*
3. *Force your heart and nerve and sinew to serve your turn long after they are gone*
4. *And so hold on when there is nothing in you*
5. *Except the Will which says to them: hold on*
6. *Fill the unforgiving minute with sixty seconds' worth of distance run*

Use four of these maximum. Six is too dense for a 60-90s piece.

**My pick for the four:**
- *Triumph and Disaster, two impostors* (the philosophical anchor)
- *Hold on when there is nothing in you* (the operator's line)
- *The unforgiving minute* (the personal tempo)
- *Yours is the Earth* (the close, if you can make the final beat land on it)

Save the others for future title cards or for the Substack companion essay.

## LinkedIn editorial post (Tier C, Day 2)

Posted the day after the film launches. Editorial angle on the launch itself, not a repost of the film.

Working draft:

```
After ten years of making other people's work, I published my own this week.

Just under two minutes of footage shot across the Dolomites, the States, New York, and home.

Set to Kipling's IF.

No face. No talking head. No "here's what I learned." Just the work and the words.

For ten years I've been pointing cameras at things for clients. Most operators in this lane never publish their own work. The reasons are good: client confidentiality, time, the gap between what you'd like to make and what you can ship at scale.

But the absence of a personal body of work is also a status signal. It says "I make things for whoever pays." It does not say "this is what I see."

I'm changing that this year. Field notes from the chase, in public.

The film is on Instagram. The longer essay behind it is in the newsletter.
```

Closing line is a soft pointer to the essay/film. No CTA bait, no "comment below."

## What to confirm before launch

1. **Cinematic score**: which track is under the Kipling reading? Confirm licensing situation. If it's a copyrighted music track that hasn't been cleared, Meta may mute it on IG and YouTube; TikTok will likely fingerprint and either remove audio or geo-restrict. Three options: (a) use a properly licensed track via Artgrid or Musicbed, (b) use a free track from YouTube Audio Library or Pixabay that has the right vibe, (c) accept the mute risk and post anyway with a fallback caption.

2. **Kipling reading**: whose voice? If yours, that anchors the personal-brand signal even with no face. If a sourced reading (Bryan Cranston's famous version, an Audible reading, etc.), credit in the caption and consider licensing. Recommend your own voice if you can stomach it.

3. **Title card timing**: send the rough cut and I'll mark exact cue points where each pulled line should land on the downbeat.

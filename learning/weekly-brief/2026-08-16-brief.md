  Published 4 August 2026, covering Karpathy's 2 August demo. - **8 Predictions for the Era of Continual Learning**, essay, Dwarkesh Patel. [https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/era-of-continual-learning](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/era-of-continual-learning)
  Published 7 August 2026. Dwarkesh's central claim: once models update from session to session instead of starting fresh each time, switching providers stops being free. Today you can start a repo with Codex and finish it with Claude Code at zero cost; once a model has months of context on your organisation, replacing it means firing an employee and retraining a new one from scratch, which is exactly the lock-in leading labs need to charge real margins. You're already running Claude and OpenAI side by side as a deliberate "diversity of intelligence" setup. This is the argument for why that posture has a shelf life, and for thinking now about what you'd lose if you ever had to consolidate onto one.

- **Broicism: How the Gym-Bros Rebranded Stoicism, and Broke It**, essay, citewise.net (term credited to Massimo Pigliucci). [https://citewise.net/broicism/](https://citewise.net/broicism/)
  The sharpest version yet of a critique that's been building for three weeks running (Pigliucci's institutional-blind-spot essay on 26 July, Trujillo's "Stoicism Sucks" paper on 9 August). Names Ryan Holiday and Tim Ferriss directly and draws one precise distinction: real Stoicism is about examining the judgment underneath a feeling, the same move CBT's Albert Ellis later rebuilt from scratch and credited to Epictetus by name. Broicism keeps the marble busts and the "nothing touches me" aesthetic and throws out that method entirely, collapsing "don't be ruled by emotion" into "don't have any." Worth reading against the Holiday shelf currently in rotation, not as a takedown of the underlying philosophy but as a precise name for what the packaging strips out.

- **Why the Benefits of Group Exercise Are Hardwired into Your Brain**, essay, Alex Hutchinson, Outside. [https://www.outsideonline.com/health/training-performance/benefits-group-exercise-science/](https://www.outsideonline.com/health/training-performance/benefits-group-exercise-science/)
  Published 6 August 2026. Oxford anthropologist Emma Cohen's research programme, "social energetics": rowers rowing to exhaustion lasted 24% longer while viewing a photo of a friend or family member versus a stranger, with identical perceived effort throughout. The theory, built with Herman Pontzer and Chris Beedie, is that fatigue is a safety margin the brain budgets down when it feels socially supported, mediated by endorphins, endocannabinoids, and possibly oxytocin. Direct implication for RTTS training: who you run long efforts with may move your actual output more than most training-plan variables, and it's a mechanism, not a vibe.

- **Gluons, not just quarks, may carry a proton's baryon number**, research news, Nature, covering the STAR Collaboration's Science paper. [https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02498-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02498-1)
  Published 12 August 2026. New results from Brookhaven's now-retired Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider challenge the textbook picture that a proton's three quarks alone carry its baryon number. STAR found that in nuclear collisions, baryons preferentially spray out perpendicular to the beam rather than forward, which matches a model where the number is carried by a Y-shaped "junction" of gluons connecting the quarks, not the quarks themselves. Independent physicists call it real evidence, not yet a smoking gun. A rare case of a 50-year-old textbook assumption about the most basic unit of visible matter getting a genuine experimental challenge.

- **The One-Person Enterprise: AI and the Case for Staying Small**, essay, Matt Hopkins. [https://matthopkins.com/business/one-person-enterprise-ai-small-business/](https://matthopkins.com/business/one-person-enterprise-ai-small-business/)
  Published 26 July 2026. Cites Fast Company's figure of 30 million US solopreneurs generating $1.7 trillion in output, and the sharpest structural point: the old bottleneck on a solo operator was capacity (only so many hours), AI removes that, so the bottleneck relocates to definition, can you say precisely enough what you want done. An agent you can't brief precisely will still act, just on whatever rule it inferred rather than the one in your head. Honest about the ceiling too: solo-plus-AI reliably lifts individual output to small-team level, but the truly original, category-defining work still comes from diverse teams amplified by AI, not soloists however well equipped. Direct relevance to the solopreneur community and white-label content partnership ideas in the pipeline right now: the real design question is whether you can define the offer precisely enough to hand pieces of it to agents or contractors, not whether you have time to build it.

## Synthesis

- **Verification, not generation, is where 2026's agentic frontier actually sits**: Karpathy's Opus 5 could write 5,500 lines of coherent, self-directed Three.js code but couldn't watch its own output and had to slowly screenshot its way through checking itself. Ethan Mollick's June essay on the shift from co-intelligence to agent management makes the same point from the human side: work is moving from prompting chatbots to managing long-running agents, which only works if you can verify what they did. Dwarkesh's continual-learning piece adds the sting: as agents get harder to audit in the moment, switching away from one you've built trust with gets more expensive, not less. None of the three needed the model to be malicious or even wrong, just autonomous and hard to check. ## Recall drills

Rotating to the five drills last surfaced on 26 July, avoiding the five used on 9 August (biological lessons, freedom/equality, race, progress, conservatism).

1. Q: Durant's claim on wealth concentration. What's the mechanism, and what periodically interrupts it?
   A: (hidden, expand after attempt) Wealth concentrates as a natural function of unequal ability. Interrupted periodically by either legislation that redistributes peacefully or revolution that redistributes violently. The cycle recurs across civilisations.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 8 "Economics and History."

2. Q: Across 3,421 years of recorded history, how many years of peace did the Durants count?
   A: (hidden) Approximately 268 years. War is the constant, not the exception.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 11 "History and War."

3. Q: What is the only real check on greed, per Durant?
   A: (hidden) The equally good greed of another. Competition, not virtue.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 11.

4. Q: Durant on morals. Are they universal or contextual, and what shapes them?
   A: (hidden) Contextual. Morals are habits of action shaped by the dominant economic mode (hunting, agricultural, industrial). When the mode shifts, morals lag and feel "broken" during the transition.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 6 "Morals and History."

5. Q: Durant on religion's function in history. What happens when it declines?
   A: (hidden) Religion acts as social cement. It declines as wealth and security rise, but its structural function, binding the group, transmitting moral training to children, consoling the suffering, reappears in secular forms. The function is structural and doesn't disappear with the institution.
   Source: The Lessons of History, Ch. 7 "Religion and History."

**Voice note 1 (7m33s) — the grow story.** Cleaned of transcription repeats; this is the source for the VO above.

> To grow a Chelsea garden it takes 10 months of growing, preening, care to deliver world-class plants on a world-class stage. Chelsea for the growers starts probably a year before the show opens, so we are discussing preliminary plant lists with designers sometimes during show week the year before. Those lists arrive on our desks to be consulted. Once the consultation process is complete the list is submitted for growing. During that consultation period lists are pulled apart, discussed, re-discussed, pushed back and forth between the designer and the grower. As the grower it is our responsibility to manage expectations and to make sure the designer is acquiring the desired effect whilst making the plants achievable. Once the plant lists are confirmed we start the purchasing process. This year we grew three gardens, which saw us use 23 suppliers across the UK and Europe. The plants get sent down to our New Forest nursery, potted on from a two litre to a five litre, a nine centimetre into a two litre, a plug into a nine, so we can put them on the ground and ensure they get the best possible care in a controlled environment to enhance root production as quickly as possible. The idea is to ensure everything is flowering, everything is performing, come press day, when judging is completed, the photographs are being taken and everyone's celebrating. So we reverse-engineer the contract grow to one specific day of the year. It takes a lot of skill, some luck, and quite a lot of luck with the judging. Through the consultation period there will be times we say that won't work, that's a July flowering plant, it will flower too early, but it's okay if you want it for foliage. We put plants into certain areas of the nursery, sunny inside, shady inside, or outside. Aquilegias inside to bulk the basal growth then out early. Ferns kept in shade inside for the humidity they need. We are constantly on the walk around, on the lookout for any signs of stress, pest or disease, to keep the quality perfect. Only the best will do for Chelsea, so we grow double what the garden requires, so when weaker plugs fall by the wayside we still have the numbers the designer needs. As Christmas approaches everything has died back, we cut back what's required, top up with mulch, and let the plants set for the Christmas break. Then before you know it you are in May delivering. Between Christmas and delivery everything ramps up, growing at different rates, and it's your job to make sure they hit the correct flowering time for the show. This year, a super wet end of winter into a really warm early spring, plants woke up and put flowers up very fast, things like cardamine pratensis bolted, so we cut back. Decisions made on instinct, trying to understand what the weather's doing, which is difficult. We don't have a cold store, we don't have super dense shade, we make it work the best way we can in the New Forest nursery. Then final selection, when the designers come and meet me at the nursery. We walk around, I consult on what they want, they ask do I think this will work, is that too far forward, will it flower in time. We create a list with final numbers, split into two or three deliveries, get it on the system. When we lift the plants our team painstakingly go through each one, removing any yellow leaf, any damage, weeding, so they go to the showground ready to plant. The designers have enough stress as it is, so we make the plants as pristine as possible. Sending 8,000 plants to the showground is a monumental task, and we have three days to complete it. When the plants arrive at the showground, it is down to the designers to create their masterpieces with the world-class ingredients we deliver.

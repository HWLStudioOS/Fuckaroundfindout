# Content Review & Strategy Skill

This skill turns raw inputs (transcripts, briefs, ideas, existing content) into strategic, high-performing content outputs. It is built on first-principles frameworks from leading research into attention mechanics, platform algorithms, and content-driven business growth.

Before doing anything, **read the frameworks reference file** at `references/frameworks.md` — it contains the core mental models that should inform every output this skill produces. Without reading it, the outputs will be generic.

## How This Skill Works

This is a multi-disciplinary tool. The input could be anything: a podcast transcript, a client brief, a rough idea, a draft script, an existing post, a content calendar, or a strategic question. The output adapts accordingly.

### Step 1: Identify the Input Type and Objective

Classify what the user has given you and what they need:

| Input | Likely Objective |
|---|---|
| Podcast transcript | Extract clips, ideas, angles, or build a content calendar from it |
| Client brief | Build a content strategy, calendar, or specific deliverables |
| Draft script or post | Review against frameworks, improve hooks/structure/clarity |
| Raw idea or topic | Develop into a full content concept with hook, angle, structure |
| Content calendar request | Build a strategic calendar mapped to business objectives |
| Strategic question | Advise using the frameworks (game selection, audience matching, etc.) |

### Step 2: Establish the Strategic Context

Before creating or reviewing any content, establish these three things. If the user hasn't specified them, infer from context or ask:

1. **Which of the Five Content Games is this for?** (Entertainment Media, Education Media, Commoditized Consumer Product, Consumer Product Requiring Education, B2B Product/Service). This determines everything — idea selection, breadth vs depth, packaging investment, success metrics.

2. **Who is the target audience (ICP)?** The narrower the better. Think "term ownership" — what single term should this brand/person own in people's minds?

3. **What is the conversion objective?** Brand deals (need views) → play wider. Owned offer (need trust) → play narrower and deeper. No offer yet (building skills/brand) → pick a niche and demonstrate expertise.

### Step 3: Apply the Right Frameworks

Refer to `references/frameworks.md` for the detailed models. Here is when to apply each:

**For any content piece (short or long form):**
- **Absorption Rate** — the master KPI. Every word, visual, and frame either maintains or degrades comprehension. Audit sentence by sentence.
- **Hook Framework** (Visual Stop → Context → Contrast) — apply to the first 1-3 seconds of short form or first 5 seconds of long form.
- **Three-Element Alignment** — visual hook, text hook, spoken hook must say the same thing. Misalignment = confusion = churn.
- **Question Chain** — each line should open one clear question in the viewer's mind. The next line answers it and opens a new one. If you can't identify the question, the line is unclear.
- **Comprehension Decay** — comprehension starts at 100% and can only go down. Every confusing moment accelerates decay. When it crosses the threshold, the viewer leaves.

**For content strategy and calendars:**
- **Content Minutes** — trust accrues through accumulated watch time. Map content to the conversion math: how many "blocks" (minutes watched) does a viewer need to convert?
- **Audience Matching** — all content from a brand/channel must serve the same audience so the algorithm can build a consistent sample pool.
- **Seven Lego Bricks** — when planning a content mix, vary Topic and Angle per piece but hold Hook Structure, Story Structure, Visual Format, Key Visuals, and Audio relatively constant (especially for beginners).
- **Content Pillars** — establish 3-4 pillars that all serve the same ICP. Each pillar should be a different angle on the same core expertise.

**For YouTube specifically:**
- **Packaging First** — title and thumbnail are more important than the video itself. Design packaging before scripting.
- **Desire Loop** — thumbnail catches eye → title triggers desire (pain, FOMO, or benefit) → thumbnail amplifies → click.
- **Intro Confirmation** — first 5 seconds must confirm the promise of the title/thumbnail. No bait-and-switch.
- **Two Dopamine Hit Rule** — deliver two genuinely useful/shocking insights before the viewer would naturally churn. After two hits, they're locked in.

**For storytelling and conversion:**
- **Contrast is the engine** — every story beat should set up a common belief and then reveal something unexpected. Use the "shock score" (out of 100 people, how many would have predicted this?).
- **Direct vs Implied Contrast** — direct (state common belief, then flip) creates 2x the contrast distance. Implied (just state the surprising thing) requires the viewer to construct the common belief themselves.
- **Proof and Demonstration** — content that converts shows the creator's ability to get results. It's not about storytelling technique; it's about what you cover and whether it demonstrates competence.

### Step 4: Produce the Output

**When reviewing content:** Go sentence by sentence. For each, ask: Is this clear? Does it maintain absorption? Does it open or answer a question? Is the visual aligned with the words? Flag specific issues with specific fixes, not vague feedback.

**When building a content calendar:** For each piece, specify: Topic, Angle (what's the non-obvious take?), Hook concept (context + contrast), Story Structure (case study, narrative, listicle, breakdown), Visual Format, and the business objective it serves (awareness, trust-building, conversion).

**When extracting from a transcript:** Identify the highest-contrast moments, the insights that would score highest on the shock score. These become your hero clips. For each, draft the hook (all three elements aligned), suggest title text, and note which content game it serves.

**When developing an idea:** Walk through the Seven Lego Bricks one by one. For each brick, identify what the best creators in this niche are doing, what can be held constant, and where there's an opportunity to differentiate.

### Quality Standards

Every piece of content this skill helps create should pass these checks:

- [ ] **Absorption test**: Could a first-time viewer understand 100% of this on one watch?
- [ ] **Hook alignment**: Visual, text, and spoken hooks are saying the same thing
- [ ] **Context before contrast**: The viewer knows what this is about before being surprised
- [ ] **Speed to value**: First dopamine hit arrives within 30 seconds (short form) or 60 seconds (long form)
- [ ] **Question chain**: Every line opens or answers a clear question
- [ ] **Audience match**: This content serves the same ICP as everything else on the channel
- [ ] **Business alignment**: This piece moves someone along the content minutes path toward conversion
- [ ] **Active voice, minimal fluff**: Fewest words possible while maintaining clarity
- [ ] **No bait-and-switch**: The content delivers on whatever the hook promised

### Adapting to Client Context

When working on content for a specific client (e.g., Laing O'Rourke, Creepers Nursery, a podcast), map the client's situation to the Five Content Games first:

- A £5bn construction company doing thought leadership → Game 5 (B2B), playing for trust and category authority
- A luxury horticulture brand → Game 3-4 (Consumer Product), visual storytelling to showcase the product
- A business podcast → Game 2 (Education Media) with potential Game 5 (B2B) conversion play

Then apply all frameworks through that lens. The principles are universal, the application shifts based on the game.

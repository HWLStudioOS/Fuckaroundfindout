## What actually happened

1. **They arrived pre-decided in principle.** Helen's own framing of the agenda: ways of working and month-in-the-life detail "so that we can then make a decision and be like, yes, we're definitely going to do it." Evaluation was over before the call started; this was relationship design.
2. **The cost difference was acknowledged and never objected to.** Helen named it in her opening summary ("there is a cost difference... we'll come back to that") and nobody came back to it as a problem. The July range survived first contact with all three decision-makers.
3. **The £45-50k is a canvas, not a ceiling.** Sarah E's actual question: "what could we do with 45 to 50 grand as a budget... take that number and be like, if that is the exact budget, this is what's possible." That is an invitation to deploy the money they already spend, not a procurement limit. It includes studio, and with Podcast House going to room hire only (Harrison's structure), phase one fits inside it whole.
4. **They designed the expansion themselves.** Helen, unprompted: "the commercial relationship you have with us could grow over time... some of the other elements is a potential additional commercial arrangement." Phase one (podcast, funded by the existing budget), phase two (growth, after 3 to 6 months of data, brings in Helen and Sarah E), phase three (brand, funnel, web, connects to the whole business). Sarah E separately flagged that their web support is an unsustainable favour and that hearing HWL's web capability "is helpful". The ladder to a much bigger account is client-authored.
5. **They are choosing a long-term person, not a supplier.** "It is a really big thing for us. You don't want to make these changes very often." Two-year horizon named. "I don't want you to feel like anything is off the table in terms of suggestions." Sarah M sent the homework within the hour with a warm personal note. Every signal is lean-in.
6. Buying reason, their words: Podcast House is cost-effective but "we have not got the sort of attention and care that would help us to keep improving", plus the unresolved tension between growth tactics and brand authenticity. They are buying the Conor standard plus brand-safe growth judgement, embodied in one person they already trust from BaW S4E1.

## The number and the ladder

**Phase one: £3,400 a month plus VAT, twelve months, review at the end of the first quarter.** The floor held exactly, deployed as the confident answer rather than the retreat. £40.8k a year takes HWL's annualised run rate from ~£132k to ~£173k on its own.

The scale-up Harrison wants (£3.6-5k a month and beyond) lives in the phases they designed, not in stretching phase one past their stated spend:

- **Phase two, growth**, from the first-quarter review (~December): retained growth support built from three months of clean data. Indicative £1,000-1,500 a month on top. They asked "how do we grow" unprompted; the appetite exists, it just wants data first.
- **Phase three, brand and web**: Squiggly Careers is mid-transition to headline brand, their web support is a favour-in-the-evenings arrangement, and Sarah E flagged interest directly. A Squiggly site and brand programme at BaW scale is a £10-20k project lane, plus possible retained web. The BaW S5 launch (15 Sept) is the live portfolio piece, deliberately teased on the call.
- Plausible relationship value by next summer: £5-6.5k a month equivalent. That is the answer to "if it's 3.6 to 5 a month, or if it's more": phase one captures the full existing budget cleanly and fast, the ladder does the rest, and Helen has already said the ladder is additional money.

Trade Harrison could still make: open at £3,600 (~£52-54k all-in with room), a modest stretch past stated spend. Costs the "same budget, redeployed" line, buys £2,400 a year. Recommendation stands at £3,400 for the clean same-day yes and the fastest route to phases two and three, but it is a one-line edit in the draft if he wants the stretch.

	# Do You Hear The Music?

Source: Harrison's old idea, December 2025.
Format: Tier B cinematic essay. 90 to 180 seconds. Built for IG Reels + repurposed for YouTube long-form later.

---

## The concept (as Harrison wrote it)

> Nolan / Oppenheimer "Do you hear the music?" line, cuts to talking about my creative process, very much inspired by the music that I find or know. Even the movie soundtracks. Like this one: phone call from Eternal Sunshine. I feel like this is the song that is being played by the world while it waits for us to get off our phones. So, I guess I wanted to know, do you hear the music?

---

## Why this is a flagship

- Direct map to the **Calder Dune format** (DW63qyOkXMz). Sourced film clip ignites the essay, personal voiceover carries it, lands on a question to the viewer.
- The Eternal Sunshine "Phone Call" piano theme is one of the most evocative pieces of film music ever recorded. Visually pairs with anything still and lit.
- Question close ("do you hear the music?") earns its place per `publishing-rules.md` line 22, it's a real question, not bait.
- The thesis (the world is playing music while we scroll) is the kind of taste-led observation `strategy.md` line 32 names as a core voice ingredient.

## Production spec (proposed)

- **Cold open:** the Oppenheimer "Do you hear the music?" line, with the Ludwig Göransson score swelling. Original clip, fair-use editorial.
- **Cut to:** voiceover begins over your own footage, London streetscape, the Thames, a tube platform, people on phones, late-night cafe.
- **Score throughout:** "Phone Call" from Eternal Sunshine and the Spotless Mind (Jon Brion). License through the publisher if pushed to YouTube; fair-use editorial on IG.
- **Title card mid-piece:** Gruppe A, white. The line "do you hear the music?" as a question on screen for 2 beats.
- **Close:** hold on a static London frame, music tails out, the question on screen.

## The script (first pass, to be developed)

> [Oppenheimer clip: "Do you hear the music?"]
>
> [Cut to your footage. Voiceover starts.]
>
> Christopher Nolan asked that question and the line stuck.
>
> Because there's a song the world is playing.
>
> While we wait for the train. While we wait for the message. While we wait to feel ready.
>
> [Phone Call piano enters]
>
> Most days I don't hear it. I hear the algorithm instead.
>
> But sometimes a film, a piece of music, a particular kind of London light, and it cuts through.
>
> The taste of the people I want to be around. The work I want to make. The version of me I keep almost showing up as.
>
> The music is always playing.
>
> [Title card: "Do you hear it?"]
>
> [Hold on London frame. Music tails.]

## Post

One question I think managers ask too late:

What am I doing that makes your job harder?

We are much more comfortable asking what the team needs from us. It sounds helpful. It also lets us imagine the problem is missing support, not our own behaviour.

Sometimes the blocker is the manager.

A decision kept open for three weeks.

Context held too close.

An urgent message sent every time a thought arrives.

A standard that changes after the work is delivered.

Feedback so vague that the other person has to guess what good looks like.

The difficult part is not asking the question. It is making it safe to answer.

If I ask, then explain every example away, the team learns that the real answer is silence.

I would try this in the next one-to-one:

"Think of one moment in the past month when my behaviour added friction. I'll listen, ask only what I need to understand it, then come back with what I'll change."

Then do exactly that.

Leadership is not only what you add. Sometimes the most useful thing is the obstacle you stop creating.

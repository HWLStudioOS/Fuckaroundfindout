### 01 / THE PULSE

**The Pulse.**

April raised one worry above all others: Spotify had broken its floor. May answered it. Plays are up 29%, consumption hours up 28%, and the platform-specific drop has reversed. At the same time the show recorded its first clean full month on Acast at 929 downloads, and it held that number while running a week short on episodes. This was a strong month that also closed the open question from the last one.

Callout stats:
- **+29%** — Spotify plays vs April (216 → 279). The April floor was transient.
- **1,184** — views on the breakout YouTube short, "Your Office is Lying to You??", at 14.7% CTR.
- **+134** — LinkedIn followers on Cathal's profile, to 4,070.

The honest read up front: the recovery is real and broad, but two things April flagged are still open. Spotify follower growth stayed slow even as plays recovered, and the show missed an episode the week of 4 June. Neither dented the month. Both are worth protecting against.

A note on metrics. Spotify changed its definition of a "play" on 11 June 2026, moving to a 30-second standard. Every Spotify figure in this report sits before that change, so May is a clean like-for-like against April. From next month, plays will be counted differently, and we will flag the discontinuity so the trend is not misread.

### 03 / SPOTIFY, RECOVERED

**The floor came back.**

April's loudest signal was a Spotify-specific drop: 216 plays, below the Season 4 floor. May reversed it.

| Metric | May 2026 | April 2026 | S4 avg (Oct 25–Mar 26) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plays | **279** | 216 | 363 |
| Consumption hours | **70.3** | 54.7 | — |
| Followers (end) | 743 | 739 | — |
| New followers | +4 | +4 | +7 to +12 |

Plays are up 29% and back above the December floor of 249, tracking toward the Season 4 average. Consumption hours rose 28% in step. Both reads from April hold: the drop was real, and it was temporary.

**The data gap from April is closed.** We can now see Spotify show-page impressions: roughly 2,632 over the 30 days Spotify retains (15 May – 13 June). New episodes are surfacing through the Home feed (Leidy Klotz 185 impressions, 139 from Home), and the back catalogue earns its impressions through Search (Tasha Eurich 72, 59 from Search). The integration is working as it should post-migration.

**One honest caveat.** Plays recovered. Followers did not. Net new followers held at +4, the same slow rate April flagged against a typical +7 to +12. People are listening more, but they are not subscribing at the same pace. That is the single clearest thing to fix on Spotify, and section 06 carries an action.

### 04 / WHO'S LISTENING

**For the first time, we can see the room.**

The migration and the LinkedIn analytics together give us an audience picture we did not have in April.

**On Spotify, the core is clear.** The audience skews strongly female and middle-career.
- Gender: 70% female, 22% male.
- Age: 68% are 45–59, a further 20% are 35–44.

So the centre of the audience is women aged 45 to 59. That is a precise, usable profile (sample is the Spotify subset, so treat it as directional rather than the whole audience). It is a strong input for guest selection and for any sponsor conversation.

**On LinkedIn, the audience is senior and professional.** Cathal's followers cluster in Sydney (27%) and London (15%), are Senior, Director or CXO level (around 40% combined), and work in Financial Services, Consulting and Banking. This mirrors the podcast geography and gives the brand a credible B2B audience to point at.

Two different surfaces, two complementary readings: a loyal mid-career listenership on the podcast, a senior professional network on LinkedIn.

### 05 / THE SOCIAL ENGINE

**Three channels, three jobs.**

**YouTube — compounding.** Every headline number rose against the April window.
| Metric | May–Jun | April window |
|---|---|---|
| Views | 2,338 | 2,140 |
| Watch hours | 65.6 | 33.8 |
| Subscribers | +10 | +5 |
| Impressions | 6,068 | 4,729 |
| CTR | 3.18% | 2.41% |

Watch hours and subscribers both roughly doubled. The shorts engine found another breakout: "Your Office is Lying to You??" took 1,184 views at a 14.7% click-through rate, the highest-performing short since "Prisoners are happier". Long-form still does the deep work, with Jennifer Moss (16.3 hours) and the Annette Q&A (8.4 hours) leading on watch time. Shorts win reach, longs win retention. The subscriber conversion is improving but remains the gap to close.

**LinkedIn — the proof of work.** Cathal's profile reached 8,167 people on 21,869 impressions, with 665 engagements at roughly a 3% rate. Followers grew by 134 to 4,070, with 103 of those in May alone, steady growth straight off the ghostwriting. The top post by reach was the 7 May "asking nicely" episode push at 5,203 impressions; the top by engagement was the 5 June season wrap-up at 114. And the newsletter is back: the 2 June "the newsletter is back" post relaunched The Better Bits, closing one of April's open items.

**Instagram — early.** A consistent channel, still small. Across the window: 9,433 views, 1,628 accounts reached, +17 net followers, 409 interactions. The best post was the Season 4 wrap-up at 975 views and 59 likes. The honest read is that 73.5% of views came from existing followers and bio-link taps numbered 4, so Instagram is serving the base rather than finding new people or sending traffic. A discovery lever to test, not a number to lean on yet.

### 06 / THE READ & THE PLAN

**What's working.**
- **The Spotify recovery.** Plays +29%, consumption +28%. April's worry is answered.
- **A clean full month on Acast.** 929 downloads, +31% on baseline, held while a week short on episodes. Resilience, not luck.
- **Every episode landed.** All five new releases cleared 100 downloads; Leidy Klotz (179) and Wendy Smith (165) led.
- **YouTube is compounding.** Watch hours and subs doubled, and the shorts engine found another breakout.
- **The newsletter is back.** The Better Bits relaunched on 2 June.
- **We can finally see the audience.** Women 45–59 on the podcast, senior finance on LinkedIn.

**What needs work.**
- **Spotify follower velocity.** Plays recovered, follower growth did not (+4, against a +7 to +12 norm). The fix: a clear follow prompt in the episode outro and show notes, tied to the strongest episodes.
- **Cadence.** The week of 4 June had no episode. It cost nothing this month, but do not test it twice. Protect the weekly drop.
- **Instagram discovery.** Active but inward-facing (73.5% of views from followers, 4 bio-link taps). One reach lever to trial, likely Reels.
- **YouTube subscriber conversion.** The view volume is not yet turning into subscribers at pace. Add an explicit subscribe ask to the shorts that travel.

**The plan, June–July.**
1. **Roger Martin, recorded 12 June, is the next tentpole.** Treat the launch as a coordinated day: Acast, YouTube long plus shorts, clips, LinkedIn and the newsletter on the same date.
2. **Hold the weekly cadence.** No more skipped weeks through the season close.
3. **Newsletter.** Hold the relaunched cadence and add the signup CTA to every episode description and Cathal's LinkedIn bio.
4. **Spotify follow prompt.** Ship the outro and show-note follow ask this month and read the follower line next report.
5. **YouTube subscribe ask** on the shorts that break out.
6. **Use the audience read.** Let women 45–59 and the senior-finance LinkedIn profile guide guest choices and any sponsor pitch.

**Data notes.**
- Spotify redefined "plays" on 11 June (30-second standard). May is pre-change and clean against April; next month will be flagged.
- YouTube figures here use Views. Engaged views (April's metric) are available on request.
- Spotify demographics are a directional subset, not the whole audience.

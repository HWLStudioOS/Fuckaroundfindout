## Subject

The skill inside the job

## Preview

What a shipyard exoskeleton teaches us about career advantage.

## Draft

Hi,

A naval shipyard is not where I expected to find one of the clearest explanations of how technology changes work.

Before an exoskeleton, a worker had to wield heavy tools for hours. Physical strength was part of the price of entry.

Put the worker in the exoskeleton and the tool becomes almost weightless. The work is safer. Russell says two people become more productive than three were without it. The employer can hire from a much wider talent pool.

The job has not disappeared.

The scarce skill inside it has.

Russell Beck's language for this is blunt: technology democratises work by destroying skills.

That is a more useful way to think about AI than another prediction about how many jobs will be gone by 2030. A tool does not need to remove your role to change its value. It only needs to make one part of it easy enough for everyone.

Russell puts the capabilities that grow in value into three buckets:

1. **Creativity.** Seeing the hidden meaning, making connections and turning information into a story that helps people move.
2. **Self-efficacy.** Continuing to learn, adapt and take responsibility for your own development.
3. **Leadership.** Helping other people think, choose and do good work as the environment changes.

Here is a ten-minute exercise for this week.

Write down the five tasks that take most of your time. Circle any that AI or automation has made materially easier in the past year. Then ask two questions:

- Which of those tasks used to make me valuable?
- What does the organisation need from me now that the task is easier?

The answer might be better judgement. A clearer point of view. More useful questions. Stronger relationships. The ability to make the work land with other people.

That is where the advantage moves next.

We cut Russell's full conversation into one eight-minute Better Moment. It includes the shipyard story, the three capabilities and the question he thinks we do not make enough time to answer.

# The dialled 2026 funnel for HWL Studio: build a world, not a triangle

This advances the May Klement research. That doc's conclusion stands and is correct: steal the mobile-first funnel mechanics, refuse the €250k/mo paid-ads playbook because it breaks premium positioning. This doc answers the next question, the one you're actually asking now that content is kicking off: given a world with many entry edges, what's the conversion architecture, and what do I build first.

## Your frame is right, and the data backs it

"You don't build a funnel, you build a world. People enter from any edge. Everything points back at you. Attention is the only currency." That isn't a vibe, it's the documented reality of how serious organisations buy in 2026:

- 70-80% of a B2B purchase is complete before the buyer ever contacts the vendor. The decision happens in the dark, private DMs, WhatsApp and Slack groups, peer referrals, a podcast on a commute, a saved reel. None of it shows in your analytics.
- Attribution is therefore dead. With 27+ touchpoints over a 6-12 month cycle, "which post converted" is unanswerable. The job shifts from chasing form-fills to building brand memory.
- The winnable game has a name: mental availability. Be the studio that pops into a brand lead's head the instant they realise they need film. You can't control the entry point and you can't measure the path, so you optimise for one thing: being unforgettable, and being easy to act on once someone decides.

So "world not funnel" has a concrete design consequence. Many valid entry edges (your five platforms), one shared destination that everything points back to, and one clear door to walk through when ready. The discipline isn't the entry, it's the centre and the door.

## The architecture, in one sentence

A world with one owned centre (the newsletter), many entry edges (the five platforms), where every edge demonstrates craft plus a named point of view, the bottom-of-funnel proof assets are case-study films, the only call to action is an application gate, and outreach converts warm because the buyer already lives in the world.

That's the Klement mobile-funnel mechanic kept (audit/quiz as the bio destination, booking in-funnel, organic-to-paid validation) but re-pointed at a world and stripped of the aggressive paid layer.

## The path, mapped (it's not a tunnel)

The buyer self-navigates; you place strong assets at each altitude and let them find their own way down.

```
ENTRY (any edge)     reel / LinkedIn post / Substack essay / podcast / referral
   the hook is the WORK; the POV is the reason to follow
CAPTURE THE FOLLOW   one owned destination: the newsletter
   email is the highest-converting channel for this kind of service; the only edge you own
DEEPEN (the 95%)     weekly POV + case-study films; a preview of what working with you feels like
   buyer accumulates mental availability over weeks
SELF-IDENTIFY        bio destination = the mobile audit funnel OR "apply to work with the studio"
QUALIFY (taste+fit)  6-8 question application: scope, timeline, budget band, outcome
   filters out, signals scarcity, arrives pre-sold
DISCOVERY (warm)     not a pitch, a scoping conversation; they already trust the POV
PROPOSAL             existing HWL proposal deck + day-rate anchors (£950 location / £550 edit)
CLIENT
```

The highest-leverage asset at each altitude:

| Altitude | The asset | Why it's the leverage point |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | The work, carrying a visible POV | Hook is craft; reason to stay is perspective |
| Capture | The owned newsletter | The only edge you own; highest-converting channel |
| Deepen | Case-study films | +80% conversion, 3-5x for high-ticket, doubles as portfolio |
| Self-identify | One named framework / POV | The defensible idea that makes buyers self-select |
| Qualify | Application gate | Status signal and scarcity proof; replaces "free call" |
| Convert | Proposal deck + day rates | You already have these |

## The premium divergence (don't skip this)

There's a real tension in the research worth surfacing rather than papering over. A strong contrarian camp argues inbound-only is a trap: the biggest budgets are the busiest people, they don't browse, they respond to relevant outreach. Don't dismiss that. It resolves cleanly for you: content creates the demand and the warmth, targeted relationship-led outreach captures it. The point is not "never reach out." It's that your outreach lands warm because the buyer has already met the world. Cold "book a free call" outreach is what kills premium positioning. Warm, specific, taste-led outreach to a named brand lead who already follows the work does not.

The anti-funnel moves that fit a scarcity brand:

- The application gate is a status signal, not a filter. Serious buyers expect a 6-8 question scoping form; it says your time is valuable. You don't book Harrison, you apply to work with the studio.
- Qualify out loudly. Scarcity is only believable if some people are turned away. A true capacity statement ("the studio takes 3-4 retained partners at a time") is more credible than a fake countdown, and at £11k/mo it's actually true.
- One named POV. Thought leadership without a distinctive perspective is just content marketing. A defensible point of view is what drops price resistance, buyers pay for established authority, not a service. Your "build a world not a funnel" thesis is itself a candidate POV. You already own it.

## The single most important asset: case-study films

This is the strongest recommendation in the whole night's work. For you specifically, the proof asset IS the product. A 2-minute film on the Laing O'Rourke or Creepers work is, at the same time: your portfolio, a demonstration of your POV, and the highest-converting bottom-of-funnel asset that exists (video case studies lift conversion ~80%, and high-ticket case studies that lead with the client's painful problem convert 3-5x better than generic ones). Nobody else in your space can make these the way you can. If you do one thing in the next 60 days, make the Creepers and LOR films. Note the lovely overlap: David is now signed and his first jobs are exactly this kind of edit. The proof engine and the delivery engine are the same engine.

## The two-audience problem (the part most worth your attention)

You have two audiences you must not muddy: BUYERS (brand and comms leaders at serious organisations who hire the studio) and PEERS (content people who want your craft and AI-systems knowledge, and are a future course/community audience). If they blend in one feed, a Laing O'Rourke director sees "AI content tips for creators" and downgrades you from trusted studio to guy who teaches Instagram. That is your H8 status risk, externalised onto the funnel. Tonight's workshop is the live version of this tension: a peer-room performance that you correctly keep separate from the buyer-room posture.

The resolution, proven by operators like Colin & Samir: same world, separated by surface and by product, never muddied in the same stream.

- BUYER track lives on LinkedIn + the case-study films + the studio-POV thread of the newsletter. Surface: how serious organisations should think about story, craft, and content as a business asset. Product behind it: the £10k+ engagement. Peers may watch; buyers are who it's for.
- PEER track lives on IG / TikTok / YouTube + a separate content thread (and eventually a separate list segment). Surface: how you run content and AI systems. Product behind it: a future productised course or community, explicitly a later, gated top-layer.

Three rules to stop cannibalisation:
1. Separate the surface, not the soul. Same voice, same world. Buyer-grade craft goes where buyers are; teaching content goes where peers are.
2. Sequence them. Buyer track is the v1 priority, it pays the £150-200k goal now. The peer/course track is deliberately later, a likely 2027 surface. Launching it early IS the status risk. This lines up with H8 rather than fighting it.
3. Let the peer audience be social proof for buyers, not a competing pitch. A buyer seeing you respected among professionals increases your authority. The danger is only when teaching content outvolumes proof-of-craft content in a buyer's eyeline. Keep buyer surfaces craft-and-proof dominant.

## What to measure, what to ignore

Measure: newsletter subscriber growth and open rate (the owned-audience leading indicator), number of qualified applications, and the qualitative "they already knew me" signal on discovery calls (a shortening sales cycle is the real proof the world is working). Ignore: last-touch attribution and per-post conversion. The journey is 70-80% invisible; chasing attribution is the named dead-end. Track brand memory ("I saw your Arsenal thing"), not click-paths.

## 30 / 60 / 90 day build

Days 0-30, centre the world (cheap, fast, all leverage): write and publish the signature POV piece. Stand up the newsletter as the one destination. Re-point every bio to it. Replace any "call" CTA with the application gate. Start the hub-and-spoke rhythm (one anchor a month, 5-7 spokes a week) from existing footage.

Days 31-60, build the proof (the bigger build): produce the first case-study film (Creepers or LOR, route to David). Establish the buyer track on LinkedIn as distinct from the peer track on short-form. Wire the mobile audit funnel as the bio destination feeding the application gate.

Days 61-90, close the loop: second case-study film. First warm-outreach batch to named brand leads who already follow the world (capturing demand you created). Review qualified-application count and sales-cycle compression. Decide whether the world has enough gravity to later open the peer/course layer (probably 2027, not now).

## The dependency nobody can skip: fix the website first

This funnel has a broken last inch, and it's the website. Every entry edge in this architecture is supposed to resolve to one destination and one door. Right now your IG bio points to hwlstudio.com, where every call to action, including "Get in Touch", is a dead button with no contact form, no email, no booking link (full findings in `spec/website-audit-2026-06-09.md`). You can build the most elegant world in the world, but if the door doesn't open, the people you've drawn in bounce. Before you drive content traffic anywhere, make one contact path work. That is the cheapest, highest-return move on this entire list, and it gates everything else.

## Bottom line

The frame is right and the research confirms it. The build order: fix the website door (now), centre the world on an owned newsletter with one named POV (weeks), then build the case-study films that are simultaneously your proof, portfolio, and highest-converting asset (months, route to David). Keep the buyer track and peer track on separate surfaces, sequence buyer-first, and let warmth, not ad spend, do the converting. That protects the scarcity positioning while finally giving the content surge somewhere to land.

**Race:** Race to the Stones 50K, Lewknor → Avebury, ~720m D+, Sat 11 Jul 2026

## 1. Headline diagnosis

The athlete data tells a different story than the existing plan assumes. Read carefully before reading the prescription.

**What the data actually says:**

1. **The chassis is the constraint, not the engine.** A 1:45 half at 5:00/km on ~30km/wk base means aerobic fitness is well ahead of structural durability. Recurrent ITB and patellar tendinopathy are not historical, they are the live ceiling on every decision. If the knees go yellow, the engine becomes irrelevant.

2. **The Birthday Run was a successful stress test, not a baseline.** 26km at 6:19/km, HR avg 164 (~Z3 low for a ~190 max), max 186, clean execution, no flags after. That's a useful data point: he can hold conversational-effort 6:20s for 2h45m on current training. It is NOT a green light to assume he can hold 8:00/km for 50km in seven weeks.

3. **The W2 undershoot was correct and should not be treated as a deficit.** 20km vs 30km plan with 10.3km long run vs 16km target was a deliberate, knee-protective response to a 30%+ single-day load spike (26km Birthday). That is good athletic decision-making. It should be reinforced as a pattern, not "made up for" in W3.

4. **Strength adherence is the single biggest injury risk.** 3 of 8-10 prescribed sessions in 30 days = 30% adherence. With his knee history this is the math that decides whether he toes the line in July healthy. Any plan that prescribes 3 strength sessions/week is fantasy. Two real sessions beat three theoretical ones.

5. **Time, not motivation, is the binding constraint.** 6-10 hr/week for running plus strength, with a £11k/m business and a 62-day public writing block. The plan must fit a real life with a real Maya, not an athlete-monk ideal.

6. **HRV is healthy, sleep is healthy.** 7.2h sleep, 82 score, baseline ~60ms HRV. No systemic recovery problem. The 38ms reading is alcohol, not fatigue. He has the recovery capacity to handle a sensible build.

7. **The 75's macro architecture is roughly right but its peak is too greedy.** 53-58 km in W8-10 with a 30km race rehearsal at W10 is reasonable on paper. In practice, with 30% strength adherence, a knee history, and a Tuesday medium-long stacked on a Wednesday tempo, it's a recipe for the same ITB flare he's been managing for years.

**What to design around:**

- **Durability before fitness.** Every session has to pass the question: does this make his knees more or less likely to hold for 7h30m on the Ridgeway?
- **Aerobic surplus, intensity restraint.** He doesn't need more VO2max to run 7:30/km. He needs more time on feet at conversational effort.
- **Strength as non-negotiable infrastructure.** Two sessions a week, ring-fenced like a client meeting, or the wheels come off.
- **Sustainable cadence over heroic peaks.** A 50km peak that he actually executes beats a 58km peak that breaks him in W9.
- **The Ridgeway-specific demand.** Mostly runnable, 720m D+ across 50km = ~14m/km average gain. That's rolling, not mountainous. Specificity is hill repeats and time-on-feet, not vert hunting.

## 2. Five governing principles

**Principle 1: Chassis-first.** Every week starts with the question "is the knee green, yellow, or red?" Green = run the plan. Yellow = swap the hardest session for cross-train, hold long run. Red = no running for 72h, physio call, walking only. The plan is hierarchically subordinate to the chassis.

**Principle 2: Aerobic surplus, intensity scarcity.** ~85% of weekly volume in Z1-Z2 (HR <155). Threshold/tempo work limited to one session per week, max 25 min total work. No VO2max intervals, ever. He doesn't need them for a 7h30m race, and they're the highest injury-cost session per fitness gain.

**Principle 3: Two strength sessions a week, ring-fenced.** Mon AM and Thu AM. 35 min each. If only one happens, it must be Thu (post-deload, pre-long-run). Strength is not a "bonus" session. It's the load-bearing wall.

**Principle 4: Long run is the king session.** Built progressively but never more than 30% of weekly volume, never two consecutive +3km long-run jumps. The long run carries the race-day specificity: fueling, kit, pacing, time-on-feet. Everything else feeds it.

**Principle 5: Sustainability over optimisation.** A 7-hour week he completes beats a 9-hour week he half-does. The plan is sized to fit Tue/Wed/Fri AM runs at 45-70 min, Sat long run, two strength windows. If he wants to add a Sunday Z2 spin, that's bonus, not required.

## 3. Weekly architecture (the rhythm)

**Standard week, 7 days, 6-9h depending on phase:**

| Day | AM | PM (optional) | Why |
|-----|----|----|-----|
| Mon | Strength A (35 min) + 30 min easy run |, | Knee armour + flush. Easy run is short and slow, primes the system without taxing it. |
| Tue | Quality session (45-60 min) |, | The week's only real intensity. Tempo, hills, or progression. Fully recovered from weekend. |
| Wed | Easy run (45-60 min, Z1-Z2) |, | Recovery aerobic. Conversational. |
| Thu | Strength B (35 min) + 20 min easy or rest |, | Second strength session. Pre-long-run loading. |
| Fri | Easy run (40 min) + 4x20s strides |, | Light, sharp, primes Saturday. |
| Sat | Long run (progressive) |, | The session. Fueling, kit, pacing rehearsal. |
| Sun | Rest or 45-60 min cross-train (cycle/incline walk) |, | True off day or low-impact aerobic. Knee-dependent. |

**Why this shape, not something else:**

- **No Sat-Sun back-to-back long runs.** The traditional ultra B2B (long Sat + medium Sun) is a 10+ hr/week structure for athletes with bulletproof chassis. With his knee history, a Sunday medium run on tired legs is the single highest-probability injury session of the week. Skipping it costs ~10% of weekly time on feet at the price of dramatically lower injury risk. Worth it.

- **No Tuesday medium-long.** This is the Jack Daniels / Pfitzinger move and it works for marathon athletes with deep volume bases. For a 6-10 hr/week athlete with a knee history, stacking a 75-min Tuesday with a Wednesday quality session is pinning the back-to-back risk to mid-week. The Tuesday medium-long earns its place when weekly volume is consistently >60km. He won't be there.

- **Quality on Tuesday, not Wednesday.** Better recovered from the Sunday rest, leaves Wednesday as a true easy day buffering toward Saturday. Wednesday tempo on Tuesday strength legs is too much load too close.

- **Two strength sessions, separated by 72h.** Mon + Thu spacing protects each session and fits the run schedule. Mon strength on fresh legs (good for compound work), Thu strength as priming (lighter, knee-focused).

- **Friday strides, not rest.** 4x20s strides at 5K pace are the cheapest neuromuscular insurance you can buy. They keep the legs sharp through low-volume taper weeks and prep Saturday's quality.

**Time budget per week:**

- Mon: 65 min
- Tue: 50 min
- Wed: 50 min
- Thu: 45 min
- Fri: 45 min
- Sat: 90-240 min (progresses)
- Sun: 0-60 min
- **Total: 5h45m (recovery week) to 9h00m (peak week)**

## 4. Strength block (the load-bearing wall)

**The adherence problem, named directly:** 30% adherence on a 3x/week prescription is not a discipline failure, it's a design failure. Three sessions of 45 min each = 2h15m of gym work in a week where he also has 7 hours of running and a business. The friction beats him.

**The fix:** Two sessions, 35 min each, AM, ring-fenced like client meetings. Both sessions live in the calendar, recurring, with the same start time. If a session is missed, it is gone for the week, not "rescheduled" into Wed and then skipped because Wed is already a run day.

**Minimum effective dose for knee survival in a 50K:**

**Strength A, Monday AM, 35 min ("the heavy day")**

Goblet squat 3x8 (45 sec rest)
Single-leg RDL 3x6 each leg (slow, 3sec eccentric)
Bulgarian split squat 3x6 each leg (bodyweight or light DB)
Hip thrust 3x10
Pallof press 3x10 each side

Rationale: Bilateral and unilateral knee/hip loading. Goblet squat over back squat because barbell setup adds friction at home and the weight isn't the limiter. Split squat is the highest-yield single exercise for ITB/patellar resilience. Hip thrust loads glutes that are otherwise underdeveloped in runners and chronically weak after sitting. Pallof for trunk stability under unilateral load.

**Strength B, Thursday AM, 35 min ("the knee-armour day")**

Diana S1 (fire hydrant 3x10, seated hip IR 3x10, SL sit-to-stand 3x5 with 3-4s eccentric, terminal knee extension 3x8, SL leg press or wall sit 3x10)
Plus:
Single-leg calf raise 3x12
Nordic hamstring curl 3x4-5 with 5sec eccentric (bands or partner)
Side plank with leg lift 3x10 each side

Rationale: This is Diana's prescribed knee-armour work, condensed and front-loaded. Nordic curls are the single best hamstring injury prevention exercise in the literature. Calf raises directly load the calf-Achilles-plantar chain that takes the most pounding on Ridgeway flint. Side plank with leg lift is the cheapest glute medius activation that exists.

**The non-negotiable:** Both sessions are AM, before he opens email. If either session falls through, it is replaced same-week ONLY if the schedule has a clear gap (e.g., a missed Wednesday run becomes a Wednesday strength makeup). Otherwise, gone. No catch-up sessions on Sat/Sun.

**The honest line:** Two sessions a week of 35 min is not "optimal." It's adequate. 90% of injury-prevention benefit comes from the first 60 min of strength work per week if the exercise selection is right. The gap between 70 min and 120 min of weekly strength is the smallest part of the curve. What kills runners is going from 0 min to 0 min, not from 90 min to 70 min.

## 6. KPIs and gates per phase

**Per week, automatic check:**

| KPI | Green | Yellow | Red |
|-----|-------|--------|-----|
| Knee pain (0-10) post-long-run | 0-1 | 2-3, gone in 24h | 4+ or persists 48h+ |
| Resting HR vs baseline | ±3 bpm | +4-7 bpm for 2 days | +8 bpm for 3+ days |
| HRV 7d avg | Within 5ms of baseline | Down 6-10ms | Down 11+ms (excluding alcohol) |
| Sleep | 7h+ for 5+ nights | 6-7h for 3+ nights | <6h for 3+ nights |
| Subjective energy on Tue quality | "Fine, sharp" | "Heavy, doable" | "Empty, dread" |
| Strength sessions completed | 2/2 | 1/2 | 0/2 |

**Trigger rules:**

- **2 yellows in a week:** Drop the next planned long run by 25% or convert the next quality session to easy.
- **1 red:** Skip the next quality session. Long run becomes easy effort only. Reassess in 72h.
- **2 reds:** No running for 72h. Diana S2 only. Physio call. Reassess.
- **Strength 0/2:** The following week, strength sessions move to first thing in the day on Mon and Thu, no exceptions, even if it means cutting Tuesday tempo to 30 min.

**Phase gates (binary go/no-go decisions):**

- **End of W3 (May 31):** BLED CHECK if relevant. For RTTS specifically: knees green = proceed to deload + peak phase. Knees yellow or worse = repeat W3 instead of advancing, push everything back one week, race becomes "complete the distance" not "sub 7:30."
- **End of W6 (Jun 21):** Did the 30km race rehearsal go cleanly? If yes, sub-7:30 is on. If no, race goal becomes "finish strong, sub-8h."
- **End of W8 (Jul 5):** Anything resembling injury here = race rehearsal (W6) becomes the de facto effort target. No heroic recoveries in 6 days.

## 7. Race day plan (Saturday 11 July 2026)

### Pre-race (3-2-1)

**3 days out (Wed Jul 8):** Carb-loading begins gently. +50g carbs at dinner. Hydration up.
**2 days out (Thu Jul 9):** +75g carbs at lunch and dinner. Caffeine taper begins (drop to 1 cup AM).
**1 day out (Fri Jul 10):** +100g carbs across the day. 500mg sodium pre-load with dinner. Race kit laid out. Asleep by 22:00.

### Race morning

- Wake 04:00 (race start ~07:00 typical for RTTS, confirm)
- 3h pre-start: oats + banana + honey + coffee, ~80g carbs
- 2h pre: 500ml water + 250mg sodium
- 60 min pre: 30g carbs (gel or banana)
- 15 min pre: 100mg caffeine (gel or tab)

### Kit (race day)

- Race vest (1.5L capacity), 2 soft flasks (500ml each)
- Race shoes (whatever was used for Birthday Run + Sat Jun 20 rehearsal, DO NOT race in untested shoes)
- Anti-chafe (Body Glide) on inner thighs, nipples, under sports bra strap line if relevant
- Cap, sunglasses, sun cream pre-applied
- Phone (for emergency, music if needed late race), small first aid (2 plasters, salt tabs)
- Spare socks in drop bag at midway aid (Goring, ~25km)

### Pacing strategy (the sub-7:30 plan)

**The race breaks into four chunks. Treat them differently.**

**Chunk 1: km 0-12 (Lewknor to Watlington, gentle climb out)**, Target 7:50-8:00/km. *Slower than goal pace.* The first hour is the most expensive km of the day. Anything saved here pays double in the back half. HR cap: 155.

**Chunk 2: km 12-25 (rolling Ridgeway to Goring midway)**, Target 7:30-7:45/km. Settle into goal pace. Walk every climb >5%. Eat every 25-30 min, drink every 15 min. HR cap: 160.

**Chunk 3: km 25-38 (Goring to Bury Down area, the test)**, Target 7:45-8:00/km. This is where 50K races are won or lost. Keep effort steady, accept slower pace. Walk all climbs without negotiation. Salt tab at km 30. HR cap: 162.

**Chunk 4: km 38-50 (the finish, mostly downhill into Avebury)**, Target 7:30-8:00/km depending on legs. If legs are there, gentle progression. If they're not, hold and grind. Last 2km, run hard regardless.

**Time math at 7:45/km average: 6h27m.** Comfortably under 7:30. Even with 30 min of total walking on climbs and aid stations, finish is ~6h57m. Sub-7:30 has 33 min of cushion. That's the right number for a first ultra.

### Fueling on race

- 50g carbs/hr from km 5 onwards (one gel = 25g, so a gel every 30 min)
- 500ml water/hr in temperate UK summer, 750ml/hr if warm (>22C)
- 250mg sodium every 60 min (electrolyte tab in second flask)
- Salt tab (additional 200mg) every 90 min
- Caffeine: 100mg at km 25, 100mg at km 40

### Heat plan

UK July can deliver anything from 15C to 32C. Strategy by temp:

- **<18C:** Race plan as above
- **18-22C:** Slow pace 10-15 sec/km from km 20 onwards. Add 250ml/hr water.
- **22-26C:** Slow pace 20-30 sec/km. Drop time goal to sub-8h. Wet cap at every aid. Walk all uphill.
- **>26C:** Survival mode. Goal becomes finish, not time. Walk-run from km 25. Risk of hyponatremia rises if drinking heavily; salt tab every 60 min.

### Contingency

- **Knee niggle in first 20km:** Walk it out for 5 min. If it resolves, continue cautiously. If it returns within 5km, race is over. DNF beats 12 weeks of rehab.
- **GI problem (gut shutdown):** Stop fueling for 30 min, sip water only. Restart with half-doses. If still bad after 60 min, switch to coke at aids only.
- **Bonk (km 30+):** Walk 5 min, eat 60g carbs, salt tab, restart at 8:30/km until energy returns.
- **Cramps:** Salt tab, 5 min walk. Pickle juice if available at aid.

## 8. Heat acclimatisation

UK June is unreliable. UK July is unreliable. He cannot count on natural heat exposure, so heat acclimatisation must be *induced*.

**When to start:** Week 6 (Jun 15 onwards). Earlier is wasted, later is too rushed. The acclimatisation protocol takes 10-14 days to confer ~70% of benefit.

**How to do it (cheap and effective):**

- **Post-run sauna sessions, 3x/week from W6:** 15-20 min at 80-90C, immediately after a normal training run. Don't shower first. This is the protocol with the strongest evidence (Stanford/Heat Lab work). Hydrate post (500ml water + 250mg sodium).
- **Alternative if no sauna access: hot bath protocol.** 40-minute bath at 40C, body submerged to neck. Less efficient than sauna but works.
- **One run per week in maximum clothing layers:** Wed easy run, base layer + long sleeve + light jacket. Just for thermal stress, not heat survival.
- **Do NOT race heat-acclimatise in race week.** No sauna after Wednesday Jul 8. The thermal stress doesn't recover fast enough.

**The honest line:** He's racing in UK July, not Death Valley. If race day is 16C and overcast, all this work is wasted. But you can't predict UK weather, so you prepare for the worst case where benefit is real.

## 9. HRV-guided modulation rules

**The principle:** HRV is signal, not gospel. Use it as one of three inputs (HRV, subjective feel, knee state). Never bail on a session based on HRV alone.

**Daily rules:**

- **HRV within 5ms of 7d baseline:** Run the planned session.
- **HRV down 6-10ms with NO context (no late night, no alcohol, no late meal, no stress event):** Run easy, replace quality session with Z2 if needed.
- **HRV down 11+ms with NO context for 2+ days:** Skip the next quality session entirely. Easy run only. Reassess.
- **HRV crashed but obvious context (alcohol, late meal, illness onset, work stress):** Note the context, ignore the number, run the planned session unless subjective feel is also off.

**The alcohol rule, named explicitly:** A 38ms reading the morning after 6-7 beers is not a fatigue signal. It's an alcohol signal. The body's HRV recovers from alcohol within 24-48h regardless of training state. Do NOT cancel a Saturday long run because Friday-night-pints-Saturday-morning-HRV was low. If subjective feel is fine on the warm-up, send the session. If you bonk by km 8, that's evidence and you can stop early.

**The two-day rule:** A single low-HRV day means nothing. A two-day downward trend (no obvious context) means something. A three-day trend means stop and reassess.

**The override:** Knee yellow flag overrides any HRV reading, in either direction. Even with perfect HRV, a yellow knee = downgrade the session.

## 11. Knee management protocols (yellow/red flag interactions)

**The diagnostic moment:** The first 10 minutes of any run. Pain that fades by km 2 = warmup tightness, run on. Pain that builds through km 2-5 = yellow flag. Pain that limits stride or compensation gait = red flag, stop now.

**Yellow protocol:**

1. Finish current run at reduced pace if pain doesn't worsen. Cut session to 60-70% planned distance.
2. Same day: 15 min ice on patellar tendon or ITB origin point. Diana S1 abbreviated (fire hydrants, hip IR, glute bridges). No NSAIDs unless physio approves.
3. Next planned run: replace with 30 min cycling or incline walk. No running.
4. Following day: test with 20 min very easy jog. If pain returns within 10 min, escalate to red.
5. If clear after 48h, return to normal week but cut next long run by 25%.

**Red protocol:**

1. Stop running immediately. Walk home if mid-run.
2. Next 72h: no running. Walking, swimming, easy cycling only.
3. Diana S2 only (knee strength), no S1 (no plyometric or eccentric load).
4. Physio call within 48h. Honest description of pain location, onset, behaviour.
5. Return to running protocol: 20 min easy → 30 min easy → 45 min easy across 3 days, with 24h between each. No quality work for 7 days.
6. If red flag occurs in W7+ (taper), the race goal automatically downgrades. No heroic comebacks in 3 weeks.

**The pre-emptive rule:** Any week with cumulative knee discomfort >3 sessions, downgrade the next long run by 25% even if all sessions were green individually. The chassis is telling you it's loaded.

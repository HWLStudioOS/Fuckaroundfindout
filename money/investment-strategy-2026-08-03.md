## The hard separation

### Company money

Company receipts pass through this order:

1. Match the receipt to its invoice and identify whether the figure includes VAT.
2. Move the exact VAT line to a named VAT pot. If an invoice is VAT-inclusive, the VAT element is one sixth of gross.
3. Fund the Corporation Tax provision. Until Litchfields produces a current profit-based number, keep the existing 25% control and do not assume it also covers VAT.
4. Fund PAYE and NIC due.
5. Restore the minimum operating floor. Set it at the greater of £5,000 or the next four weeks of committed company outflows, excluding separately reserved taxes and discretionary dividends.
6. Pay any business card statement that is due. Interest-bearing debt beats a larger idle buffer and any investment contribution.
7. Build the resilience floor toward the next 13 weeks of committed company outflows before an extra dividend or employer pension top-up.
8. Only then identify retained profit that can legally support salary, a dividend or an employer pension contribution.

Dividends require post-tax distributable profit and formal records. An invoice landing does not itself create a dividend. Do not use an informal director's loan to reach the investment accounts.

HWL savings and any employer pension contribution are funded only from an HWL Studio bank account. Personal Kraken, Trading 212 and Vanguard accounts are funded only from a same-name personal bank account after lawful extraction.

### Personal money

Personal cash then passes through this order:

1. Keep the next 30 days of household bills and rent available.
2. Pay personal card statement balances in full, unless a balance is genuinely at 0% and has a dated repayment plan.
3. Set aside personal tax due on dividends.
4. Make the month's £50,000 glidepath transfer.
5. Invest only the remaining long-term surplus.

The company tax reserve, company operating cash, pensions, shares, crypto, unused credit and cash economically belonging to Maya do not count toward the £50,000 cash target.

## The £50,000 target

Let:

- **P0** be Harrison's starting eligible cash. This is fixed once on day one.
- **C** be current eligible cash. It includes designated personal deposits and Harrison's agreed share of joint deposits, less personal tax, bills due within 30 days and any cash economically belonging to Maya.
- **N** be the number of scheduled monthly transfers remaining.
- **M** be the minimum monthly cash transfer.
- **S** be verified after-tax personal surplus for the month after bills, debt payments and personal tax.
- **G** be long-term investable surplus for the month: `max(0, S - M)`.

If G is zero, every investment order is cancelled or deferred. The calendar never overrides that result.

The starting rule is:

`M = (£50,000 - P0) / 12`

Expected company receipts do not enter C until they have landed and been lawfully extracted. At each later review use: `M = (£50,000 - C) / N`.

The old June programme aimed for about £3,000 of real monthly saving. On that rate, P0 needs to be at least £14,000. If P0 is lower, the gap must come from higher revenue, lower spending or a later target date. It cannot defensibly come from assumed investment returns.

At a 4.2% savings rate, £50,000 present for a full year earns about £2,100 gross. A balance built evenly from zero earns roughly half that in year one. Interest helps. It does not solve the target.

### Monthly glidepath

On the third working day of each month:

1. Record actual eligible cash.
2. Calculate the straight-line target: `P0 + ((£50,000 - P0) x elapsed months / 12)`.
3. Transfer enough to meet it.
4. If the target cannot be met, pause every investment contribution.
5. If the gap reaches £2,500 or one full M, whichever comes first, reset the spending and revenue plan.
6. Resume investing only after C fully meets the scheduled target and G is positive.

**Green** means every kill switch in this brief is false, C fully meets the scheduled cash target, and the current month has a verified positive G. Anything else is red.

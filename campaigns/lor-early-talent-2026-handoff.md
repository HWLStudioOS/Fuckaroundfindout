## Strategy work that needs doing

Three phases.

### Phase 0 — Diagnose

Pull together the data above. Map the actual funnel: top (awareness, content reach) → middle (consideration, landing page, register-interest) → bottom (application submitted) → bottom-of-bottom (hire). Identify where the leak is. AS's leak was at the recruitment-handoff layer. Early Talent's leak is unknown until data is in.

### Phase 1 — Research

Three research tracks. Use web search liberally — this is where claude.ai earns its keep over the local environment.

1. **Competitor scan.** Skanska, Mace, Balfour Beatty, Multiplex, Mott MacDonald, Bouygues, Costain, ISG. UK Tier 1 + Tier 2 contractors who recruit early talent. Who is winning, what formats, what hooks, what channels, what does their LinkedIn early-talent content look like, what does their TikTok presence look like (if any), what does their grad-cracker / rate-my-apprenticeship presence look like? Anything that looks asymmetric — a contractor doing something nobody else is — is worth flagging.
2. **Channel reality.** Where do UK 16–22 year olds actually consume career content in 2026? Look at: TikTok career hashtags (#GraduateJobs #ApprenticeshipUK #ConstructionCareers), LinkedIn early-talent groups, Reddit (r/UKJobs, r/cscareerquestionsEU equivalent), university careers service patterns, Discord communities, YouTube long-form vs Shorts. Pressure-test Emma's "no TikTok" decision. She said no based on a thin model; if the data supports re-introducing it, surface it.
3. **Format research.** Which Day-in-the-Life patterns actually convert (not just rack up views)? Which hook structures work on LinkedIn for graduate recruitment specifically? Which CTA structures drive application clicks vs vanity engagement? Pull 8-12 worked examples and annotate why each works.

### Phase 2 — Strategy

Synthesise into a strategy doc with these sections:

1. **Personas.** At minimum: 16–18 school leaver weighing apprenticeship vs A-levels; 18–22 university final-year weighing grad schemes; 18–22 already in trades looking to upskill into engineering / supervisor. Real personas with jobs-to-be-done, not "early talent" lumped. Extend the AS demographic-differentiated muscle here.
2. **Messaging architecture.** Per persona. What does this persona need to hear, at what funnel stage, in what tone? Anchored to LOR's Quiet Confidence framework.
3. **Channel mix.** Mapped to where each persona lives. Owned vs paid mix. Reach + frequency assumptions.
4. **Funnel mechanics.** Awareness → consideration → conversion. Where the recruitment handoff has historically broken, what HWL can control vs what LOR-internal recruitment needs to fix in parallel.
5. **Asymmetric bets.** One or two things HWL would do that no UK Tier 1 contractor is currently doing. The high-upside differentiators. This is where the work earns its strategic-comms-advisor positioning.
6. **Measurement plan.** What counts as success per persona, per channel, per phase. How to attribute. What to instrument before Day 1.
7. **Risks + dependencies.** Including the recruitment-handoff scar from AS.

## Voice constraints (non-negotiable)

These apply to every deliverable, every slide, every line of copy:

- **No em dashes.** Ever. Commas or full stops. Cmd+F your output before delivering. This is the single most-violated rule.
- **No engagement bait. No motivational fluff. No AI slop.**
- **Specific over vague.** Named people, real numbers, real dates.
- **British-inflected Kiwi.** Short sentences. Quiet confidence.
- **McKinsey-grade output** for LOR specifically. Boardroom-ready. Helen Fraser directive: "raw and real, not box-ticking celebration content."
- **Demographic-differentiated.** A 22-year-old apprentice and a 50-year-old project director don't read the same way. Same goes for a school leaver vs a uni grad vs a tradesperson looking up.
- **Don't dismiss inspo as slop.** When Harrison shares reference content, isolate the structural device (hook / format / closer / pacing), don't react to the surface message.

## What Q2 actually said

Released after US close, 4 August. First public report.

- Revenue **$7.814bn, up 92%**, beating consensus near $6.72bn by about a billion. Adjusted EBITDA **$3.538bn**, nearly tripled. Net loss narrowed to **$541m** ($-0.09/share vs $-0.26 expected).
- Segments: **Connectivity (Starlink) $4.291bn, up 66%**, subscribers doubled year on year to **12 million**, up 1.7m sequentially. **AI segment $2.561bn, up 247%**. Space $962m, up 29%.
- **Capex $18.369bn for the quarter**, up from $2.825bn a year earlier. **$15.828bn of it is AI**, Connectivity $1.367bn, Space (Starship and launch infrastructure) $1.174bn.
- Cash and equivalents about **$93.5bn** post-IPO, roughly **$100bn including marketable securities**. Backlog **$47.5bn**. New Cloud Services Agreements totalling **$14.1bn contracted**.
- **Announced acquisition of Cursor for $60bn** to accelerate the AI enterprise line.
- Stock fell about 8% in extended trading on the capex number after closing 4 August around $125. It had rallied with the record-high market during the day.

## The five gates, evidence attached

**Gate 1. Disclosure clear enough to judge the business?**
Largely yes. Segmented revenue, segmented capex, EBITDA, cash, backlog. Open items to check in the filed release before verdict: free cash flow line, Starlink ARPU, AI segment margin, and debt if any. [Q2 release PDF](https://s21.q4cdn.com/184289198/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/SpaceX-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results.pdf), [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052515/earningsreleaseq22608042.htm)

**Gate 2. Emergency capital raise risk?**
Low near term. ~$100bn liquidity against ~$18.4bn quarterly capex is five to six quarters fully self-funded even with zero EBITDA, and EBITDA is $3.5bn and growing. The question is capex guidance: if AI capex accelerates further, the runway shortens and a raise (equity or debt) re-enters. Check any H2 capex guide on the call transcript.

**Gate 3. Related-party, dilution or governance surprise?**
**This is the gate that now matters most. The $60bn Cursor acquisition** was not in any pre-IPO thesis. Before Monday: find the deal terms. Cash, stock or mixed? A stock deal at this scale is meaningful dilution. Check for related-party angles (xAI, Tesla compute arrangements) and whether Musk's ~82.3% voting control affected process. This single item can fail the gate on its own if the terms are opaque.

**Gate 4. Does the long-term case still hold?**
The original thesis was Starlink recurring revenue, launch cadence, Starship. Starlink is delivering (12m subs, doubled). But the company is visibly pivoting capital toward being an AI compute provider: 86% of quarterly capex is AI, the AI segment is growing 247%, and they are buying a $60bn AI software company. The honest question is not whether the quarter was good. It is: **is this still the company the thesis described, and do you want the company it is becoming?** That deserves a written yes or no, not a shrug.

**Gate 5. Ten-year ownership, not a bounce trade?**
At roughly $115 to $125, implied equity value is near $1.5 trillion, about 47 to 50 times annualised revenue run-rate. Strong quarter, very full price. The doc's line stands: a fall from $135 is not a valuation thesis.
